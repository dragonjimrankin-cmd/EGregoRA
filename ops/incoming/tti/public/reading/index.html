Front matter · how to enter this site

The Reading Path

Most people arrive at the middle of something and judge it from there. This page is the remedy: four routes through the framework, each honest about what it costs.

The site is a chain, and a chain has a first link. Everything after it assumes it; nothing before it depends on anything. If you read one thing here and stop, read the first link — it is the whole argument in miniature, and the rest of the site is that argument carried further.

Every page carries its own ledger, and so does this one. Earned means it follows by argument or arithmetic and can be checked. Chosen means it is an interpretation laid over the mathematics — coherent, not forced. Refused means it was available, it would have been dramatic, and it was left out. The grading marks used throughout: ◆ established · ◆ scholarship · ◆ the framework's argument · ◆ vision, stated as vision.

Route one — about forty minutes

The Shortest Honest Route

  • Foundations. The two boundary conditions, π as the condition of there being a closed path at all, and the six links of the chain of necessity. Read this first and read it slowly. ◆
  • The Self. Experience as dx, the self as the integral sign. This is where the framework stakes its most contestable claim, and it says so on the page. ◆
  • The Cosmic Ledger. The measurement function, the three poles, and where the numbers 68.10 / 26.95 / 4.95 come from. This is the page a physicist should read first, and it is the page with the most to lose. ◆

Three pages, one spine. If the chain has not gripped you by the end of them, no other page on this site will do it, and you will have spent forty minutes finding that out rather than forty hours.

Route three — to break it, not to believe it

The Sceptic's Route

If you have come to falsify rather than to be persuaded, start where the framework is weakest and work inward. An argument is only as good as the best attack it has survived, and the site would rather you found the soft places with a map in your hand.

  • Stress Tests first. It is written by the same hand as everything else and it concedes real failures — the entropy objection among them. Read the concessions before the claims. ◆
  • Then the Cosmic Ledger. It is the only part of the framework that produces numbers, which makes it the only part that can be wrong in a way that counts. Check 68.10 / 26.95 / 4.95 against Planck and decide for yourself what you are looking at. ◆
  • Then Seams, where the framework goes nearest to a measurement it could fail. ◆
  • Then the ledgers at the foot of every page. They are the fastest route to what a page is actually claiming, as opposed to what its prose implies. Every page has one.
  • And ask the oracle the hardest question you have. It is built to answer from the papers rather than around them. ◆

If you find a refutation, send it. There is a page for exactly that, and it is kept in public: Corrections.

Route four — five minutes, one idea

If You Are Only Going to Read One Thing

Read the first link of the chain and look at one plate: zero and one, closed through π. Then ask ShakDrah one question, chosen to be awkward.

That is the framework in miniature: a claim, the figure that carries it, and an oracle that will not pretend the claim is a measurement. Everything else on this site is elaboration of those three things, and the first of them is the only one that has to be right.

Housekeeping

Three Things Not To Do

  • Do not start with the Megatubule. It assumes the torus, the seam and the ledger, and it is the page that looks most like a claim about the world while depending on the most. ◆
  • Do not read the Plates as decoration. Each plate note is part of the argument, and the numbers beside the diagrams are the numbers the text uses. ◆
  • Do not skip the “earned and chosen” cards. They are the site's most useful invention. A reader who cannot tell the theorem from the vision has not been given the work; they have been given a mood.

Two further tools, once you are reading: Notation & Terms lists every symbol the site uses and the grade each one carries, and The Library is where its borrowed claims can be checked.
