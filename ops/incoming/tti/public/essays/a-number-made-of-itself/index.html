A Number Made of Itself

The one place the framework stops being a vision and becomes, for a moment, plain arithmetic. Everything else on this site asks for your patience; this essay asks for your scepticism.

In this essay

The claim, stated small enough to check

Most of this site deals in readings — geometry read this way, a field read that way, a horizon read as a seam. Readings are interesting and none of them is safe. This essay is about the one thing on the site that is not a reading at all, and the way to appreciate it is to state it small.

Imagine a process that branches. Start with one thing. It divides, and each half does something — one half climbs, one half stays — and at the next level something divides again. Suppose the rule is the simplest one you could write down: at each level, every active thing that can divide does so. Count the total number of things at each level, starting from one, and write the sequence down. Here it is.

1   2   4   7   12   20   33   54   …

Now look for that sequence. It is not doubling: 1, 2, 4 is three steps of doubling and then the pattern breaks — 7 is not 8. It is not the primes, it is not the squares, and it is not any sequence you would meet in a school textbook unless someone had pointed it out to you. But add one to each term and something happens:

2   3   5   8   13   21   34   55   …

That is the Fibonacci sequence — the one where each number is the sum of the two before it, the one that turns up in the spacing of sunflower seeds and the branching of stems. The two sequences are the same sequence, shifted by two places and reduced by one. In symbols, the population after n steps is

Pn = Fn+2 − 1

and that formula is not a guess about the world. It is a piece of arithmetic that can be proved in three lines, and the proof is in this essay. ◆ Of everything the framework claims, this is the claim least able to be wrong. That is the subject, and it is worth unpacking why such a small thing matters so much.

Why a tree that forks gives you Fibonacci

The reason is not mystical, and it is not a coincidence in the sense of an accident. It is a consequence of the rule, and once you see it you cannot unsee it.

The rule, stated in plain words: at every level, a thing that is able to divide does divide; a thing that has just divided cannot divide again immediately, because it has only just become two new things. So the number of divisions at the next level is the number of things that were made at the previous level — which is the count from two levels ago, since what was made last time is what is dividing now. That sentence is the whole proof, but here it is in steps, because a proof you can follow is worth ten proofs you can admire.

Why does the shift by two appear — why Fn+2 rather than Fn? Because the starting values are 1 and 2 rather than 1 and 1. Same recursion, different seed. Nothing rides on the shift, and the shift is exactly the sort of thing that trips people up, so it is worth saying clearly: the sequence 1, 2, 4, 7, 12… is the Fibonacci sequence, with a different first term.

Now the part that makes it interesting rather than merely neat. A recursion is a rule that produces a sequence out of itself: the next term is made from the previous terms, not from anything outside. The framework’s phrase for this is a number made of itself, and it takes that phrase seriously. If the universe’s deep structure is a process that divides and recombines according to its own previous states, then the arithmetic that governs it is not imposed from outside — it grows the way the tree grows. That is a reading, and it is the framework’s own, graded accordingly. But the arithmetic underneath it is not a reading, and it does not care whether anyone agrees with the reading. ◆ The numbers are the numbers.

The splice, and what a density really is

The tree in the last section is the framework’s picture of accumulation: small things dividing, and their products adding up. The framework calls the act of division at a threshold a splice — the fork in the road at which a quantity becomes dense enough to count — and the full name for it is the Rankin Skeletal Splice. The adjective matters. The splice is not made at an arbitrary point; it is made at the skeleton: the three places where the structure cannot move, zero, one and minus one, and their recurring images, three hundred and thirty-three, six hundred and sixty-six, nine hundred and ninety-nine. The fork happens at the joints of the skeleton rather than anywhere along the bone.

Where the accumulation shows up in the arithmetic is in the deposit per density: how much new structure appears per unit of densification, measured as the tree adds levels. Take consecutive populations, look at the ratio of what is added at each step, weight it by the golden ratio the way the geometry demands, and the sequence of deposits marches towards a fixed number. The number it marches towards is not roughly anything. It is exactly

φ²/√5 = (5 + 3√5)/10 = 1.170820393…

which is a combination of φ — the golden ratio, the number that satisfies φ² = φ + 1 — and nothing else. The convergence is fast: by the fourth or fifth level, the running value is within a part in ten thousand of the limit, and the gap closes by a factor of φ each level, which is to say it becomes invisible quickly.

Why does this matter for a theory of the universe? Because of a trick that thermodynamics has already taught everyone. Temperature is not a thing any single molecule has: no molecule is warm. Warmth is what you get when you add up an enormous number of molecular motions that individually mean nothing. Pressure is the same — no single impact is pressure. The framework’s claim is that the same move applies to structure: individual splices are beneath notice, but below the threshold of one, deposits accumulate, and past it they produce a gradient — a state rather than a heap. If that is right, then the constant 1.1708… is not a curiosity of a tree diagram. It is the rate at which a universe buys structure, and it is the same rate at every scale where the skeleton recurs.

Where the constant comes from

It is worth unpacking the number itself, because a reader who has never met φ is being asked to take a lot on trust, and there is no need for that.

The golden ratio φ is the number that solves the simplest interesting equation you can write about a ratio: that the whole is to the longer part as the longer part is to the shorter. Written out, the whole divided by the part equals the part divided by the remainder, and if you set the part to one, that sentence becomes φ = 1 + 1/φ, which rearranges to φ² = φ + 1. Solving that gives φ = (1 + √5)/2, about 1.618. Every power of φ is a whole-number combination of φ and one, and the √5 keeps appearing whenever you rearrange anything, in the same way the number three keeps appearing in anything to do with triangles. So the framework’s constant —

φ²/√5 = (5 + 3√5)/10

— is not an exotic number bolted on from outside. It is φ squared, divided by the thing that φ is built from. φ² is 2.618; divide by the square root of five, 2.236, and you get 1.1708…. The rearrangement into (5 + 3√5)/10 is included because it shows you the whole number content at a glance: five, three, five, ten. Every one of those is a small whole number, and the entire constant is a ratio of small whole numbers of the √5 kind — which is what mathematicians call an algebraic number of degree two, and what the framework reads as a sign that the constant lives in the geometry rather than in any particular physical choice.

There is a further reason the magnitude is what it is. A ratio that tends to a limit greater than one describes something that grows: each level of the tree is bigger than the last, and the growth rate settles. But the rate is not φ itself (1.618), nor is it the plain ratio of consecutive terms in the tree, which is closer to 1.618 at large n. The extra factor is the square root of five, and the square root of five is exactly what enters when you go from counting terms to measuring density: the cumulative population, divided by the number of levels, picks up the normalisation, and the normalisation is the same √5 that appears in Binet’s formula for the Fibonacci numbers themselves. So the constant is not a free parameter of the framework — it is not tuned to anything — which is the reason it can be quoted to ten digits in a table on the summary page with a straight face. Nothing was fitted.

What has not been done is the step from the constant to a measurement. The framework would like 1.1708 to appear somewhere in a laboratory — a ratio of accumulated intensities, a threshold at which a system changes state, a quantity that steps up rather than sliding. So far it does not, and the plate called The Constant Quantum is a drawing of a convergence, not a plot of data. That is the honest status: an exact number with no measurement attached to it yet.

What is exact here, and what is not

Now the honest sorting, which is the reason this essay is short and cold rather than long and thrilled.

Exact: the recursion. The population sequence. The convergence to φ²/√5. The appearance of 666 as the thirty-sixth triangular number, and thirty-six as six flavours of quark times three colours times two. Each of those is arithmetic, each can be checked by hand, and none of them depends on anything else on the site being true. If every other page were withdrawn tomorrow, the formula Pn = Fn+2 − 1 would still be correct.

Not exact, and not arithmetic: that the universe is such a tree. That the accumulation described is the accumulation that actually happened. That the density thresholds of the physical world are the joints of the skeleton. That the deposit rate shows up in anything measurable. Those are the framework’s moves, and they are made in the open, with the grade marks on them. The arithmetic is the hook; the physics is what has been hung on the hook. Hooks do not hold things up by themselves.

Why this is the place to start attacking

If you have come to this site wanting to break it, this is where to begin, and the framework invites you.

Here is the reason. Attacking a vision is easy and proves nothing: a dream about a wheel cannot be refuted, so refuting it is no achievement. Attacking a mathematical claim is hard and proves something, because arithmetic either holds or it does not. So the useful question about Pn = Fn+2 − 1 is not “is this suggestive?” — everything is suggestive if you have looked at it for long enough — but “is the rule stated precisely enough to have a counterexample?” If the rule is “every active thing that can divide does divide, immediately on becoming able”, then the recursion is forced and there is no counterexample to find. If instead the rule is vague — “things divide when they are ready” — then the sequence can be anything, and the arithmetic evaporates into decoration. The whole value of the claim sits in the precision of the rule.

This is the test the framework passes and most of its competitors do not, and it is the reason this essay is worth an afternoon even for a reader who is unmoved by cosmology. There is a general lesson buried in the tree: the parts of a speculative system that can be made exact should be made exact, and then separated ruthlessly from the parts that cannot. Most speculative systems do the opposite — the loose parts get dressed in mathematical clothing, and the exact parts get buried under rhetoric. The Two Infinities has this one small, sharp, provable corner, and it puts that corner first rather than last.

What the corner does not do is deliver a universe. A recursion is a rule for producing the next term; it does not tell you what a term is made of, whether anything actually runs the rule, or why the rule should apply to anything physical. The framework’s account of all that lies elsewhere: in the half-twist, in the foundations, and in the dark-sector scorecard, where the framework takes marks off itself. The tree does one job and does it completely: it shows that somewhere underneath all the reading and the geometry, there is a piece of the picture that does not need you to believe anything. Read on to The One Edge, where the tone changes and the certainty drops.
