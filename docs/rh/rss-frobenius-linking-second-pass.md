## Executive result

A closer source search found a stronger exact bridge than the finite-cover observation alone:

> For every prime \(p\ne11\), the arithmetic linking homomorphism \(\operatorname{lk}_p\) has a 11-adic unit coordinate equal to \(p\in\mathbb Z_{11}^{\times}\). Reduction modulo 11 sends that coordinate to \(p\bmod11\), which is precisely the Frobenius \(\sigma_p\) in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{11})/\mathbb Q\).

Thus the \(C_{10}\)-monodromy in the Connes–Consani finite cover is a concrete finite quotient of Morishita's arithmetic linking data. This ties the literal factors 2 and 5 in 10, 8, and 10/8 to a genuine arithmetic-topology linking cocycle, not merely to an abstract multiplication table.

Finite-character Fourier projectors can therefore isolate the \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{11})\) sectors. This is a known spectral framework, not a new TTI operator or cancellation result.

**A newly identified scaling-site/cover bridge, now tested spectrally:** TTI’s logarithmic half-turn per decade is anti-periodic under scale 10 and periodic under the double cover 100, but the fundamental frequency \(t_{10}=\pi/\log10\) is not a zeta-zero ordinate. The cover match is exact as character arithmetic but does not put that TTI mode in the zeta spectrum. Higher harmonics remain unclassified globally. The cycle theorem realizes critical-line zeros only; it does not establish RH or identify TTI’s affine phase with a zeta eigenmode.

## 1. The new exact arithmetic-topology link

Morishita's 2025 preprint, revised in January 2026 and listed as appearing in *Münster Journal of Mathematics* in 2026, recalls the arithmetic linking homomorphism
\[
\operatorname{lk}_p:p^{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\longrightarrow
\widehat{\mathbb Z}_{(p)}^{\times}
=\prod_{q\ne p}\mathbb Z_q^{\times}.
\]
The element \(p\) maps diagonally to the tuple whose \(q\)-component is the \(q\)-adic unit \(p\). The same map is identified with the map on étale fundamental groups induced by reduction \(\mathbb Z_{(p)}\to\mathbb F_p\). Its value at the Frobenius generator is the linking monodromy. For \(q\ne p\), its \(q\)-component is the familiar residue/Frobenius data; for example, its mod-2 reductions recover the Legendre-symbol linking numbers.

Take \(q=11\). For every \(p\ne11\), reduction of the 11-coordinate gives
\[
\operatorname{red}_{11}\bigl((\operatorname{lk}_p(p))_{11}\bigr)
=p\bmod11\in(\mathbb Z/11\mathbb Z)^\times
\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{11})/\mathbb Q),
\]
where \(a\) acts by \(\zeta_{11}\mapsto\zeta_{11}^{a}\). This is exactly \(\sigma_p=\operatorname{Frob}_p\) for this abelian extension. In other words, the finite cover monodromy is the residue-mod-11 shadow of the arithmetic linking map.

The qualifications matter: this uses the 11-coordinate only when \(p\ne11\); the cover is ramified at 11 and has no unramified \(\sigma_{11}\). The source's full linking tuple has coordinates at every \(q\ne p\); the mod-11 projection is just one finite quotient, not a new global reciprocity law.

## 2. Decimal TTI calculations in the linking quotient

The exact residues are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
p & p\bmod11 & \operatorname{ord}_{11}(p)\\\hline
2&2&10\\
5&5&5\\
7&7&10\\
13&2&10
\end{array}
\]
so the cover has the familiar orbit splitting: \(p=2,7,13\) each give one lifted circle of length \(10\log p\); \(p=5\) gives two circles of length \(5\log5\). These are the geometric local Euler factors.

TTI's literal decimal comparison is multiplicative in the linked residue:
\[
10=2\cdot5\quad\mapsto\quad2\cdot5=10\equiv-1\pmod{11},
\]
\[
8=2^3\quad\mapsto\quad2^3=8\pmod{11},\qquad
10\,8^{-1}\equiv10\cdot8^{-1}\equiv4\pmod{11}.
\]
The associated additive scale labels are \(\log10=\log2+\log5\), \(\log8=3\log2\), and \(\log(10/8)=\log(5/4)\). Hence the joint label is exactly
\[
(\log(10/8),\sigma_4)
=(\log(5/4),\sigma_4).
\]
Under a character \(\chi\) of \(C_{10}\), this acts by
\[
e^{it\log(5/4)}\chi(4).
\]
This is an actual character of the product of the real scaling parameter and finite linking quotient. It does **not** give a relation between \(t\) and \(\chi\), nor force this character to equal 1 at any zero ordinate.

The mod-11 shadow of decimal powers is small and periodic:
\[
10^n\equiv(-1)^n\pmod{11}.
\]
Thus the successive TTI thresholds \(10,100,1000,\ldots\) carry only the alternating order-two monodromy \(\sigma_{-1},1,\sigma_{-1},\ldots\). There is one exact partial match with TTI's stated order-two splice: under the convention \(\chi_j(\sigma_2)=e^{2\pi i j/10}\), the quadratic character is \(\chi_5\), and it has
\[
\chi_5(\sigma_2)=-1,\qquad \chi_5(\sigma_5)=1,
\qquad \chi_5(\sigma_{10})=-1,
\]
so TTI's sign at the endpoint 10 agrees numerically with \(\chi_5(\sigma_{10})\), where \(\sigma_{10}:=\sigma_2\sigma_5\) is a formal abelianized product of the 2- and 5-return labels, not the Frobenius of a prime orbit \(C_{10}\). This does not match the complete TTI clock. Its linear phase is \(e^{i\pi x/10}\), giving \(i\) at the hinge \(x=5\), whereas \(\sigma_5\) has order 5 and every one-dimensional character takes a fifth root of unity there; no character of \(C_{10}\) can take the value \(i\) on \(\sigma_5\). Thus the endpoint sign has an exact quadratic-Artin realization, while the five-hinge and full \(\pi/10\) phase clock do not arise from one finite Galois character under the natural prime-label assignment. This is a precise partial resonance and a precise obstruction to over-identifying the two phase systems; it cannot by itself explain why the TTI hierarchy is base ten. **Notation correction:** an earlier pass called this character \(\chi_2\); with the explicit indexing \(\chi_j(\sigma_2)=e^{2\pi i j/10}\), that was incorrect—the quadratic character is \(\chi_5\). The displayed character values and the parity conclusion are unchanged; all subsequent notation is corrected.

The finite group gives a further exact obstruction to making the order-two seam into a zeta-only projector. Choose \(\sigma_2\) as a generator of \(G=C_{10}\), and write \(\chi_j(\sigma_2)=e^{2\pi i j/10}\). Since \(\sigma_{10}=\sigma_2^5\),
\[
\chi_j(\sigma_{10})=(-1)^j.
\]
The involution \(\sigma_{10}\) therefore separates the ten character sectors into five even and five odd sectors. The even sector contains the trivial character **and four nontrivial even Dirichlet characters**; the odd sector contains five odd characters. Thus, **if** TTI's sign is realized by this deck involution, it can at most distinguish parity. It cannot isolate \(\zeta(s)\) from the other even-character L-functions. A full \(C_{10}\) Fourier idempotent can isolate the trivial character, but that is precisely the tautological trivial-isotypic projection, not cancellation derived from TTI.

There is, however, a lawful multiplicative/logarithmic phase candidate in the TTI material. If the phase coordinate is the log-decade \(u=\log_{10}x\), one half-turn per decade is the character \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{it_{10}}\) of the positive real scaling group (equivalently, the archimedean-component restriction of the idelic norm character \(|\cdot|_{\mathbb A}^{it_{10}}\)), with
\[
t_{10}=\frac{\pi}{\log10},\qquad e^{it_{10}\log p}=e^{i\pi\log_{10}p}.
\]
On the real scaling subgroup it is multiplicative and satisfies \(e^{it_{10}\log10}=-1\), exactly as the quadratic Artin character \(\chi_5\) satisfies \(\chi_5(\sigma_2\sigma_5)=-1\). Thus the TTI logarithmic alternative and the finite Frobenius sign agree at the single decade return. The archimedean scale evaluation and the finite Frobenius character are distinct local components; with the matching inverse/sign convention they define a Hecke character \(|\cdot|^{it_{10}}\chi_5\), not the trivial character. Its \(L\)-function is a twisted/vertically shifted character \(L\)-function, not \(\zeta\), and no equality with a zeta-zero ordinate or zero set follows. At the hinge \(x=5\), the affine TTI phase is \(i\), while the logarithmic phase is \(e^{i\pi\log_{10}5}\); neither yields a character of \(C_{10}\) matching the affine three-point clock. The TTI page itself treats affine and logarithmic phase laws as distinct alternatives, so the bridge may use the logarithmic version only if TTI independently selects it.

This distinction has an exact algebraic test. An Euler/idele character on positive rational scales must be multiplicative. If one naively feeds the integer scale into the affine TTI phase \(a(x)=e^{i\pi x/10}\), then
\[
a(2)a(5)=e^{i7\pi/10}\ne -1=a(10),
\]
so this phase cannot be the multiplicative Euler character of the prime-scale monoid under the natural assignment \(x=p\). By contrast,
\[
a_{\log}(x)=e^{i\pi\log_{10}x}
\]
is multiplicative and gives \(a_{\log}(10)=-1\). Thus choosing the logarithmic coordinate is not cosmetic: it is necessary for a character-valued Euler product. It supplies the shifted functions \(\zeta(s-it_{10})\) or \(L(s-it_{10},\chi_5)\) (depending on the finite character), with correspondingly shifted zeros and gamma factors; it does not by itself produce an identity for the original completed \(\xi\) zeros.

## 3. A second geometric bridge: Deninger–Morishita to Connes–Consani

Masanori Morishita's paper proves a continuous map from Deninger's foliated dynamical systems for abelian number fields to the Connes–Consani adelic spaces. Theorem 3.6 states that the map is Galois-equivariant and flow-anti-equivariant; prime closed orbits in the Deninger system map to the corresponding circles \(C_p\) in the adelic space. The proof identifies the relevant arithmetic linking homomorphism as the monodromy in the coverings of the phase spaces.

This gives a useful two-step geometric diagram:
\[
\text{Deninger foliated prime orbit}
\longrightarrow
\text{Connes--Consani prime orbit }C_p
\xrightarrow{\text{finite cover}}
\text{Frobenius/linking monodromy}.
\]
It is potentially relevant to TTI's two-inversion/return language because the map reverses the direction of the positive scaling flow while preserving Galois data. But the theorem is about spaces, flows, and prime orbits. It does not construct the arithmetic cohomology conjectured by Deninger, a zero-spectrum on that cohomology, or a trace pairing proving RH. The anti-equivariance alone cannot be interpreted as an operator contracting horizontal zero coordinates.

There is a useful nearby existence theorem, but its scope must also be kept exact. Álvarez López, Kim, and Morishita prove regularized determinant formulas for certain **already-constructed** three-dimensional Riemannian foliated dynamical systems, using reduced leafwise cohomology and the infinitesimal flow generator. Their dynamical spectral \(\xi\)-functions belong to those systems; they are not the Riemann \(\xi\)-function. This provides a technical template for what a genuine flow/cohomology/trace package looks like, not a construction of the arithmetic foliation for \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\). For TTI to use this route it would need to specify a foliated space, the transverse flow, the closed-orbit length measure, and its leafwise cohomology, then independently prove that its prime-orbit trace is the completed Riemann explicit formula. No current TTI page supplies those data.

## 4. What spectral theory is already known (correction to the prior research gate)

The known adelic theory already contains most of the abstract spectral decomposition:

1. **Connes–Consani–Marcolli:** the cohomological Lefschetz formula realizes L-functions with Grössencharakter on the \(H^1\)-type space associated with the adele class space; their Proposition 4.13 identifies the induced idele-class-group representation with the spectral realization for a chosen character. The trace formula is the explicit formula. The positivity needed for RH is separate and RH-strength.
2. **Ralf Meyer:** for a global field, a single virtual representation of the idele class group has spectral multiplicities equal to the zero/pole orders of the universal completed L-function over all quasi-characters. Its character equals the Weil distribution, so the equality of the spectral and geometric characters is the explicit formula. The paper explicitly says the missing Connes-style geometric computation would be equivalent to GRH.
3. **Connes–Consani–Moscovici:** the semilocal prolate/Sonin operator is a developed operator theory for the zeta-zero side and is stable as finite sets of places are enlarged. The cited result is for the zeta construction; it does not furnish a \(C_{10}\)-equivariant operator on the cyclotomic cover or a decimal-rescaling projector.

4. **A potentially relevant annihilator already exists in the cohomological trace setup.** In Connes–Consani–Marcolli, Proposition 6.4 identifies the range \(\mathcal V\) of the adelic restriction/reduction map as a radical: for \(f\in\mathcal V\), the induced operator on the primitive cohomology \(H^1(\mathbb A_K/K^*,C_K)\) vanishes, and its trace pairing with every test function is zero. The quotient by this radical is the cohomological space on which the explicit-formula trace acts. Their Lemma 7.3 also shows that adding such a trace-invisible \(f\in\mathcal V\) can alter the correspondence degree \(\widehat f(1)\) arbitrarily, through a failure of Fubini.

This is a genuine, non-tautological annihilation mechanism in the **test-function/correspondence direction**. It is not a theorem that nontrivial Dirichlet zero sums cancel, nor does it isolate the trivial character. If TTI's second inversion or history residue is to use this route, it must define a map into the adelic test space and prove that its residue lies in \(\mathcal V\), or define a nonzero class in the quotient and calculate its trace. Membership in \(\mathcal V\) would mean the contribution is invisible to the primitive cohomological trace—not that it leaves a nonzero \(\xi\)-only identity. This is a sharper candidate for the word “residue,” but the required TTI-to-\(\mathcal V\) map is not present in the current framework.

Thus it is not accurate to say that no equivariant spectral decomposition of character L-functions exists. The current obstruction is narrower and stronger: the literature provides the character sectors and the explicit formula, but the TTI framework has not supplied an independently defined operator whose action on those sectors proves a new identity or non-tautological cancellation. Applying the trivial-character idempotent simply selects \(\zeta(s)\); it does not make the other eight nontrivial characters' zero ledgers cancel.

For \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{11})\), be precise about the principal character. The trivial Artin character has L-function \(\zeta(s)\), including the Euler factor at 11. The principal Dirichlet character modulo 11 is imprimitive and has \(L(s,\chi_0)=(1-11^{-s})\zeta(s)\). Confusing these two changes the zero divisor by the zeros of \(1-11^{-s}\).

## 5. Exact scale correspondences on the arithmetic site: a further bridge, with a boundary

Connes–Consani, *Geometry of the Arithmetic Site* (arXiv:1502.05580), supplies the most literal established geometric counterpart to positive-scale rescaling. The arithmetic site has Frobenius correspondences \(\Psi(\lambda)\) for positive real \(\lambda\), constructed as subvarieties of the square of the site. Its geometric points over \(\mathbb R_{+}^{\max}\) identify with the adele-class quotient \(X_{\mathbb Q}\), and the Frobenius action \(\mathrm{Fr}_{\lambda}\) corresponds to multiplication by \(\lambda\) on those points. Theorem 1.2 gives the composition law: \(\Psi(\lambda)\circ\Psi(\lambda')=\Psi(\lambda\lambda')\) when \(\lambda\lambda'\notin\mathbb Q\), and also when both parameters are rational; if both are irrational but their product is rational, the result is \(\operatorname{Id}_\epsilon\circ\Psi(\lambda\lambda')\), with \(\operatorname{Id}_\epsilon\) the tangential deformation of identity. Thus all TTI scale labels 10, 8, and 5/4 are rational parameters and their rational compositions are exact—for example \(\Psi(8)\circ\Psi(5/4)=\Psi(10)\), matching the literal arithmetic \(8\cdot(10/8)=10\), as well as \(\Psi(2)\circ\Psi(5)=\Psi(10)\)—without needing an inverse-correspondence assumption. The TTI clock also has \(\omega_4/\omega_5=(\pi/2)/(2\pi/5)=5/4\), so the same rational appears as the four-to-five angular-rate ratio and as \(10/8\). This is an exact numerical alignment plus an exact site-correspondence identity. TTI's five-to-four clock, however, defines phase-state rates and does not itself declare \(\Psi(5/4)\) or a cohomological action.

The same paper's Theorem 4.2 identifies the complete Riemann zeta function
\[
\zeta_{\mathbb Q}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)
\]
as the Hasse–Weil zeta function of the arithmetic site, via its counting distribution. This is an established geometric/counting construction of the untwisted completed zeta—not an RH proof, not a new TTI identity, and not a cancellation between twisted character zero sets. A necessary refinement: the positive-real scaling action on \(X_{\mathbb Q}\) is already a genuine group action, so pullback gives operators \(T_\lambda\) on appropriate function spaces; Connes–Consani–Marcolli also use the induced scaling representation in their cohomological trace theory. Thus a scale parameter 10 does have a natural operator representative in the established representation, once its embedding and domain are fixed. What is not automatic is that the site correspondence \(\Psi(10)\) acts on that cohomology as a correspondence operator, that \(T_{10}\) has a pointwise trace, or that its action is TTI's affine phase. The trace formula is distributional and is paired with admissible test functions through an integrated operator \(T(h)=\int h(\lambda)T_\lambda\,d^\times\lambda\); a Dirac evaluation at one scale needs its own regularization/domain proof. On the standard Mellin normalization, a zero parameter \(s=1/2+\eta+i\gamma\) corresponds formally to multiplier \(10^{\eta+i\gamma}\); unit modulus of every such multiplier is simply RH, not an independent result. The log-decade character \(10^{it_{10}}=-1\) matches only the chosen frequency, not a theorem that this frequency is a zeta zero. Keep the affine phase \(e^{i\pi x/10}\) separate from multiplicative scaling.

This does sharpen the research route. TTI's *scale labels* 10, 8, and 10/8 may be sent to the established correspondences \(\Psi(10)\), \(\Psi(8)\), and \(\Psi(5/4)\); on the point-space side, \(\Psi(10)\) factors according to multiplicative scale composition (e.g. 10=2·5). But to obtain a TTI theorem, one still needs a canonical map from TTI states/operations to these correspondences and a functorial action on a stated trace/cohomology domain. In particular, establish whether composition is exact on the relevant correspondence class rather than assume an inverse or an operator identity from notation alone; then compute its action on the untwisted spectral object and all local/archimedean terms. If this yields only the known Hasse–Weil counting formula or the trivial-isotypic projection, it is not new zero-ledger cancellation.

## 6. Scaling-site zeta cycles and a possible two-pass cover

A further close match appears in Connes–Consani, *Hochschild homology, trace map and ζ-cycles* (arXiv:2207.10419, revised 24 August 2026). For each scale \(\mu>1\), they use the multiplicative circle \(C_\mu=\mathbb R_{>0}^*/\mu^{\mathbb Z}\), length \(L=\log\mu\), and a scaling action. The family of Hilbert spaces over these circles is organized over the scaling site; the integer map \(n\) is represented by the covering \(C_{\mu^n}\to C_\mu\). Their Theorem 1.2 identifies a sheaf-cohomology representation with the spectral realization of the **critical zeros** through the action \(\lambda^{is}\), on a quotient by the closed ideal generated by \(\zeta(1/2+is)\). This is a genuine scale-and-cover spectral construction, not merely analogy.

For TTI's logarithmic alternative, put \(t_{10}=\pi/\log10\). Then
\[
10^{it_{10}}=-1,\qquad 100^{it_{10}}=1.
\]
Thus \(x\mapsto x^{it_{10}}\) is anti-periodic under the base decade and periodic on its two-fold cover:
\[
\mathbb R_{>0}^*/10^{\mathbb Z}\ \longleftarrow\ \mathbb R_{>0}^*/100^{\mathbb Z}.
\]
On \(C_{100}\), this is a genuine unitary Fourier character in the fiber \(L^2(C_{100})\). For the standard pullback \(T_\lambda f(x)=f(\lambda^{-1}x)\), it satisfies \(T_{10}f=-f\) and \(T_{100}f=f\). This is an exact representation of the abstract positive-scale group matching TTI's logarithmic sign/return rule; it is not the affine phase and, by itself, is not a global section of the scaling-site sheaf.

Equivalently, \(t_{10}\) is not in the ordinary character lattice \((2\pi/\log10)\mathbb Z\) of the base circle, but it is the first positive frequency \(2\pi/\log100\) on the double cover. This is an exact mathematical model of the logarithmic phase’s sign flip and double return. It becomes a realization of TTI only if TTI independently identifies one pass with multiplication by 10 and its two-pass extension with the cover \(C_{100}\to C_{10}\); the current TTI pages do not define that identification. It does not realize the affine phase \(e^{i\pi x/10}\), which is not multiplicative.

The paper gives an exact test in Eq. (5.11): on a circle of length \(L\), the \(n\)-th Fourier component transforms by \(\lambda^{-2\pi i n/L}\). At \(L_0=2\log10\), the first mode is \(\lambda^{-it_{10}}\) (inverse convention to TTI, with the same order-two monodromy). Lemma 5.3 and Theorem 5.4 identify the quotient by the closed ideal generated by \(\zeta(1/2\mp2\pi i/L)\). Since \(\zeta(1/2\pm it_{10})\ne0\), those multipliers are smooth units near \(L_0\) (their reciprocals are smooth there); the corresponding quotient stalk is the zero module. Thus the fundamental character is excluded from the zeta-cycle spectral quotient at this scale—not merely an unverified near-match. This says nothing about higher modes \(n\ge2\), whose test is \(\zeta(1/2\pm n t_{10})\).

The spectral boundary is exact: Theorem 1.2 realizes critical-line zeros (and multiplicities) in the quotient; it does not show that every nontrivial zero is critical. The scale-10 cover cannot identify its fundamental mode with a zeta zero, as the previous calculation shows. The unresolved all-height question is whether any higher harmonic \(k\pi/\log10\), \(k\ge2\), is an actual zero ordinate. The first zero lies between harmonics 10 and 11 (\(10t_{10}=13.64376\ldots<14.13473\ldots<15.00814\ldots=11t_{10}\)), so even the first visible candidate near-match is not equality. LMFDB data can test finitely many harmonics but cannot establish noncoincidence for all \(k\); known linear-independence conjectures for zero ordinates concern rational relations among ordinates and do not directly settle this comparison with \(\pi/\log10\). The quotient by the zeta-generated ideal is a spectral construction, not an independent proof that all zeros lie on the line; it also gives no cancellation among cyclotomic character sectors.

There is also an embedding distinction to preserve. The positive real scaling group acts on the adelic quotient through the idele class represented by an archimedean scale. A positive rational \(q\) embedded diagonally in the ideles is trivial in the idele-class quotient by \(\mathbb Q^\times\). Therefore \(q^{it}\) is a valid character of the abstract multiplicative group \(\mathbb Q_{>0}^\times\), and the scale character \(\lambda^{it}\) is valid on \(\mathbb R_{>0}^\times\), but neither should be called the value of the global idèle-class character on the diagonal rational idele. The global norm character is trivial there by the product formula; its local archimedean and finite components must be kept distinct. This repairs an easy source of overstatement in “prime phase = idele character” language.

**Research value:** the zeta-cycle framework gives a candidate scaling-site counterpart for TTI's *two-pass logarithmic rule*: define a morphism of TTI's phase/return data into the scaling-site sheaf, then test whether the induced section survives the zeta-cycle quotient and how its scaling spectrum acts. The match currently proves only an anti-periodic character/cover identity. It neither supplies that morphism from the TTI axioms nor strengthens the known critical-zero realization to a statement about all zeros.

### 6.1 The stronger untwisted target in the same paper

A useful correction to an overly narrow focus on Theorem 1.2: Sections 3–4 of the same Connes–Consani paper give an untwisted spectral model for **all non-real zeros**, not only the critical-line zeros. Let \(H=x\partial_x\) generate positive-real scaling on the specified adelic Schwartz space, and put \(\Delta=H(1+H)\). Proposition 4.2 identifies the spectrum of \(\Delta\) on the strong Schwartz quotient
\[
\mathbf S(\mathbb R_{>0}^*)/\overline{\operatorname{range}(\operatorname{Tr}\circ\Delta)}
\]
with \(\{(\rho-\tfrac12)^2-\tfrac14:\zeta(\rho)=0,\ \rho\notin\mathbb R\}\), with possible multiplicities. This is the image of the full non-real zero divisor under \(\rho\mapsto\rho(\rho-1)\), not an injective labeling: the functional-equation pair \(\rho,1-\rho\) has the same spectral value. Corollary 4.3 states that this spectrum is negative exactly when RH holds. Thus it is an established untwisted all-zero spectral model, but its negative-spectrum condition is RH-equivalent—not an independent proof or a positive self-adjoint RH operator. It is a better target for a TTI map than the critical-only zeta-cycle sheaf if the aim is the full untwisted \(\xi\)-divisor.

There is also a concrete admissibility obstruction to a naive deposit map. TTI defines \(\delta\Pi_n=(F_{n+2}-1)\varphi^{-n}\to\varphi^2/\sqrt5\ne0\), whereas the trace construction starts from specified Schwartz/strong-Schwartz domains and imposes vanishing conditions (including \(f(0)=0\), zero integral in the zeta-cycle construction). Directly treating the raw deposits as Schwartz samples or coefficients fails rapid decay. A smoothing, renormalization, and intertwining map could potentially be proposed, but it is not supplied by TTI; its exact definition and proof of compatibility with \(\operatorname{Tr}\circ\Delta\) are the first missing axioms. The TTI two-Möbius block \(\begin{psmallmatrix}0&Z\\M&0\end{psmallmatrix}^2=I\) is an involution on its ledger, not the scaling generator \(H\) or this Laplacian.

**Updated research value:** pursue both targets separately: the all-zero quotient of \(\Delta\) for a full \(\xi\)-ledger identity, and the scale-parameterized critical-zero sheaf for the exact decade-cover question. Any TTI bridge must define the map from its state/ledger to admissible test data before claiming a trace or spectrum.

### 6.2 An exact Mellin generating distribution from TTI's deposits

The failed direct map into Schwartz functions does not preclude a distributional encoding. The actual post-seed deposit is
\[
d_n:=\delta\Pi_n=(F_{n+2}-1)\varphi^{-n},\qquad n\ge1.
\]
Using Binet's formula with \(\psi=-\varphi^{-1}\), this is exactly
\[
d_n=\frac{\varphi^2}{\sqrt5}-\frac{\varphi^{-2}}{\sqrt5}(-\varphi^{-2})^n-\varphi^{-n},
\qquad d_n\longrightarrow\frac{\varphi^2}{\sqrt5}.
\]
Thus the scale-lattice measure \(\mu_{\rm TTI}=\sum_{n\ge1}d_n\,\delta_{n\log10}\) is a positive tempered measure: its bounded weights and linearly growing mass allow pairing with Schwartz tests, even though the sequence itself is not a Schwartz vector. Its Laplace/Dirichlet generating function, initially for \(\Re s>0\), is the exact rational function
\[
D_{\rm TTI}(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}d_n10^{-ns}
=\frac{\varphi^2}{\sqrt5}\frac q{1-q}
+\frac{\varphi^{-4}}{\sqrt5}\frac q{1+\varphi^{-2}q}
-\varphi^{-1}\frac q{1-\varphi^{-1}q},\qquad q=10^{-s}.
\]
Its meromorphic poles occur on three explicitly different vertical lattices (write \(L=\log10\)):
\[
s=\frac{2\pi i k}{L},\qquad
s=-\frac{\log\varphi}{L}+\frac{2\pi i k}{L},\qquad
s=-\frac{2\log\varphi}{L}+\frac{(2k+1)\pi i}{L},\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]
The last, alternating-Fibonacci family contains \(\operatorname{Im}s=t_{10}\) and all odd multiples; it gives an exact analytic explanation for the half-turn/double-return frequency inside the deposit data. These are poles of the TTI generating function, **not** zeta zeros. In the natural but not TTI-axiomatized centering \(\rho=\tfrac12+s\), the first such pole maps to \(\rho\approx0.0820247+i1.3643764\), below the first nontrivial zero height, so it is not a zeta zero either.

There is an important spectral distinction. On the unitary boundary \(s=i\tau\), only the constant tail of \(d_n\) creates a real-frequency Dirac comb, at \(\tau=2\pi k/L\); the two golden corrections have absolutely summable coefficients and give a smooth periodic function there. Explicitly, with Fourier convention \(\widehat\mu(\tau)=\int e^{-i\tau x}d\mu(x)\) and the lattice sum interpreted in the tempered-distribution (equivalently Abel-limit) sense,
\[
\widehat\mu_{\rm TTI}(\tau)=\frac{\varphi^2}{\sqrt5}\left(-\frac12+\frac{\pi}{L}\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\delta\!\left(\tau-\frac{2\pi k}{L}\right)-\frac{i}{2}\operatorname{PV}\cot\frac{L\tau}{2}\right)+\frac{\varphi^{-4}}{\sqrt5}\frac{e^{-iL\tau}}{1+\varphi^{-2}e^{-iL\tau}}-\varphi^{-1}\frac{e^{-iL\tau}}{1-\varphi^{-1}e^{-iL\tau}}.
\]
So \(t_{10}=\pi/L\) appears as the imaginary part of a pole off the unitary axis, **not** as a real-axis delta eigenfrequency of the deposit measure. This differs from the separate log-phase character \(x^{it_{10}}\) on \(C_{100}\), which is a genuine circle eigencharacter before quotienting.

There is a separate support obstruction to identifying \(\mu_{\rm TTI}\) with the prime side of the Weil explicit formula: its atoms lie at \(n\log10=\log(10^n)\), and \(10^n\) is not a prime power. The explicit-formula prime distribution is supported at \(\log(p^m)\) (and reflected points), so the atomic measures are not equal. Convolution of the \(2\)- and \(5\)-prime channels could create \(n\log10\), but neither that convolution nor the required weights are specified by TTI. This yields a useful exact distributional candidate and a precise first obstruction—not a trace identity. A related Euler-factor check is equally decisive: the leading TTI tail contributes \(q/(1-q)=\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-ns}5^{-ns}\), the diagonal \(a=b\) only, whereas the two Euler factors multiply to \(1/((1-2^{-s})(1-5^{-s}))=\sum_{a,b\ge0}2^{-as}5^{-bs}\), with every pair of exponents. The arithmetic-site composition \(\Psi(2)\circ\Psi(5)=\Psi(10)\) does not supply this missing diagonal projector or convert the TTI weights into von Mangoldt weights.

### 6.3 Two useful exact refinements from the arithmetic-site/Jacobian literature

**Primary-source check of composition.** The full Connes–Consani paper *Geometry of the Arithmetic Site*, Theorem 1.2 (and the detailed result in §7.4), explicitly says that \(\Psi(\lambda)\circ\Psi(\lambda')=\Psi(\lambda\lambda')\) when the product is irrational, and that the same equality holds when both parameters are rational. Therefore \(\Psi(8)\circ\Psi(5/4)=\Psi(10)\) is source-verified. Its proof is composition of reduced correspondences built from the square/diagonal construction; it is a site-level result. The paper's introduction also states that at that stage a suitable Weil cohomology to interpret its zeta counting equality as a Lefschetz formula remains an open question. This is a direct warning against reading Theorem 1.2 as a pointwise trace or a cohomological action theorem.

There is a closer exact match to TTI's *cyclic* 5-versus-4 clock than the bare product identity. In §6.3, Proposition 6.12, the rational correspondence for \(r=n/m\) is described by a semiring generated by \(X,Y\) with relation \(X^m=Y^n\) (for coprime \(n,m\)); thus \(r=5/4\) gives \(X^4=Y^5\). TTI's five-state and four-state clocks likewise close after 20 ticks: four complete 5-state turns and five complete 4-state turns. This is an exact shared commensuration relation, not evidence that the clock phases are the correspondence, and not an intertwiner to zeta cohomology. The source's correspondence parameter is a positive multiplicative scale ratio; TTI explicitly frames 5 and 4 as rival cyclic phase-state counts. Any identification must specify a map between those distinct structures.

**New 2026 arithmetic-Jacobian test.** Connes–Consani, *On the Jacobian of* \(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}\) (arXiv:2602.15941, 2026), identify the Riemann sector \(X_{\mathbb Q}\) with a monoid of rank-one groups; its product is tensor product, and the Abel–Jacobi map sends a prime \(p\) to \(H_p=\mathbb Z[1/p]\). They state \(H_p\otimes H_p\cong H_p\), so these prime points are idempotent in the Picard monoid. Consequently the multiplicative label \(10^n=2^n5^n\), for every \(n\ge1\), maps under this prime-generated Abel–Jacobi/product construction to
\[
H_2^{\otimes n}\otimes H_5^{\otimes n}\cong H_2\otimes H_5\cong\mathbb Z[1/10]=H_{10},
\]
independent of \(n\). The framework therefore supplies a canonical geometric image for the *support label* 10, but this idempotent stratum collapses the entire decimal tower and cannot retain the distinct deposit weights \(\delta\Pi_n\) without extra grading, multiplicity, metric, or other structure. The paper does describe richer archimedean rigidifying data and the full adelic space; whether that data can retain TTI's \(n\)-dependence is a concrete next test, not a conclusion. The paper's trace/Lefschetz interpretation still uses the established adelic action/trace framework; it does not make TTI's deposits a trace identity.

**A canonical candidate for the missing diagonal operation—and its limit.** Standard diagonal extraction is explicit. For \(F(x,y)=((1-x)(1-y))^{-1}=\sum_{a,b\ge0}x^ay^b\), define \(\mathcal D F(z)=\operatorname{CT}_x F(x,z/x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint F(w,z/w)\,\frac{dw}{w}\), on any annulus contour \(|z|<|w|<1\). Then \(\mathcal D F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}z^n=(1-z)^{-1}\); deleting the \(n=0\) term yields \(z/(1-z)\), the leading TTI support series after setting \(z=10^{-s}\). This is an explicit torus/Cauchy coefficient projection. This makes the *additional operation* mathematically precise rather than mysterious. But it is an imposed diagonal extraction: neither TTI's splice rules nor the site-level composition theorem defines it as an action on the adele-class test domain. Moreover it yields diagonal support \(10^n\), not the prime-power distribution with \(\Lambda(p^m)\) weights. Thus it repairs only the combinatorial support comparison at the cost of an extra operator; it does not furnish the sought explicit-formula trace.



### 6.4 A TTI-generated Weil test family: exact construction, and why it does not yet prove RH

A useful next experiment is not to feed the raw deposit measure into a trace as if it were a test function. Instead, truncate it and build an admissible compactly supported *test vector* first. Put \(L=\log10\), choose \(g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\) with \(\operatorname{supp}g\subset[-a,a]\), and define
\[
f_{N,g}(u)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}d_n g(u-nL),\qquad d_n=\delta\Pi_n.
\]
This is a legitimate smooth compactly supported vector. For \(\widetilde f(u)=\overline{f(-u)}\), its autocorrelation \(F_{N,g}=f_{N,g}*\widetilde f_{N,g}\) is an admissible positive-type Weil test, with exact Fourier transform
\[
\widehat F_{N,g}(t)=|\widehat g(t)|^2\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}d_ne^{-itnL}\right|^2\ge0,
\]
and support
\[
\operatorname{supp}F_{N,g}\subset[-(N-1)L-2a,(N-1)L+2a].
\]
Thus the explicit formula gives an exact identity for every finite \(N\), including its prime-power, gamma, and pole terms. On the idele-class group, the corresponding norm-only test is invariant under the compact norm-one subgroup; Haar orthogonality kills every nontrivial compact character and leaves only the trivial-character (zeta) sector. This isolates zeta by an explicit projection, not by TTI cancellation.

The autocorrelation exposes what the decimal scale does and does not do. For \(N=1\), \(F_{1,g}=d_1^2(g*\widetilde g)\): translation by \(\log10\) cancels exactly, so the single-deposit test contains no decade information. For \(N>1\), the new terms are cross-correlations at shifts \(k\log10\), with coefficients \(c_{N,k}=\sum_{n=1}^{N-k}d_{n+k}d_n\); these create a genuine but finite windowed test family. Its positivity under the Weil functional is not implied by \(d_n>0\): positive type of the *test* is not Weil positivity of the explicit-formula functional.

There is a useful exact operator test at the first nontrivial truncation. From TTI, \(d_1=\varphi^{-1}\) and \(d_2=2\varphi^{-2}\), so \(d_2>d_1>0\). On log-scale test vectors let \(A_2=d_1\tau_L+d_2\tau_{2L}\), where \(\tau_a g(u)=g(u-a)\). Its Fourier multiplier is
\[
\widehat{A_2g}(t)=e^{-itL}(d_1+d_2e^{-itL})\widehat g(t),\qquad |d_1+d_2e^{-itL}|\ge d_2-d_1=2\varphi^{-2}-\varphi^{-1}>0.
\]
The reciprocal multiplier is smooth, periodic, and has bounded derivatives, so \(A_2\) is an automorphism of the Schwartz test space. More explicitly, since \(d_1/d_2=\varphi/2<1\),
\[
A_2^{-1}=\frac1{d_2}\sum_{m\ge0}\left(-\frac{d_1}{d_2}\right)^m\tau_{-(m+2)L};
\]
the geometric coefficients dominate the polynomial growth of Schwartz seminorms under translation, so the series converges in \(\mathcal S(\mathbb R)\). Write the Weil quadratic form as \(Q_W(f):=W(f*\widetilde f)\). Since \(A_2\) is a Schwartz automorphism, on a Schwartz domain where \(Q_W\) is continuous,
\[
Q_W(A_2g)\ge0\quad\text{for every }g
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Q_W(f)\ge0\quad\text{for every admissible }f.
\]
The right side is Weil's criterion and is equivalent to RH. This proves that the first two actual TTI deposit weights do define a nontrivial, invertible two-shift filter—but that demanding positivity for all filtered seeds is exactly as strong as RH, not a new proof. For compactly supported seeds the inverse need not preserve compact support; the Schwartz-domain extension and continuity must be stated when using the equivalence.

In fact the same finite-domain result holds for **every finite deposit truncation**. The exact increment is
\[
d_{n+1}-d_n=\varphi^{-(n+2)}+(-1)^n\varphi^{-(2n+3)}>0\qquad(n\ge1),
\]
so \(d_1<d_2<\cdots<d_N\). The Eneström–Kakeya annulus theorem applied to \(P_N(z)=\sum_{n=1}^N d_nz^{n-1}\) places all its roots within \(|z|\le\max_{1\le n<N}d_n/d_{n+1}<1\) (for \(N=1\), it is a nonzero constant). Thus \(e^{-itL}P_N(e^{-itL})\) never vanishes for real \(t\), and \(A_N=\sum_{n=1}^Nd_n\tau_{nL}\) is a Schwartz automorphism. So no finite positive deposit truncation creates a proper test subspace on Schwartz space: positivity on all such filtered seeds is always exactly full Weil positivity.

The infinite-truncation limit is different. Since \(d_n\to\varphi^2/\sqrt5\ne0\), the limiting multiplier has a unit-circle pole/Dirac-comb boundary at \(t=2\pi k/L\), as in §6.2; it is not a smooth Schwartz multiplier. Finite invertibility therefore does not justify an infinite-limit inverse or passing positivity through \(N\to\infty\). The raw finite-filter inverses in fact have no uniform bound (shown below); uniform Weil-form/tail estimates and any positivity passage remain unresolved.

There is a small but rigorous partial result on the uniform-in-\(N\) question. Write \(d_n=c+e_n\), where \(c=\varphi^2/\sqrt5\), and define \(M_N(q)=\sum_{n=1}^N d_nq^n\). For any \(N\)-th root of unity \(q\ne1\), the constant-tail geometric sum vanishes and \(M_N(q)=E_N(q):=\sum_{n=1}^N e_nq^n\). The absolutely convergent limit is
\[
E(q)=q\left(\frac{\varphi^{-4}/\sqrt5}{1+\varphi^{-2}q}-\frac{\varphi^{-1}}{1-\varphi^{-1}q}\right).
\]
Its numerator has no zero on \(|q|=1\): with \(r=\varphi^{-2}\), \(s=\varphi^{-1}\), and \(A=\varphi^{-4}/\sqrt5\), the reverse triangle inequality gives
\[
|E(q)|\ge \frac{s-A-(As+sr)}{(1+r)(1+s)}=\frac{s-A-(As+sr)}{\sqrt5}>0.
\]
Also \(\sup_{|q|=1}|E(q)-E_N(q)|\le (\varphi^{-2}/\sqrt5)r^{N+1}/(1-r)+s^{N+1}/(1-s)\), which is smaller than that explicit margin for \(N\ge7\). At \(q=1\), \(M_N(1)=\sum d_n\ge Nd_1\). Hence the multiplier has a uniform positive lower bound when sampled only at the \(N\)-th roots of unity, for all \(N\ge7\). This does **not** control the continuous unit circle between those sample points, so it proves neither a uniform inverse bound nor a Schwartz-operator estimate. It is a concrete reduction target: bound \(M_N(q)\) off the roots of unity or exhibit near-cancellation there.

The discrete bound does not extend to the full circle: there is an explicit near-cancellation sequence. Since \(E(1)=\varphi^{-3}/5-\varphi<0\), set \(x_*=-2\pi E(1)/c>0\) and \(q_N=\exp[-i(2\pi/N+x_*/N^2)]\). The exact decomposition above and the expansions
\[
\frac{q_N}{1-q_N}=-\frac{iN}{2\pi}+O(1),\qquad 1-q_N^N=\frac{i x_*}{N}+O(N^{-2}),\qquad E_N(q_N)=E(1)+O(N^{-1})
\]
give
\[
M_N(q_N)=c\frac{x_*}{2\pi}+E(1)+O(N^{-1})=O(N^{-1}).
\]
Here the tail in \(E_N-E\) is exponentially small, and \(E\) is analytic near 1. Retaining one more order gives \(N M_N(q_N)\to C_*\), where, with \(a=2\pi\),
\[
C_*=-c\frac{x_*^2}{a^2}-i c\left(\frac{x_*^2}{2a}+\frac{x_*}{2}\right)-iaE'(1),
\qquad E'(1)=\frac{s^2}{5\sqrt5}-\varphi^3.
\]
Its real part is \(-cx_*^2/a^2<0\), so \(C_*\ne0\) (numerically \(|C_*|\approx14.9945\)). Consequently \(|M_N(q_N)|\sim |C_*|/N\) along this sequence, and the inverse \(L^2\)-operator norms are at least asymptotic to \(N/|C_*|\); they cannot be uniform in \(N\). The sampled-root lower bound and this near-cancellation are consistent: \(q_N\) is displaced from the nearest \(N\)-th root by order \(N^{-2}\). This is a genuine obstruction to a naive unrenormalized infinite-filter inverse. It does not rule out a renormalized limit or establish any positivity passage through that limit.


There is also a well-defined *background-subtracted* infinite filter, which is distinct from the singular raw limit. Subtract the asymptotic constant coefficient \(c=\varphi^2/\sqrt5\) at each shift and set \(e_n=d_n-c\). Then \(\sum_{n\ge1}|e_n|(1+n)^k<\infty\) for every fixed \(k\), so \(R_N=\sum_{n=1}^N e_n\tau_{nL}\) converges to \(R_\infty=\sum_{n\ge1}e_n\tau_{nL}\) in every Schwartz operator seminorm. Its multiplier factors exactly. With \(s=\varphi^{-1}\), the coefficient relations \(A=s^4/\sqrt5\) and \(As+sr=s/\sqrt5\) give
\[
E(q)=-\frac{s}{\sqrt5}\,\frac{q(q+2)}{(1+s^2q)(1-sq)}.
\]
Thus its only numerator roots are \(0,-2\), while its denominator poles are \(-s^{-2},s^{-1}\); none lies on the unit circle. In particular \(R_\infty\) is a Schwartz automorphism. Its inverse multiplier is explicitly
\[
E(q)^{-1}=-\frac{\sqrt5}{s}\left(-s^3+\frac{1}{2q}+\frac{s^3-1/2}{q+2}\right),
\]
so the nontrivial inverse tail has an absolutely summable geometric expansion in shifts. Since the finite residual multipliers converge to \(E\) in \(C^\infty\) on the circle, their reciprocals also converge there for all sufficiently large \(N\). This supplies a rigorous renormalized limit with stable inverse—but the counterterm subtracting the constant tail is an extra choice, not a TTI operation, and the limiting filter remains an automorphism. Thus positivity on all renormalized filtered seeds is still full Weil positivity/RH-equivalent, not an independent positivity estimate. The raw positive-deposit limit remains singular; this subtraction does not justify passing positivity for the raw \(A_N\) through \(N\to\infty\).


This gives a clean RH gate. If one proves nonnegativity of the Weil quadratic form \(Q_W(g)=W(g*\widetilde g)\) for **all** admissible seed functions \(g\) and all required windows in a class dense enough to recover the full test space, the result is Weil positivity and hence RH. For one fixed seed, finite \(N\), or the one-decade autocorrelation, no such implication follows. The compact-window result of Zhu (arXiv:2608.24827v2) reports an unconditional certificate for test-vector support \([-0.8,0.8]\), whose autocorrelation is supported on \([-1.6,1.6]\) (radius 1.6), while reporting a steeply collapsing lower margin and a much larger computational barrier. For the two-shift TTI filter, after centering, the test vector has support radius \(L/2+a\approx1.1513+a\), and its autocorrelation has support radius \(L+2a\approx2.3026+2a\). This is beyond the certified radius 1.6; at most it can fall within the paper’s exploratory radius 2.38 if \(a\lesssim0.0387\), which is not a certificate. This is useful calibration, not an all-window theorem and not RH evidence.

A separate, recent computational lead is GitHub PR `teal-sea/zeta-lab#258` (posted and merged into that repository’s `main` on 2026-09-28). It reports a lower bound at `L=1.19` (autocorrelation support 2.38) for **real even** test functions, using an out-of-band correction to Zhu’s prime-comb envelope and two interval-arithmetic implementations. The PR says it is not kernel-checked and awaits external verification; its current page lists no reviewers. Merging into the project repository is not peer review. Treat the bound as a reported certificate claim, not an established source theorem, until the proof, code, archived matrices, and analytic reduction hypotheses have been independently audited. The lead is geometrically close to the two-shift TTI test radius `1.1513+a` (so fits under `L=1.19` only for `a≤0.0387`), but the reported even-sector-only bound does not by itself control arbitrary filtered seeds or the odd sector. It cannot establish RH, and neither this claim nor Zhu’s retracted exploratory 2.38 computation should be described as a certified all-sector result.

A potentially useful operator-theoretic lead is Connes–Consani, [*Weil positivity and Trace formula: the archimedean place*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.13771). Its archimedean-only theorem constructs a positive trace functional by compressing the scaling representation to Sonin space; the paper relates this positive trace to the archimedean Weil distribution up to a compact/quantized-calculus error, and develops a finite-semilocal extension. This is **not** positivity of the full zeta Weil form: the prime-place contributions and their interaction remain, and the paper explicitly treats full Weil positivity as RH-strength. The relevant TTI question is sharply limited: can any stated TTI map produce the Sonin projection or its finite-place analogue, and can its deposits control the residual prime terms? No such map or domination estimate is presently found. Do not conflate a positive archimedean compression with RH.

An additional third-party manuscript lead, Vincent Liu’s [*Certified Weil Positivity Beyond the Unit Window*](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.weil-positivity-riemann-zeta-bounds), reports computer-assisted bounds at test half-widths \(1\) and \(17/16\), and links an [immutable, checksummed reproduction release](https://github.com/luciferyu666/certified-weil-positivity/releases/tag/v1.0-mcom-submission). The release says the submission is frozen but external reproduction is not completed; the page labels it under review and disclaims journal acceptance. Treat these as claims to audit, not established theorems. The larger half-width \(17/16=1.0625\) is still below the centered two-shift TTI radius \(1.1513+a\), so even if verified it would not directly cover those TTI tests. No RH implication follows from fixed-window positivity.


A distinct external route is Connes’s finite-prime Weil-minimizer programme in [*The Riemann Hypothesis: Past, Present and a Letter Through Time*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04022), §§5–6. For cutoff \(x=\lambda^2\), it defines a compact-support Weil-form minimizer \(\eta_x\) and its recentering \(\theta_x(u)=\eta_x(x^{1/2}u)\); this positive dilation preserves whether Fourier-transform zeros are real. Theorem 6.1 gives real zeros of the minimizer’s Fourier transform **if** the lowest eigenvalue is simple and isolated with an even eigenfunction. The paper constructs a candidate \(k_\lambda\) from localized Hermite/prolate functions and the summation map \(\mathcal E\); Fact 6.4 proves \(\widehat k_\lambda\to\Xi\) locally uniformly on closed substrips, with a stated error bound. But it does not prove that \(k_\lambda\) approximates \(\theta_x\) well enough, nor the required simple/even ground-state property for the Weil operator; §6.6 names these as remaining steps. Thus the conditional Hurwitz chain is precise: prove the ground-state hypothesis and that \(\widehat{\theta_x}-\widehat{k_\lambda}\to0\) locally uniformly on the strip, then \(\widehat{\theta_x}\to\Xi\); Hurwitz would force every \(\Xi\)-zero in that strip to be real, yielding RH. Cutoff-13 matches are numerical, not this convergence theorem.

Akiva Groskin’s [independent numerical study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20224), v4 (14 August 2026), reports a public implementation over many cutoffs and strong empirical convergence. It explicitly makes no proof claim; its August revision withdraws a proposed Paley–Wiener mechanism after its preregistered test failed, and earlier negative-sign blocks were traced to finite archimedean integration cutoff artifacts. Treat its tables and code as reproducibility targets, not a theorem or evidence that the limit is RH-valid.

The summation map here is \(\mathcal E(f)(u)=u^{1/2}\sum_{m\ge1}f(mu)\), a sum over integer multiples in the original positive-real variable. Its exact log-coordinate form sharpens the comparison: set \(g(x)=e^{x/2}f(e^x)\), so \(f(e^x)=e^{-x/2}g(x)\). Then
\[
(\mathcal E f)(e^x)=\sum_{m\ge1}m^{-1/2}g(x+\log m).
\]
Thus it is a weighted translation sum over all integer-log shifts. Restricting it to the sparse terms \(m=10^n\) gives decade shifts with weights \(10^{-n/2}\) (up to reversing translation direction), not TTI's \(d_n=(F_{n+2}-1)\varphi^{-n}\to\varphi^2/\sqrt5>0\). These coefficient sequences are not proportional, and TTI states no operation removing the omitted integer shifts. The obvious diagonal reweighting is not a harmless fix: to turn the sparse E-coefficient \(10^{-n/2}\) into \(d_n\), it must multiply the \(n\)-th decade shift by \(w_n=d_n10^{n/2}\sim (\varphi^2/\sqrt5)10^{n/2}\). Such exponentially growing weights do not define a continuous translation-series operator on all of \(\mathcal S(\mathbb R)\): choose a Schwartz function with subexponential tails, e.g. \(g(x)=\exp(-\sqrt{1+x^2})\), and the positive terms \(w_ng(nL)\) fail even to tend to zero. This excludes the natural coefficient-by-coefficient reweighting as a Schwartz intertwiner. It does not exclude every conceivable map: a different weighted projection or conjugation must be defined and justified. The cited spectral-triples paper uses \(\mathcal E\) within its Weil/prolate construction; it does not identify it with TTI's deposit filter. The TTI connection is sharply constrained: the background-subtracted \(R_\infty\) is a fixed Schwartz automorphism, so a sequence is relatively compact in \(\mathcal S\) exactly when its \(R_\infty\)-image is; likewise for each fixed \(A_N\). A fixed invertible filter also cannot improve convergence of \(\theta_x-k_\lambda\) in the Schwartz topology. A cutoff-dependent TTI smoothing could behave differently, but no TTI rule defines one, and the raw \(A_N\) inverse norms are nonuniform. There is no map to \(\eta_x\) or cutoff parameter \(x\) in the current framework; absent a new, explicit x-dependent construction and estimates, the finite-prime minimizer route is context, not a TTI proof.

**Exact useful outcome:** TTI deposits can be converted, after finite truncation and a declared seed, into valid Weil-test vectors; the explicit formula then supplies a real operator/trace identity. **Failure to prove RH:** the map is chosen by adding the seed, truncation, and compact-character projection; the resulting identity is the standard Weil formula, and the missing assertion is precisely positivity on a complete family, RH-equivalent. The raw infinite deposit limit is not in the compactly supported test domain; proving a distributional-trace extension and its cutoff limit would be an additional theorem, not a formal passage.

**TTI-adapted discrete-curvature coordinates.** The prefix basis admits a second exact coordinate choice that may expose the asymptotic constant-tail structure more efficiently. Set \(A_1,A_2\) as the first two columns and, for \(m\ge3\), use \(D_m=A_m-2A_{m-1}+A_{m-2}\). This is a unimodular triangular change of basis, so it leaves the finite Weil matrix's inertia unchanged. With coefficients padded by zero outside their degree, the constant cosine coefficient is \(\Delta^2 C_{m,0}=d_m^2-d_{m-1}^2\). For \(1\le k\le m-1\), the second-difference autocorrelation coefficient is \(\Delta^2 C_{m,k}=C_{m,k}-2C_{m-1,k}+C_{m-2,k}\); this reduces to \(d_m d_{m-k}-d_{m-1}d_{m-1-k}\) for \(1\le k\le m-2\), while the top autocorrelation coefficient is \(d_1d_m\). Since the actual cosine coefficient is twice the autocorrelation coefficient for \(k\ge1\), the top cosine coefficient is \(2d_1d_m\). For the idealized constant sequence \(d_n=c\), these second differences cancel every lower mode and leave \(D_m(\theta)=2c^2\cos((m-1)\theta)\) for \(m\ge3\). More precisely, set \(d_0=0\). Along a fixed distance \(j=m-k\) from the top frequency, the coefficient of \(\cos(k\theta)\) in \(D_m\) tends to \(2c(d_j-d_{j-1})\); the increments \(d_j-d_{j-1}\) decay exponentially, while every fixed low-frequency coefficient tends to zero. Thus the coefficient matrix has an asymptotic upper-triangular Toeplitz edge profile with exponentially decaying off-diagonals, not an exactly diagonal form. This profile can in fact be analyzed exactly. Put \(r=\varphi^{-1}\), \(\alpha=r^2/\sqrt5\), and \(\delta_1=d_1=r\), \(\delta_j=d_j-d_{j-1}\) for \(j\ge2\). From the closed form \(d_n=c-\alpha(-r^2)^n-r^n\), direct summation gives
\[
\sum_{j\ge1}\delta_jz^{j-1}=\frac{r}{(1-rz)(1+r^2z)},\qquad
q(z):=2c\sum_{j\ge1}\delta_jz^{j-1}=\frac{2cr}{(1-rz)(1+r^2z)}.
\]
Index cosine rows by \(k+1\) and columns by \(m\). The limiting edge matrix is upper-triangular Toeplitz with symbol \(q\), whose reciprocal is the degree-two polynomial \((1-rz)(1+r^2z)/(2cr)\). Its finite sections and their inverses are therefore uniformly bounded on \(\ell^2\); explicitly, the inverse norm is at most \((1+2r^3)/(2cr)\) by the \(\ell^1\) coefficient bound. Since the strict increase of \(d_n\) was already proved above, \(\delta_j>0\), whence \(\|T\|_2\le\|q\|_1=q(1)=2c^2<3\).

The exact TTI coefficient matrix differs from these sections by a Hilbert–Schmidt tail. Indeed, \(|d_n-c|\le (1+\alpha)r^n\), and for \(m\ge3\) each column residual has norm at most \(C\sqrt m\,r^{m-1}\), with \(C=4c(1+\alpha)(1+r)\); the first two columns are a finite exception. Hence the residual restricted to columns \(m>K\) tends to zero in operator norm, with the explicit bound
\[
\|E_{>K}\|_{HS}\le C\sqrt{x^K\left(\frac{K+1}{1-x}+\frac{x}{(1-x)^2}\right)},\qquad x=r^2.
\]
The exact rational-interval check in `scripts/certify-tti-curvature-toeplitz.py`, using \(2.236067977499789696<\sqrt5<2.236067977499789697\), gives at \(K=9\) the bounds \(\|E_{>9}\|_{HS}<0.483789\) and \(1/(2L)>0.491535\), where \(L=(1+2r^3)/(2cr)\) bounds every inverse Toeplitz section. Thus the tail block is invertible by a Neumann series, with inverse norm at most \(2L\). The leading \(9\times9\) block is upper triangular with positive diagonal \(d_1^2,2d_1d_2,2d_1d_3,\ldots\), and the off-diagonal block is uniformly bounded. Block inversion proves that \(\widetilde B_M\) and \(\widetilde B_M^{-1}\) are uniformly bounded for all \(M\): the TTI discrete-curvature coordinate matrices are uniformly well-conditioned in the ordinary Euclidean cosine-coefficient norm. The companion exact-interval script also bounds every leading-head inverse (sizes 1–9) by 5, the first-nine-column Toeplitz correction by 2, the off-diagonal block by 3.24, the tail inverse by 2.04, and the full basis and inverse norms by 5.24 and 40.1; hence \(\kappa_2(\widetilde B_M)<211\) for every \(M\). This gives a uniformly conditioned TTI-adapted basis, not positivity: the induced Weil matrices remain congruent and keep their inertia. A separate unit-column-normalized, double-precision Jacobi diagnostic gives \(\kappa(B_M)=15.07,109.87,323.98,664.46,1130.93,1722.83\) and \(\kappa(\widetilde B_M)=2.98,3.36,3.49,3.54,3.56,3.57\) at \(M=4,8,12,16,20,24\), respectively. This is numerical motivation, not a theorem on prefix conditioning; its reproducible diagnostic is `scripts/diagnose-tti-curvature-conditioning.py`. A separate exact rational-interval comparison now certifies one matched unit-column normalization at \(M=4\): \(\kappa_2(B_{\rm prefix})>8\), while \(\kappa_2(B_{\rm curvature})<\sqrt{40}<6.33\). The verifier is `scripts/certify-tti-unit-column-comparison.py`; it concerns basis maps only, not the Weil form. General finite-section and inverse-decay context is given by Thomas Strohmer, [*Four Short Stories about Toeplitz Matrix Calculations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0010243), which treats off-diagonal decay, finite-section error bounds, and Toeplitz preconditioning; see also Gröchenig–Rzeszotnik–Strohmer, [*Convergence Analysis of the Finite Section Method and Banach Algebras of Matrices*](https://homepage.univie.ac.at/karlheinz.groechenig/preprints/fsm2008fin2.pdf). These sources motivate the method but do not prove this TTI-specific bound, which follows from its rational symbol and exponential coefficient decay. The classical [Fejér–Riesz factorization](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4020-5125-8_1) says every nonnegative one-variable trigonometric polynomial has some squared-modulus spectral factor, but TTI fixes the coefficients to \(d_n\); the theorem does not show that TTI prefixes or positive mixtures generate the whole nonnegative cone. This distinction prevents the coefficient-space basis result from being mistaken for an admissible-history positivity theorem.

Two recent preprints are relevant as research leads but do not close an RH proof. Yaoming Shi, [*Construction of Finite Hilbert–Pólya Matrices from Weil’s Explicit Formula*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04908), builds arithmetic finite matrices and a contrast-space Hermitian pencil from the Weil formula. Its exact finite zero-side reconstruction takes the ordinates as input, while the paper identifies the remaining arithmetic-to-zero step as a relative prime-to-zero perturbation theorem in the compressed metric, plus control of the limiting spectrum. The paper itself states no RH proof; its numerical finite-cutoff matches are not that missing transfer theorem. A TTI use would require an explicit embedding of its seed-generated tests into the matrix test class and estimates in the quotient metric; none is supplied by the TTI rules audited here.

Michael Shaughnessy, [*Quasicrystal Scattering and the Riemann Hypothesis*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03673), explicitly claims a proof, but Appendix A does not prove RH. First, the asserted prime-log measure \(\chi=\sum_p\delta_{\log p}\) is not tempered. The function \(\psi(x)=\exp(-(1+x^2)^{1/4})\) is Schwartz, but PNT gives \(\pi(e^{X+1})-\pi(e^X)\asymp e^X/X\); each prime in that block has \(\psi(\log p)\gg e^{-\sqrt{X+O(1)}}\). Hence the block contribution is \(\gg e^{X-\sqrt{X+O(1)}}/X\to\infty\), so \(\sum_p\psi(\log p)\) diverges. Thus \(\chi\) is not tempered and its ordinary Fourier transform is not defined in that sense. Independently, the step “varying zero real parts would make the Fourier spectrum two-dimensional, contradicting one-dimensional Fourier duality” is invalid: Fourier transformation on \(\mathbb R\) does not convert scale-dependent amplitudes into a second domain dimension. Its prime-only sum \(\sum_p p^{-s}\) is not the von Mangoldt expansion \(-\zeta'/\zeta=\sum_{p,r\ge1}(\log p)p^{-rs}\); the manuscript does not justify a transformation from its unweighted prime sum to the prime-power/log-weighted expansion used in the explicit formula. These are fatal gaps in the stated proof, not merely missing exposition, and the article gives no TTI bridge.
