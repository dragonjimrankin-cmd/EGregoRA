Limb IV

Astrology

♈ ♉ ♊ ♋ ♌ ♍ ♎ ♏ ♐ ♑ ♒ ♓  ·  ☉ ☽ ☿ ♀ ♂ ♃ ♄ ♅ ♆ ♇  ·  ☌ ☍ △ □ ⚹ ☊ ☋ ⚷ ℞

The oldest continuously practised discipline in the world, the parent of astronomy, the mother of the calendar — and, as a physical theory, wrong. All four statements are true at once, and this page refuses to drop any of them.

Begin Here

Two Questions, Not One

Nearly every argument about astrology collapses because two entirely separate questions are being asked in the same sentence.

Does the sky cause events in a human life? ◆ This is an empirical question with an empirical answer, and the answer, after a century of testing, is no. Not weakly, not ambiguously — the controlled studies fail.

Is astrology worth anything? ◆ This is a different question entirely, and the answer is obviously yes. It built the calendar. It drove the observational astronomy that eventually killed it. It gave four millennia of human beings a working vocabulary for character, time, fate and temperament, and nothing has replaced that vocabulary — we simply pretend we no longer need one.

The order's position is that you can hold both without discomfort, and that the discomfort people feel is a symptom of thinking that only causal things are real. A map is not a force. A language is not a mechanism. Neither is useless for failing to be one.

Grading throughout: ◆ established science · ◆ scholarship and history · ◆ speculative and contested · ◆ myth, tradition and primary esoteric text.

The Hard Part First

What the Testing Found

This order puts the evidence before the romance, including — especially — where the evidence goes against something we love.

Carlson, 1985 ◆

The Nature Study

Shawn Carlson ran a double-blind test designed with the participation of the astrological community, who agreed the protocol was fair before it began — which is what makes it hard to dismiss. Twenty-eight astrologers, selected as among the best available, were given a birth chart and three personality profiles, one belonging to the subject.

They had predicted they would identify the right profile at least half the time. They scored at chance: one in three.

Dean & Kelly, 2003 ◆

The Time Twins

The cleanest natural experiment available. More than 2,000 people born in London within minutes of each other in March 1958 were followed for decades, with over a hundred measured variables — IQ, anxiety, aggression, sociability, occupation, marital status, ability.

If the moment of birth imprints a temperament, these people should resemble each other more than chance allows. The correlations were essentially zero across every variable tested.

Agreement ◆

Astrologers Disagree With Each Other

The least-discussed finding and arguably the most damaging. Given the same chart, independent astrologers produce readings that correlate poorly with one another. A discipline with a real signal should show convergence between competent practitioners, as radiologists do.

Dean's meta-analyses across roughly 300 studies found no effect that survived when the astrologer did not know the subject, and no consistency where they should not have known.

Barnum ◆

Why It Feels So Accurate

Forer's 1948 demonstration: give a class of students an identical personality sketch, tell each it is theirs, and they rate it about 4.3 out of 5 for accuracy. "You have a great need for others to like you, yet you tend to be critical of yourself."

Add selective recall — the hits are vivid, the misses never encoded — plus the reader's own work in finding the fit, and the subjective experience of uncanny accuracy is fully accounted for without any planet doing anything.

No special pleading. The standard rescues — that astrology is "too complex to test", that a real astrologer must meet the client, that it works only for believers — would each, if true, place the claim outside evidence entirely. That is a decision to stop making a factual claim. It is allowed, and it has a price: you may no longer say that it works, only that it means something. This page is written on the assumption that meaning is worth plenty.

The Apparatus

How a Chart Is Actually Built

If you are going to use the thing, know how it is made. Most people defending or attacking astrology have never looked inside one.

The Twelve Signs ♈ ♉ ♊ ♋ ♌ ♍ ♎ ♏ ♐ ♑ ♒ ♓

Thirty-degree divisions of the ecliptic, measured from the spring equinox — not the constellations, which are irregular and nothing like equal. Each carries an element (fire, earth, air, water) and a mode (cardinal, fixed, mutable). The element-and-mode grid is the actual engine of interpretation; the animal names are decoration.

The Planets ☉ ☽ ☿ ♀ ♂ ♃ ♄ ♅ ♆ ♇

Seven classical bodies — Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn — plus three modern ones discovered in 1781, 1846 and 1930. Each is read as a drive rather than a thing: Mars as assertion, Venus as value and attraction, Saturn as limit and consequence.

Note the awkwardness that the tradition absorbed three new planets without ever having predicted their effects beforehand.

The Twelve Houses I II III IV V VI VII VIII IX X XI XII

Domains of life — self, resources, communication, home, creativity, work, partnership, death and inheritance, belief, vocation, community, the hidden — fixed to the local horizon at the moment of birth, which is why the exact minute and place matter.

There are at least a dozen incompatible house systems in use. They disagree substantially, and no test has ever favoured one.

The Aspects ☌ ☍ △ □ ⚹

Angular relationships between bodies: conjunction at 0°, opposition at 180°, trine at 120°, square at 90°, sextile at 60°. The geometry is genuinely the heart of the practice — a chart is read as a web of tensions and supports, not as a list of positions.

Kepler, who cared about this deeply, tried to put the aspects on a musical and harmonic footing. See Limb III.

The Big Three ☉ ☽ Asc

Sun sign (the conscious self), Moon sign (the interior and emotional), Ascendant or rising sign (the presented surface). Newspaper astrology uses only the first, which is why it is so thin — it is a chart with ninety-five percent of the information discarded.

Transits and Returns ♄ ℞

Where current positions fall against the birth chart. The Saturn return around ages 29 and 58 is the famous one, and the reason it lands is not astronomical: those are the ages at which almost everyone confronts structural questions about the life they have built.

The Standard Objection

“Your Sign Is Wrong Because of Precession”

This is the most common attack on astrology and it is, on its own terms, a misfire — which is worth saying even though we think the practice fails for other reasons.

Earth's axis wobbles like a slowing top, one full circuit in about 25,800 years. Since the zodiac was fixed in Babylon and Greece, the constellations have slipped roughly one sign against the seasons.

But Western astrology is tropical: it measures from the moment of the spring equinox and is deliberately anchored to the seasonal year, not to the background stars. Vedic astrology is sidereal and tracks the actual constellations. Both systems know about precession — Hipparchus described it around 130 BCE — and neither is in error by its own definitions.

Ophiuchus is likewise a non-event. The Sun has always passed through it; constellation boundaries are a 1930 International Astronomical Union convention drawn for astronomers' convenience, and a zodiac was never a list of constellations in the first place.

If you want to argue against astrology, argue from Carlson and Dean. Precession is a rhetorical win that does not survive contact with anyone who knows the system.

✦

The Long Hand ◆

The Precessional Ages

The same wobble that causes precession drags the equinox point slowly backwards through the zodiac, about one sign every 2,150 years. The ages are named for where it sits: Taurus, then Aries, then Pisces, and — eventually — Aquarius.

The symbolic correspondences are striking enough to be worth looking at honestly. The Age of Taurus coincides with bull cults from Crete to Çatalhöyük. Aries with the ram-headed Amun, the sacrifice of lambs, and the rise of the warrior empires. Pisces with two thousand years of a faith whose first symbol was a fish, drawn from two intersecting circles.

And the honest part: ◆ this is pattern-matching after the fact, on a system with no agreed boundaries. Proposed start dates for the Age of Aquarius span roughly five hundred years, from the nineteenth century to the twenty-seventh. A framework that cannot date its own epochs to within half a millennium cannot be said to be measuring anything.

Read it as humanity's longest-running piece of symbolic self-description — which is genuinely remarkable — and not as a clock.

Practice

Using a Chart Honestly

Given everything above, what is left? Quite a lot, provided you are precise about what you are doing.

1 · Treat it as a mirror, not a transmitter

A chart is a structured set of prompts covering domains you would never systematically visit on your own — death, inheritance, the hidden, the enemies you have made. Being handed an image and asked "where is this true of you?" produces far more honest material than "tell me about yourself." The insight is real. It is yours, not Saturn's.

2 · Say “this image suggests”, never “the transit means”

One phrasing keeps the authority with the person; the other hands it to the sky. In practice this single change in language removes nearly all the harm astrology is capable of doing.

3 · Never make a decision you could not otherwise defend

If the chart points somewhere you can argue for independently, act. If it points somewhere you cannot, you have found a wish, not a reading. Use it to generate options, never to authorise them.

4 · Watch for predictive fatalism

This is where astrology does genuine damage. People have stayed in bad marriages, declined treatment and postponed their lives because a practitioner told them to wait for a transit to clear. Any reading that reduces your agency is a bad reading, whatever its technical merit.

5 · Learn the astronomy alongside it

The strongest recommendation on this page. Go out and actually find Jupiter. Watch the Moon's phase for a full month. Mark where the Sun rises against the horizon at the solstices. You will get more from three months of looking up than from thirty books of interpretation — and you will be doing the thing the first astrologers were doing, which was paying extraordinarily close attention.

Lineage

Four Thousand Years of Looking Up

  • c. 1800 BCE Babylonian omen texts — the Enūma Anu Enlil tablets read the sky for the fate of the king and the state, never for individuals.
  • c. 500 BCE The twelve-sign zodiac of equal 30° divisions is fixed in Mesopotamia.
  • c. 410 BCE The oldest known individual horoscope, cuneiform, from Babylon.
  • c. 130 BCE Hipparchus discovers precession by comparing his star positions to records 150 years older.
  • c. 150 CE Ptolemy writes the Tetrabiblos alongside the Almagest — the same man, the same method, astrology and astronomy as one discipline.
  • c. 800 Baghdad's House of Wisdom preserves, translates and extends the Greek material while Europe forgets it.
  • 1277 The Condemnations at Paris attack astrological determinism as a threat to free will — the theological objection, not the scientific one.
  • 1543 Copernicus moves the Earth. The symbolic system carries on using geocentric positions, which it still does.
  • 1596–1619 Kepler casts horoscopes for a living, writes contemptuously of common astrology, and seeks a harmonic basis for the aspects in Harmonices Mundi.
  • 1666 The Académie des Sciences formally excludes astrology. The professional divorce of astronomy and astrology is complete.
  • 1930 R. H. Naylor writes a column on Princess Margaret's birth for the Sunday Express; the newspaper sun-sign horoscope is invented, and the public image of astrology is set for a century.
  • 1948 Forer demonstrates the Barnum effect with a sketch assembled from a newsstand astrology book.
  • 1985 Carlson publishes the double-blind test in Nature.
  • 2003 Dean and Kelly publish the time-twins study.
  • 2010s– Astrology apps bring a 4,000-year-old symbolic language to tens of millions of phones, mostly stripped of everything that made it interesting.

Glossary

Ecliptic
The apparent annual path of the Sun across the sky — the plane of Earth's orbit, seen from inside it.
Tropical zodiac
Signs measured from the spring equinox, anchored to the seasons. Standard in the West.
Sidereal zodiac
Signs measured against the actual constellations. Standard in Vedic astrology. Currently about 24° apart from the tropical.
Ascendant
The degree of the ecliptic rising on the eastern horizon at the moment of birth. Changes roughly every two hours, which is why birth time matters.
Aspect
An angular relationship between two bodies — the geometric core of chart interpretation.
Orb
The tolerance allowed around an exact aspect. Generous orbs are where a great deal of unfalsifiability enters.
Transit
A current planetary position forming an aspect to a birth chart position.
Saturn return
Saturn's return to its birth position, roughly every 29.5 years. The best-known transit and the most psychologically plausible.
Precession
The 25,800-year wobble of Earth's axis, which shifts the equinox point against the stars.
Barnum effect
The tendency to accept vague, broadly applicable statements as specifically and strikingly true of oneself.

Where This Order Stands

Astrology does not work as a physical theory, and the order will not soften that to keep anyone comfortable. The tests were fair, some were designed with astrologers' agreement, and they failed.

And: it is the longest-running attempt humans have made to read meaning into time, it invented the observational rigour that eventually refuted it, and it preserves a vocabulary of temperament and season that our own age is conspicuously poorer for lacking. Kepler kept casting charts while despising the practice of his contemporaries, and he was not a fool.

Use it as a mirror and it is a fine instrument. Use it as a forecast and it will quietly take your agency away. The sky is not writing about you — but four thousand years of people watching it very carefully is a library, and we are not in the business of burning libraries.
