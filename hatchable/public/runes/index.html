Limb II · ix

Runes, Charms & Natural Energies

An alphabet, a grammar of small made things, and a word — energy — that has been so thoroughly abused that we had better define it before we use it once.

Begin Here

They Were Letters

The single most important fact about the runes is the one most often left out: the Elder Futhark is a writing system, used to write ordinary things.

It appears in the second century CE across Germanic-speaking Europe, almost certainly adapted from an Old Italic alphabet, and the surviving corpus — some four hundred-odd Elder Futhark inscriptions — is mostly names. Maker's marks. Ownership tags. "Hariso made this." A comb inscribed with the word comb. ◆ Scholarship, and not a disappointment: the shapes are angular because they were cut across the grain of wood and into stone, which is also why so few of them have horizontal strokes.

Some inscriptions are charms, and plainly so — the Lindholm amulet, the Kragehul spear-shaft, bracteates carrying the word alu. So the magical use is genuine and attested. ◆ But the modern system of twenty-four meanings, elemental correspondences, reversed readings and three-rune spreads is a twentieth-century construction, largely from Guido von List's invented Armanen set of 1908 and from the divinatory tarot it borrowed its shape from. The order uses that system. It simply refuses to lie about where it came from.

Grading: ◆ established · ◆ scholarship and history · ◆ speculative · ◆ myth and tradition.

✦

Plate I

The Elder Futhark, Cut

Twenty-four staves, in their attested order, drawn here as strokes rather than set in type — because the stroke order and the absence of curves is the whole physical argument of the script. The glosses are the traditional ones; the first column is simply the sound.

The three groups of eight — the ættir — are attested on the Kylver stone (c. 400 CE) and the Vadstena bracteate, so the ordering is real and ancient. The names, however, are reconstructed backwards from the much later Anglo-Saxon, Norwegian and Icelandic rune poems. ◆ We do not have a Proto-Germanic speaker telling us what they called the fourteenth rune. Perthro is a scholarly guess at a word nobody is sure of.

Limb VIII · Required Reading

The Theft of the Staves

You cannot study this alphabet honestly and walk past this, so we will not.

Guido von List, blind after a cataract operation in 1902, claimed to have received an eighteen-rune "Armanen" futhark — a set that never existed, built to fit the eighteen charms of the Hávamál and an Austrian-German racial mysticism. ◆ Myth, and a deliberate one. Through the Ariosophists it passed to Karl Maria Wiligut and from there into the SS, where Sowilo was doubled into the Schutzstaffel insignia, Algiz became the Lebensrune on birth announcements and gravestones, Tiwaz marked Hitler Youth leadership courses, and Odal was stitched onto a division that murdered civilians.

None of this made the regime occult, and we are careful about that claim: the Nazi state was an industrial atrocity run by a bureaucracy, and its leadership was largely contemptuous of the mystics it tolerated. The runes were not a source of its power. They were stolen decoration — antiquity borrowed to make a new and murderous movement look like an inheritance rather than an invention. That is the only accurate reading, and it is worse than the occult one, not better.

The order's position: the Elder Futhark belongs to the people who cut it into combs and spear-shafts seventeen centuries ago, and not to its thieves. Learn which shapes still carry that freight in public — Algiz, Odal, the doubled Sowilo — know why, and do not be naive about who you are standing next to when you wear one. Reclamation is possible; it is done by knowing the history out loud, never by pretending there isn't one.

See also: the Black Tribunal material, on enforced integration with the choices of previous beings — nothing disowned, nothing romanticised.

Practice

Four Honest Workings

Cut a Set

Twenty-four pieces of a fallen branch, one rune each, cut rather than drawn — the tradition is emphatic that the stave is carved. You will learn the shapes properly in an afternoon because your hands will, and you will never again mistake Algiz for Eihwaz. Leave the twenty-fifth blank piece out of it.

Read Three, Honestly

Situation, obstacle, what is actually being asked of you. Draw, then say the reading aloud before you consult any book. What the staves reliably do is externalise — they give a stalled mind a random prompt and permission to say the thing it already knew. ◆ That mechanism is real and is enough. Do not use them for medical or legal decisions.

One Charm, Properly Made

All five components from Plate III: a material you gathered, a line of words you wrote, a cutting or knotting act, a chosen hour, and a carrier you will actually keep on you. Make it for one specific thing. Then — this is the part people skip — retire it when the thing is done, and say so.

The Measurable Energies

Twenty minutes of morning light in the eyes within an hour of waking; two hours a week under trees; bare feet on cold ground not because of electrons but because of the shock and the attention it compels. ◆ These are the strongest interventions on this page and the least glamorous, which is the usual arrangement.

What Holds Up

  • ◆ The Elder Futhark is a genuine second-century script with a fixed, attested order in three ættir.
  • ◆ Charm and amulet use of runes is real and documented — Lindholm, Kragehul, the bracteates, alu.
  • ◆ The five-part charm grammar recurs independently across unrelated cultures.
  • ◆ Randomised prompts genuinely help stalled decision-making by forcing articulation.
  • ◆ Carried objects function as effective commitment cues under stress.
  • ◆ Forest exposure, daylight timing, cold and heat all have real, measured physiological effects.

What Does Not

  • ◆ The Armanen futhark. Eighteen runes received in a vision in 1902. Not ancient, and politically poisoned at the root.
  • ◆ The blank rune. Introduced in 1982. Appears in no historical set.
  • ◆ Reversed meanings. Imported from tarot; several staves are symmetrical and cannot reverse at all.
  • ◆ Ley lines as energy currents. Alfred Watkins proposed sightlines of old tracks in 1921; the energy reading arrives in the 1960s and nothing has ever been detected.
  • ◆ Orgone. No instrument has found it in eighty years.
  • ◆ Earthing claims. The studies are small, unblinded and largely funded by people selling mats.
  • ◆ That any rune is unlucky. Hagalaz means hail. Hail is weather.
✦

A Line Through It

  • c. 150 CE The Vimose comb — among the earliest datable runic objects. It says harja, probably a name.
  • c. 400 The Kylver stone preserves the full futhark in order; the ættir are confirmed.
  • c. 500 The Lindholm amulet: unambiguous charm use, including a long string of repeated staves.
  • c. 800 The Younger Futhark reduces to sixteen staves as the language changes — fewer letters, more ambiguity.
  • c. 1000 Anglo-Saxon futhorc expands to thirty-three; the rune poems begin to fix names.
  • c. 1300 Codex Runicus — an entire legal code in runes, long after Christianisation. Letters to the end.
  • 1639 Denmark bans runic writing as sorcery; the script's magical reputation is partly a product of its suppression.
  • 1908 Von List publishes the invented Armanen set.
  • 1933–45 The staves are conscripted by the SS; Sowilo, Algiz, Tiwaz and Odal are turned into insignia.
  • 1982 Ralph Blum's Book of Runes; divination as practised today is largely downstream of it.
  • now Careful scholarship and careless commerce run in parallel. Tell them apart by whether the source names its sources.

Reading

  • ◆ R. I. Page, Runes (British Museum, 1987) — short, authoritative, bracingly unimpressed by mysticism.
  • ◆ Terje Spurkland, Norwegian Runes and Runic Inscriptions (2005) — what the stones actually say.
  • ◆ Nicholas Goodrick-Clarke, The Occult Roots of Nazism (1985) — the essential, careful account of von List, Wiligut and the Ariosophists.
  • ◆ Owen Davies, Popular Magic: Cunning-folk in English History (2003) — charms as they were really commissioned and paid for.
  • ◆ Qing Li, Shinrin-Yoku (2018) — the forest-bathing research, read with attention to effect sizes.
  • ◆ The Hávamál, stanzas 138–164 — Odin on the tree, and the eighteen charms. Primary myth, thirteenth-century manuscript.

Closing Stance

Cut the stave. Know the history. Claim nothing you cannot measure.

The runes do not need to be magic to be worth a life's attention. They are a writing system invented by people who had no writing, cut into wood by hands that then carried the comb home — and that is a stranger and better fact than any power attributed to them since. Honour them by reading them accurately, including the part of their history that is shameful.
