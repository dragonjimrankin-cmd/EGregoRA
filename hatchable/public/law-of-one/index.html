Limb VII · i — a sub-limb of God, the Law of One & the Contact Question

The Law of One

A channeled cosmology dictated, it claims, by a sixth-density intelligence called Ra and taken down over three years in a Louisville living room. This order reads it more closely than any other modern revelation — not as scripture, but as philosophy, and as the single most internally coherent document of its kind.

The Order's Position ◆

Shelved, Without Embarrassment

Let us say plainly what this page is and is not. The Law of One makes claims about the structure of reality that cannot be tested and are not, by any stretch, established. We shelve it with the vision mark and do not pretend otherwise.

What earns it a room of its own is different. Across five books and some two thousand questions, the material holds a single metaphysics with almost no self-contradiction: one Creator knowing itself through its parts, a ladder of densities climbed by the discipline of love or the discipline of wisdom, free will so sacred that even the highest intelligences refuse to break it. It declines the falsifiable predictions its imitators trade on. And it closes nearly every session by telling the reader to keep only what resonates and discard the rest — an extraordinary posture for a text that could easily demand obedience.

We read it, then, as a serious work of esoteric philosophy and as a primary document of the twentieth-century contact imagination — the way one reads the Corpus Hermeticum or the Neoplatonists. Not because it is proven. Because it is thought through, and thinking is the subject of this order.

Begin Here ◆

What the Material Actually Is

Before the cosmology, the record — because the record is a matter of history, and history can be checked.

The circle. ◆ The material comes from L/L Research, a small group in Louisville, Kentucky: Don Elkins, a former physics professor and airline pilot who had spent two decades investigating the paranormal; Carla Rueckert, the instrument; and later Jim McCarty, who joined as scribe and editor. From 1981 until Elkins's death in 1984 — and in a longer second arc from 1981 through the 1990s — the group held sittings in which Rueckert entered a trance, and an intelligence identifying itself as Ra answered questions put to it.

The method. ◆ This was not automatic writing or a Ouija board. Elkins (and later McCarty) asked prepared questions aloud; Rueckert, in trance, spoke the answers in a flat, formal, deliberately archaic register; McCarty took them down. The sessions were tape recorded and transcribed. Rueckert reported no memory of the content while tranced. Whatever one makes of it, the process is documented in extraordinary detail — the group published the raw transcripts, its own doubts, and even the "instrument's" physical cost.

The voice. ◆ Ra describes itself as a social memory complex of the sixth density — not a single being but a merged, collective consciousness — and claims two prior contacts with Earth: among the Atlanteans some eleven thousand years ago, and in Egypt around the reign of Akhenaten, the pharaoh who briefly turned his kingdom toward the worship of one god, the sun-disc Aten. Ra says both contacts were, in its telling, failures — the message of unity was distorted into hierarchy and empire. It is a striking admission for a revelatory text to make about itself.

The five books — The Law of One, Books I–V — were published between 1982 and 1998 and remain in print, freely distributed by L/L Research. The material seeded much of what is now called New Age vocabulary: the "densities," "wanderers," "service to others," "harvest."

Eight Plates

The Architecture of the Teaching

The whole system turns on one sentence and its consequences. Here is the sentence, then the machinery built from it.

Plate I ◆

The Law Itself: All Is One

The first and governing claim. There is no separation. Every person, stone, star and galaxy is a distortion — a local inflection — of a single infinite Creator. "All things are one. There is no wrong way. There is no polarity." Evil and good, self and other, are not ultimate; they are surface ripples on one substance that has chosen, in order to know itself, to appear as many.

This is not a fuzzy sentiment in the material; it is the axiom everything else is derived from, the way a geometry is derived from its postulates.

Plate II ◆

Intelligent Infinity and the Distortions

The source Ra calls intelligent infinity — undifferentiated, infinite, conscious, without parts. It cannot be described, only approached through its distortions, the successive "bendings" by which the One becomes a universe.

The first distortion is free will — the Creator's decision to allow itself to be surprised. The second is the Logos, creative love or light, the shaping principle. The third is light itself. From these the whole cosmos unfolds, and free will — being first — outranks everything, including truth and even love. This is why non-interference is absolute in the material.

Plate III ◆

The Densities

Existence is not flat. It is a ladder of seven densities — octaves of being — with an eighth that begins the next octave, as the eighth note of a scale begins the next scale:

  • First — earth, water, air, fire: the awareness of being.
  • Second — plant and animal: growth, movement, the awareness of other.
  • Third — self-consciousness: the density of choice. This is humanity's present station, and the only one where the great choice is made.
  • Fourth — love and understanding.
  • Fifth — wisdom and light.
  • Sixth — the balance of love and wisdom into unity. Ra's own density.
  • Seventh — the gateway, the completion of the octave, where the soul turns back toward the source.

Each density has its "lesson," and a being advances only by mastering it — there is no skipping.

Plate IV ◆

The Veil of Forgetting

Why would a being choose to be born blind to its own divinity? The material's answer is its most elegant move. In third density a veil is drawn: the soul forgets that it is the Creator, forgets its past lives, forgets that its enemy is itself. Only against that forgetting can a choice be genuine. A being that knew it was one with all could not truly choose to serve or to seize; the answer would be given, not earned.

The veil makes love possible by making love risky. Suffering, on this account, is not punishment but catalyst — the friction against which polarization happens.

Plate V ◆

Service to Others and Service to Self

The great choice of third density is a polarization, and the material is radical in insisting that both poles are legitimate. One may climb by service to others — the positive path, the way of love — or by service to self — the negative path, the way of separation, domination and control. Neither is forbidden. Both are coherent. Both are climbed.

The asymmetry is not moral but structural. The negative path, Ra says, is ultimately entropic: a being wholly turned inward eventually consumes everything it can dominate and is left alone with itself, which is a kind of death. The positive path compounds. The material never calls the negative path wrong — only, in the end, a dead end of its own logic.

Plate VI ◆

The Harvest

Third density runs in great cycles — a major cycle of roughly 75,000 years — at whose end comes the harvest or graduation. Each soul is weighed, not by deeds tallied but by its polarization: the degree to which it has oriented toward others or toward self.

The thresholds are the material's most quoted figures. To graduate to the positive fourth density a soul must be at least 51% service to others. To graduate to the negative fourth Density it must be at least 95% service to self — a far harder mark, which is one way the text encodes its claim that selfishness is the narrower door. Souls that reach neither repeat third density. There is no eternal hell here; there is only more time, and another choice.

Plate VII ◆

Wanderers and the Social Memory Complex

Two ideas do a great deal of work in the material. A wanderer is a being from a higher density — fourth, fifth, sixth — who voluntarily incarnates into the heaviness and the veil of third density in order to serve, risking a kind of amnesia and, Ra says, real danger of getting caught in the karma it came to lighten. Wanderers are the material's saints and bodhisattvas.

A social memory complex is what a group becomes at the upper densities: not a hive that erases the individual, but a communion in which every member's experience is available to all and the group mind is richer than any part. Ra is one. It is the material's picture of heaven — not a place but a way of being together.

Plate VIII ◆

The Confederation and the Orion Group

The cosmos is populated by intelligences, grouped by their polarization. The Confederation of Planets in the Service of the Infinite Creator — some fifty-three civilizations, of which Ra is one — serves. The Orion group seeks to conquer and enslave. Both are bound by the first distortion: neither may violate free will. So the Confederation answers only when called and gives no proof that would compel belief, while Orion works by temptation and the exploitation of those already turned toward self.

This is the material's theodicy: the "negative" is permitted, even useful, as the resistance that makes the positive choice mean something. It is also why Ra will not simply prove itself — proof would collapse the veil and void the choice.

Other traditions would fill the same grid with their own answers — Judaism's covenantal monotheism, Sikhism's formless One, Taoism's Tao, the layered worlds of the indigenous cosmologies — and the pattern would hold: the Law of One is a monism like Vedanta, a reincarnationism like both Dharmic faiths, a moral dualism of service unusual in all of them, and a revelation-by-contact like none of the classical canons.

Reading the Chart

What the Comparison Actually Shows

It is a monism with a moral spine. On ultimate reality the Law of One sits closest to Advaita Vedanta — the non-dualism that says the self and the ground of being are one. But where Shankara's non-dualism dissolves the many into the One as illusion, Ra keeps the many as a real school, and bolts onto the monism a sharp ethical dualism — serve or seize — that Vedanta does not require.

It is a reincarnationism with a deadline. Like Hinduism and Buddhism it teaches rebirth and liberation. Unlike them it adds a quantitative threshold and a cosmic clock: a percentage, a harvest, a graduation. The wheel has an exit ramp with a posted speed limit. That is a modern, almost bureaucratic turn, and it is worth noticing as such.

Its dualism is the strange part. No major tradition so evenly blesses two opposed paths. Christianity, Islam, the Dharmic faiths all treat the selfish path as a failure, a fall, a fetter. Ra treats it as a legitimate, coherent, if ultimately self-defeating, way up. That single move — refusing to call the negative path wrong, only narrow — is the most distinctive thing in the material, and the one most worth arguing with.

And its authority is unlike all of them. Every classical canon grounds itself in history, prophecy, or a founder's awakening. The Law of One grounds itself in a contact — a voice, tranced, in a room, on tape. That is its vulnerability and, oddly, its honesty: it shows its work, publishes its doubts, and tells you to disbelieve it where it does not ring true.

What Survives Scrutiny

  • ◆ The material exists, is documented, and its process is unusually transparent — tapes, transcripts, published doubts.
  • ◆ It is internally coherent across five books and some two thousand questions — rare for a channeled text.
  • ◆ It demonstrably seeded modern New Age vocabulary: the densities, wanderers, service-to-others, the harvest.
  • ◆ Its ethics — non-interference, service to others, the refusal to compel belief — are philosophically serious and worth engaging on their merits.
  • ◆ Read as a modern theodicy, the "evil as permitted catalyst" move is coherent and has real antecedents in Irenaeus and in process thought.
  • ◆ As a primary document of the twentieth-century contact imagination, it is genuinely valuable — a window onto what the century longed for.

What Does Not

  • ◆ That Ra is a sixth-density intelligence. Untestable, and the trance channel that produced it is fully explicable as a dissociative or imaginative state of a gifted instrument.
  • ◆ The densities, the harvest, the 75,000-year cycles. No independent evidence; the numbers are asserted, not derived, and no prediction has been confirmed.
  • ◆ The Atlantis and Akhenaten contacts. Atlantis is not an archaeological reality, and the Aten cult is thoroughly documented without any need of extraterrestrial advice.
  • ◆ Wanderers among us. A claim that cannot be distinguished from the ordinary fact that some people are unusually kind or unusually cruel.
  • ◆ That it is unique. Its cosmology is a synthesis — Neoplatonist emanation, Theosophical root-races and rounds, Vedantic non-dualism, and the channeled idiom of its own decade. Knowing the sources makes it richer, not poorer.

As ever, discarding the unsupportable strengthens the serious residue. The material does not need Ra to be real in order to be worth reading; it needs only to be thought through, and it is.

Lineage

A Record of the Material

  • 1950s–60s — Don Elkins, a physicist, spends two decades investigating UFO contactees, hypnotic regression and the paranormal, growing steadily more sceptical of the sensational and more interested in the consistent.
  • 1970s — Elkins and Carla Rueckert develop the trance method; early "personality" contacts give way to something the pair take more seriously.
  • 1981 — The Ra sessions begin in earnest. The intelligence gives its name and its cosmology; Jim McCarty joins as scribe.
  • 1982 — The Law of One, Book I is published.
  • 1984 — Books II and III appear; Don Elkins dies, and the first arc of the material closes with him.
  • 1986–1998 — A second, longer series of sittings produces Books IV and V; L/L Research continues to publish and to distribute the transcripts freely.
  • 1990s–present — The vocabulary escapes into the wider culture: "wanderer," "density," "service to others." The material becomes a reference point — argued with, borrowed from, and rarely read in full.

Glossary

Intelligent infinity
The undifferentiated, infinite, conscious source; the One before it bends into a universe.
Distortion
A "bending" of the One into a particular thing. Free will is the first distortion; everything real is one.
Density
An octave of being, from mineral (first) to the gateway (seventh), climbed by mastering each one's lesson.
The veil
Third density's forgetting, drawn so that the choice to serve or to seize can be genuine.
Polarization
The degree to which a soul has turned toward others or toward self; the measure by which it is harvested.
Harvest
The graduation at a cycle's end: 51% service-to-others for the positive fourth density, 95% service-to-self for the negative.
Wanderer
A higher-density soul incarnated into third density to serve, at the risk of the veil and of karma.
Social memory complex
A group mind in which every member's experience is shared; what Ra is, and the material's picture of heaven.

Reading

  • ◆ The Law of One, Books I–V (L/L Research, 1982–1998) — the primary material, distributed free by the circle that took it down.
  • ◆ Carla L. Rueckert, What Love Is and her other writings — the instrument's own, untranced voice, and a useful corrective to reading Ra as scripture.
  • ◆ Hans Baer & colleagues, on the New Age milieu — for placing the material in the twentieth-century esoteric revival it both drew on and fed.
  • ◆ The Corpus Hermeticum and Plotinus — the emanation cosmology the densities quietly rhyme with.
  • ◆ Shankara's Vivekachudamani — non-dualism proper, against which the Law of One's monism can be measured.
  • ◆ Jane Roberts, the Seth Material and A Course in Miracles — the material's closest kin in the channeled idiom of its decade.

The Closing Stance

Where This Order Stands

We do not believe Ra is waiting on the other side of a trance in Kentucky. We are not convinced by the densities, the harvest, or the arithmetic of salvation, and this page has said so in the ledger without flinching.

And yet we give the material a room of its own, because it does something few revelations manage: it thinks. It takes one axiom — that all is one — and follows it, honestly and without special pleading, into cosmology, ethics, suffering and death. It refuses to compel belief. It blesses the freedom of the being who would seize as readily as the being who would serve, and then shows, without rage, why seizing is a corridor with no door at the end. It tells the reader, again and again, to keep only what rings true.

A text that confident in its own thinking has nothing to fear from ours. Read it as the modern esoteric document it is: shelved ◆, deliberately and without embarrassment, beside the Hermetica and the Upanishads, and worth every hour spent arguing with it.
