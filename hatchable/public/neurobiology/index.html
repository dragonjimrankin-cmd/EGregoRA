Limb V

Neurobiology

Three pounds of electrified fat that models a universe. Everything this order studies — the vision, the grove, the star, the god — arrives through it, which makes the organ the one instrument we cannot afford to be romantic about.

Begin Here

The Instrument Problem

An astronomer who refused to learn the optics of their telescope would be laughed out of the observatory. Yet most people pursuing the inner life never learn the first thing about the apparatus producing it.

Every experience this order takes seriously — the sense of presence in a wood at dusk, the geometry behind closed eyes, the conviction of contact, the dissolution of self at the peak of a meditation — is delivered by a biological system with known tissue, known chemistry and known failure modes. That is not a debunking. It is the opposite: it is the only route to telling a genuine signal from an artefact of the receiver.

So the rule for this limb is severe. Describing the mechanism of an experience says nothing whatsoever about whether its content is true. Finding the serotonin receptor involved in a vision no more disproves the vision than finding the rods and cones disproves the Moon. But it does mean that "it felt utterly real" is a statement about the apparatus, not about reality — and the apparatus is demonstrably capable of manufacturing total conviction out of nothing at all.

Grading throughout: ◆ established science · ◆ scholarship and history · ◆ speculative and contested · ◆ myth, tradition and primary esoteric text.

Seven Plates

The Machinery, Plate by Plate

From one cell to the whole predictive apparatus, and then out to the edge where the evidence thins.

Plate I ◆

The Cell That Decides

A neuron holds roughly −70 millivolts across its membrane, maintained by pumps burning a fifth of your body's entire energy budget. Inputs arrive at thousands of synapses; if the summed voltage crosses about −55 mV the cell fires an all-or-nothing spike, propagating at up to 120 metres per second.

Roughly 86 billion of them, with an equivalent number of glia doing work we badly underestimated for a century. The spike itself carries no meaning. Meaning lives in which cells fire, when, and what the pattern has been wired to predict.

Hodgkin & Huxley worked out the ionic mechanism on a squid giant axon and took the 1963 Nobel for it. Azevedo et al. (2009) replaced the folkloric "100 billion" with an actual count.

Plate II ◆

Plasticity, or Why Practice Works

Synapses that are repeatedly active together strengthen; those that are not, weaken and are pruned. Long-term potentiation is measurable in a slice of hippocampus within minutes, and involves physical trafficking of receptors into the membrane.

This is the honest biological core of every contemplative tradition's insistence on daily practice. You are not accumulating merit. You are rewiring a physical organ by repetition, in the same way and by the same mechanism as learning an instrument — and like an instrument, it degrades when abandoned.

Note what this does not license: "neuroplasticity" is not a permission slip for any claim you like. Adult brains change in specific, bounded, slow ways.

Plate III ◆

Rhythm and the Binding of a Moment

Populations of neurons oscillate together — delta through theta, alpha, beta and gamma. Alpha (≈8–12 Hz) rises when the eyes close; theta (≈4–8 Hz) dominates hippocampal navigation and deep absorption; gamma (≈30–100 Hz) accompanies tightly bound perception.

Rhythm appears to be how a brain decides what belongs with what: cells firing in phase are treated as reporting on the same thing. It is also the mechanism by which drumming, chant and flicker genuinely alter state — a periodic drive can pull an oscillator towards its own tempo.

The entrainment effect is real and well documented for photic drive. The popular claim that specific frequencies unlock specific spiritual states is ◆ not.

Plate IV ◆

The Brain as a Prediction Engine

The dominant modern account inverts the naive picture. The brain is not primarily receiving the world; it is generating a model of the world and sending it downwards, while the senses return only the error — the part the model got wrong. Perception is a controlled hallucination that the data keeps correcting.

It explains an enormous amount: why expectation changes what you see, why pain is modulated by belief, why a face appears in bark, why the first night in a strange house is full of intruders. The prior is doing most of the work, always, in everyone.

Helmholtz's 1867 "unconscious inference", formalised by Friston, Clark and Hohwy. Dominant and productive — but a framework, not a settled fact.

Plate V ◆

The Default Mode Network

When a person stops doing a task, a specific set of midline structures — medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate, precuneus, angular gyrus — becomes more active. This network handles autobiography, mental time travel, modelling other minds, and the running narrative of being a self.

It is the closest thing neuroscience has to a physical correlate of the ego in the technical sense: not arrogance, but the continuous construction of a protagonist. Its activity is reduced in experienced meditators, and strongly disrupted by classic psychedelics.

Raichle (2001) named it. Brewer et al. (2011) found the meditation effect; Carhart-Harris et al. (2012) the psilocybin one.

Plate VI ◆

The 5-HT2A Key

Psilocybin, LSD, mescaline and DMT differ chemically but converge on one action: agonism at the serotonin 5-HT2A receptor, densely expressed on layer-5 pyramidal cells of the cortex. Block that receptor with ketanserin and the experience simply does not occur.

Downstream, the cortex becomes less hierarchically constrained. Regions that normally do not talk begin to; the prior loosens its grip on the error signal. On the predictive account above, that is precisely what a vision is — a model running with the brakes off.

The clinical findings for depression and end-of-life distress are genuinely promising and genuinely preliminary: small samples, near-impossible blinding, enormous expectancy effects.

Nothing on this page is medical advice. These are powerful, legally controlled compounds with real psychiatric risk, particularly where psychosis runs in a family.

Plate VII ◆

Form Constants: Why the Geometry

People across every culture and every method — peyote, migraine, flicker, pressure on the eyeball, sensory deprivation, near-death — report the same four classes of figure: lattices and honeycombs, cobwebs, tunnels and funnels, spirals.

There is a strong explanation. The retina maps onto primary visual cortex in an approximately logarithmic-polar fashion. Under that mapping, simple stripes of spontaneous activity rolling across flat cortex are seen as concentric rings, radiating spokes and logarithmic spirals. The geometry of the vision is the geometry of the cortical sheet, read through a known distortion.

Klüver catalogued the constants in 1926; Ermentrout & Cowan modelled them in 1979; Bressloff et al. (2001) tied them to cortical symmetry. See Limb III — this is where the two limbs meet, and it is the most honest thing we can say about why sacred art looks the way it does.

✦

What Survives Scrutiny

  • ◆ Meditation measurably alters attention networks and reduces default mode activity in long-term practitioners.
  • ◆ Classic psychedelics act at 5-HT2A and reliably disrupt hierarchical cortical processing.
  • ◆ Form constants are explained by the retinocortical map — a real bridge from vision to geometry.
  • ◆ Photic and auditory rhythm can entrain cortical oscillations.
  • ◆ The gut hosts ~500 million neurons and its microbiome influences mood and behaviour in animal models.
  • ◆ Experience physically rewires tissue: plasticity is structural, not metaphorical.
  • ◆ Sleep clears metabolic waste from neural tissue; chronic deprivation damages cognition severely.
  • ◆ Psychedelic-assisted therapy shows real promise in small trials for treatment-resistant depression.
  • ◆ Interoception — the sense of the body's internal state — is a major input to emotion, not a sideshow.

What Does Not

  • ◆ "You use 10% of your brain." Fabricated. Imaging shows essentially all of it active across a day; damage anywhere costs you something.
  • ◆ Left-brain / right-brain personalities. Lateralisation of specific functions is real; "creative" versus "logical" people is not.
  • ◆ The pineal gland as the third eye secreting DMT. It makes melatonin. Trace DMT has been found in rodent pineal tissue at concentrations nowhere near psychoactive.
  • ◆ Binaural beats as a state technology. Pleasant, mildly relaxing, repeatedly fails to deliver the advertised specific states.
  • ◆ Quantum consciousness in microtubules. Not impossible, but the decoherence objection at body temperature remains unanswered after thirty years.
  • ◆ "Rewire your brain in 21 days." Durable structural change takes months of consistent practice, and the number is marketing.
  • ◆ Learning styles. Tested repeatedly, never found. Teach to the material, not the self-report.

Each of these is believed sincerely by people we like. That is exactly why they are listed.

The Open Wound

Nobody Knows Why Any of It Is Lit

Everything above describes function. None of it explains experience.

We can say in detail how light at 700 nanometres is transduced, relayed, and categorised. We cannot say why there is redness — why the processing is accompanied by anything at all rather than running in the dark, as it manifestly does for the parts of your nervous system managing blood pH. Chalmers called this the hard problem in 1995, and it has not moved.

The live positions, graded honestly:

Physicalism ◆
Consciousness is what certain information processing is; the puzzle is a confusion that will dissolve. Majority view; no demonstration yet.
Global Workspace / Higher-Order theories ◆
Experience arises when information is broadcast widely enough to be available to many systems. Testable, productive, contested.
Integrated Information Theory ◆
Experience is identical to integrated information (Φ). Mathematically bold, accused by critics of being unfalsifiable and of granting experience to grids of logic gates.
Panpsychism ◆
Experience is fundamental, like charge. Avoids the emergence miracle, inherits the combination problem: how do atoms of experience make one unified mind?
Idealism and the Law of One ◆
Consciousness is primary and matter is its appearance. A coherent and ancient position, and the one this order's seventh limb engages with — held here as a serious philosophical and esoteric claim, not an empirical result.

EGregoRA takes no house position. Anyone who tells you this is solved is selling something, including when they are selling materialism.

Application

What To Actually Do With This

Four practices with a defensible mechanism behind them, and nothing to buy.

Lengthen the Out-Breath

Exhaling longer than you inhale raises vagal tone and shifts autonomic balance towards parasympathetic within a minute or two. Four in, eight out, five times. The most reliably evidenced intervention on this entire page, and it is free.

Go Outside Without a Destination

Natural settings restore directed attention and lower measured stress markers; the effect is modest, replicated, and multiplied by removing the phone. Limb II has the druidic frame; this is the mechanism underneath it.

Defend Your Sleep First

Before any practice, any supplement, any technique. Chronic short sleep degrades emotional regulation, memory consolidation and judgement more thoroughly than almost anything you could add. Visions on four hours a night are a symptom.

Watch the Prior, Not the Percept

Given Plate IV, the useful contemplative move is not to interrogate what you saw but to notice what you were expecting. Most of the content of a strong experience is your own model handed back to you wearing a costume.

Keep a Dated Record

Write the experience down the same hour, before the mind tidies it into a better story. Memory is reconstructive — each recall rewrites the trace. A contemporaneous note is the only honest evidence you will ever have about your own inner life.

Know the Contraindications

A family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder, certain cardiac conditions and several medication classes — SSRIs, MAOIs, lithium — change the risk picture of intense practice and of psychedelics completely. This is a matter for a clinician, not an order.

Limb V · vii — with Limb VII

Hallucinogens and the Interdimensional Draw

Every tradition that uses these compounds says the same thing in different words: the material does not invent what arrives, it opens a channel to something already there. This order takes that claim seriously enough to examine it properly, which means neither dismissing it nor swallowing it.

The honest position is a fork, and we will not pretend it has been resolved. Under psilocybin, LSD, mescaline, ayahuasca or DMT, people reliably report contact with sources that present themselves as other than the self — autonomous, intelligent, often unsurprised to see you, frequently conveying information the visitor insists they did not already possess. That phenomenology is ◆ established: it is measurable, replicable, dose-dependent and startlingly consistent across people who have never met. What is ◆ contested is the ontology — whether the channel reaches somewhere, or whether a nervous system under altered constraint generates company for itself.

We call the practice the draw, in both senses the word carries: to draw upon a source, and to draw from life what you were shown. The order's working stance is that the first sense is unproven and the second is not — whatever the origin, the material is real enough to be recorded, cross-referenced and tested, and most practitioners waste it by failing to write anything down.

What the Draw Reliably Yields

  • ◆ Mystical-type experience, on demand. Griffiths' 2006 psilocybin study produced complete mystical experiences in a majority of participants, and two-thirds still rated the session among the five most personally meaningful events of their lives fourteen months later.
  • ◆ Durable trait change. Measured increases in openness persisting over a year — one of very few interventions ever shown to shift adult personality.
  • ◆ Genuine therapeutic signal in depression, end-of-life distress and alcohol use disorder, in small trials with notoriously weak blinding. Promising; not yet settled.
  • ◆ Entity encounter as a stable phenomenon. Davis and Griffiths' survey of over 2,500 DMT entity encounters found most respondents rated the being as conscious, benevolent and real — and over half of those who had identified as atheist beforehand no longer did.
  • ◆ A deep cultural lineage. Eleusis for two millennia, Amazonian ayahuasca, Wixárika peyote, Mazatec mushroom velada, Siberian Amanita. This is not a 1960s invention.
  • ◆ Art that could not otherwise be made. The visionary tradition from Huichol yarn paintings to Alex Grey. Whatever the source, the draw demonstrably produces work.

What It Does Not

  • ◆ Verifiable information from elsewhere. Never, under any protocol, despite the obvious incentive to demonstrate it.
  • ◆ The pineal DMT story. The gland produces melatonin; trace DMT has been detected in rodent brain tissue, at concentrations nowhere near psychoactive. The endogenous-DMT-at-death claim is an untested hypothesis repeated as fact.
  • ◆ Access to a shared location. Two people dosed together do not meet in the same place and report the same contents. This has been looked at.
  • ◆ Wisdom. Insight under the compound feels certain regardless of quality — the noetic sense is part of the pharmacology. Write it down and judge it sober on Thursday.
  • ◆ A shortcut past practice. Every tradition that uses these plants embeds them in years of discipline, diet, song and obligation. The compound is the smallest part of the apparatus.
  • ◆ Safety for everyone. A personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder is a hard contraindication, not a caution.

Said Plainly

The Order's Position, and the Warnings That Go With It

EGregoRA holds that hallucinogenic meditation is a legitimate instrument of enquiry — and that an instrument is only as good as the discipline around it. Nothing here is medical advice, nothing here is an encouragement to break the law of the country you are in, and the compounds discussed are controlled in most jurisdictions including the United Kingdom.

  • Contraindications are absolute. Personal or family history of psychosis, schizophrenia or bipolar disorder. Lithium with a classical psychedelic has caused seizures. Ayahuasca contains MAO inhibitors that interact dangerously with SSRIs and with a long list of foods and drugs. Check, properly, every time.
  • The set and the setting are the method, not a nicety. REBUS says precisely why: in a state of flattened priors you are extraordinarily suggestible, and whoever is in the room is, for that period, writing on you.
  • Guides without accountability are a known hazard. Abuse in psychedelic settings is documented and recurrent, and the same structural warning applies as in the Black Tribunal, Limb VIII: secrecy plus hierarchy plus a charismatic figure is the shape that goes wrong.
  • Integration is the work. The session is the easy part. Write the account within an hour, in full, including what you would rather omit; reread it at a week and at three months; keep only what survives both readings.
  • If it goes badly — persistent fear, sleeplessness, the sense that the ordinary world has not re-sealed — that is a clinician's matter, immediately. In the UK: Samaritans, 116 123, day or night, free.

The draw is permitted here. Credulity is not. If something you met told you something true, it will still be true on Thursday, and you can check.

Lineage

How We Learned This

  • 1791 Galvani makes a dead frog's leg twitch and establishes that nerves run on electricity.
  • 1848 A tamping iron passes through Phineas Gage's frontal lobe; he survives, and personality is shown to be tissue.
  • 1873 Golgi's silver stain renders single cells visible; Cajal uses it to prove the brain is discrete neurons, not a continuous web.
  • 1924 Berger records the first human EEG and names the alpha rhythm.
  • 1926 Klüver catalogues the four form constants in mescaline intoxication.
  • 1943 Hofmann's accidental ingestion of LSD-25 opens the serotonergic era.
  • 1953 Aserinsky and Kleitman discover REM sleep; dreaming becomes measurable.
  • 1957 Patient H.M. loses his hippocampi and reveals that memory is plural and localised.
  • 1963 Hodgkin and Huxley take the Nobel for the ionic basis of the action potential.
  • 1971 O'Keefe finds place cells — an actual map of space, made of neurons.
  • 1990 Ogawa demonstrates the BOLD signal; fMRI makes the living brain watchable.
  • 1995 Chalmers formulates the hard problem of consciousness.
  • 2001 Raichle describes the default mode network.
  • 2012 Carhart-Harris shows psilocybin decreasing brain activity, overturning the expectation of a stimulated brain.
  • 2021 AlphaFold-era structural biology begins resolving receptor conformations that were guesswork a decade earlier.

Glossary

Action potential
The all-or-nothing electrical spike a neuron fires when its membrane voltage crosses threshold.
Synaptic plasticity
Lasting change in the strength of a connection between two neurons; the substrate of learning.
Entrainment
The pulling of an oscillator's rhythm towards an external periodic drive — drum, flicker, chant.
Predictive processing
The account in which the brain generates a model and the senses supply only correction to it.
Default mode network
Midline structures active at rest, handling self-narrative, memory and mental time travel.
5-HT2A
The serotonin receptor subtype through which every classic psychedelic acts.
Form constant
One of the four universal geometric hallucination classes: lattice, cobweb, tunnel, spiral.
Interoception
Perception of the body's internal state — heartbeat, gut, breath — and a major driver of emotion.
Qualia
The felt character of experience: the redness of red. What the hard problem is about.
Neurogenesis
Birth of new neurons. Abundant in development; in the adult human hippocampus, genuinely disputed.

Reading

  • ◆ Anil Seth, Being You (2021) — the predictive-processing account of self, written for the general reader and not over-sold.
  • ◆ Matthew Walker, Why We Sleep (2017) — powerful, occasionally over-stated; read alongside its published critiques.
  • ◆ Antonio Damasio, The Feeling of What Happens (1999) — body, emotion and the making of a self.
  • ◆ Bressloff, Cowan, Golubitsky, Thomas & Wiener (2001), "Geometric visual hallucinations…" — the technical source behind Plate VII.
  • ◆ Oliver Sacks, Hallucinations (2012) — the clinical phenomenology, told with the dignity of the patients intact.
  • ◆ Michael Pollan, How to Change Your Mind (2018) — history and reporting, not pharmacology.
  • ◆ Christof Koch, The Feeling of Life Itself (2019) — the case for Integrated Information Theory, from its leading advocate.
  • ◆ David Chalmers, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness" (1995) — short, clear, still unanswered.
  • ◆ William James, The Varieties of Religious Experience (1902) — a psychologist taking mystical states seriously, a century before it was respectable.

A Closing Caution

This limb is the one most often used as a weapon, in both directions. Materialists wield it to tell people their deepest experiences were "just" chemistry; seekers wield a garbled version of it to dress wishes in the vocabulary of science. Both are doing the same disreputable thing — borrowing the authority of neuroscience to end an argument early.

Hold the line instead. The mechanism is astonishing and worth learning properly. The experience is real as experience, always, whatever produced it. And the question of what any of it means is not a neuroscientific question at all, which is precisely why there are ten other limbs.

Nothing on this page is medical or psychiatric advice. If you are unwell, or considering any intense practice while on medication, speak to a clinician rather than to an order.
