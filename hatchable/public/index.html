The Charter

What an Egregore Is — and Why We Named Ourselves For One

An egregore is the old name for a thought-form grown large: the living spirit that forms when many minds attend to the same idea for long enough that it begins to attend back. A grove, a guild, a nation, a religion, a scientific paradigm — each is an egregore, fed by attention and starved by neglect. EGregoRA is one deliberately built in daylight, with its hands open: EG for its co-founder Edward Gregory, RA for the old solar name that keeps turning up wherever people go looking for the source.

We take the position that the division between physics and philosophy, between laboratory and grove, is an administrative convenience of the last four centuries, not a feature of the universe. The electron does not know it belongs to a department. Neither does the oak, the mycelium under it, the serotonin receptor, or the strange recursive feeling of being someone reading this sentence.

The word is not a boast. An egregore is an ordinary thing, and most of them are accidents — formed by people who never decided to form one and are steered by it anyway. A company's culture, a family's silence about a particular subject, a discipline's conviction that certain questions are beneath it. This order's only unusual feature is that it was built on purpose, with the mechanism written on the door.

What follows from that is a method rather than a doctrine. EGregoRA has no revealed text, no lineage to defend, nothing it needs you to believe before you are allowed in. It has a reading list, ten fields of enquiry, a grading system for evidence, and one instruction. Everything else is argument.

“Study the stars with a telescope, a calendar, a myth and a nervous system. Four instruments, one sky. Discard whichever of them lies to you — but find out first which one it was.” Edward Gregory, Co-Founder

HOW TO READ THIS SITE — THE GRADING SYSTEM

The Four Diamonds

Every substantive claim in this order carries a mark. The mark tells you what kind of thing the claim is and how much weight it will bear. It is the single most important convention on the site, and it exists because the material here ranges from textbook physics to primary esoteric experience, and mixing those two without labelling them is how orders become cults.

  • ◆ Established. Measured, replicated, and accepted across the relevant field. You could look it up in a textbook and find the same thing. If we have graded something green and you can show it is not, that is a correction we owe you and will make.
  • ◆ Scholarship, history and the documented record. What the manuscripts, excavations and dated sources actually support — distinct from what a tradition says about itself. A gold diamond means: this is the historical position, with the usual academic uncertainty, not a law of nature.
  • ◆ Speculative — our reading. A position the order holds, argued from evidence but not established by it. Everything original here lives in this column. It is offered as a reading, not a finding, and it may be wrong without the rest of the site falling over.
  • ◆ Myth, tradition and primary esoteric experience. Material whose value is symbolic, practical or personal rather than evidential. Past lives, the Ra material, correspondences, visionary accounts. Real as experience, undemonstrated as physics, and never moved leftward because we would like it to be.

You will also meet four narrower labels in the same style. ◆ Tested, negative — the claim has been examined under proper conditions and failed; we say so even when it is ours. ◆ Contested — competent people disagree and the evidence does not yet settle it. ◆ Craft guidance — practical instruction, offered because it works in the hand, with no metaphysical claim attached. ◆ Our stance — a value or a commitment rather than a finding, which no experiment could settle either way.

Two rules govern the whole scheme. First: nothing is promoted by enthusiasm. A claim moves towards green when evidence moves it, and never because it has become important to us. Second: the grading is a claim in itself, and therefore arguable. If you think something here is mislabelled, that is the most useful letter you could write to us.

The oracle at Ask Ed uses the same vocabulary in words rather than diamonds — when an answer there says Established, Contested, Tested, negative or Our stance, it means exactly what the corresponding diamond means on these pages.

On the Black Tribunal. EGregoRA studies the Nazi appropriation of myth, symbol and pseudo-science for one reason only: because the twentieth century proved that mystical language is flammable, and that people who never learn how it burns are the easiest to set alight. Nothing in this order romanticises fascism. We name it for what it was — an industrial atrocity dressed in stolen runes — and we teach the warning signs so that the symbols can be returned to the people they were taken from.

There is a personal reason this limb exists, and Ed asks that it be stated plainly rather than hinted at. Through sustained meditation and ritual recall he has walked a number of what he holds to be past lives, and among them he has identified one character who stood inside that regime. He does not offer this as evidence of anything, and he does not expect it to be believed; he offers it as the reason he refuses to study fascism at a comfortable distance. What this present life asks of him, as he understands it, is not a renunciation but an enforced integration: a compelled reckoning with the feelings, the choices and the paths taken by the beings he has been — those that healed and those that harmed, the positively and the negatively impacting alike — held together in one awareness rather than sorted into a side worth keeping and a side to be buried. Nothing is disowned, because what is disowned only goes underground and steers from there. The inherited predispositions — the pull toward order, hierarchy, certainty and the purifying idea — are met as they rise, named for what they are and where they came from, and answered consciously, again and again, by choice.

Whether one reads this as literal reincarnation, as the psyche's way of giving a shape to inherited shadow, or as myth doing the work myth does, the ethical content is identical and is not negotiable: the atrocity was real, the victims were real, and nothing recovered in trance excuses or softens a single hour of it. Recall is not a credential, and it confers no authority here. Past lives are filed in this order where they belong — ◆ myth, tradition and primary esoteric experience — never among the demonstrated.

The Co-Founders

Edward Gregory & Jim Rankin

Edward is a natural philosopher in the old, unembarrassed sense of the phrase: someone who refuses to choose between the telescope and the tree. He co-founded EGregoRA to build the thing he could never find — a place where cosmology, ecology, neuroscience and the esoteric traditions could be argued about in the same room, by the same person, without anyone having to pretend.

Jim Rankin is co-founder of the order — Ed's understudy, and on the better days his muse. Where Ed presses a question until it yields, Jim is the one who asks why that question and not another; a good deal of what EGregoRA now calls its own began as something Jim said sideways and Ed refused to let go of. The two halves of the work, the pursuit and the provocation.

Their standing instruction to every member is three words long: test everything kindly.

The Standing Instruction

Test everything kindly.

Three words, and both halves carry weight. Test, because a belief you have never pressed on is furniture, not knowledge. Kindly, because the person whose idea you are pressing on is usually yourself, and nobody has ever thought their way clear while being sneered at — least of all by their own interior voice.
