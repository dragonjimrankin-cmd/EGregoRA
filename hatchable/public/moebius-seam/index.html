Limb I · iii — a sub-limb of Cosmic Physics

The Moebius Seam

The order's own doctrine, written out at full length for the first time — and then held against the evidence without mercy, because a doctrine that cannot survive that treatment is not worth keeping.

Begin Here — and read this paragraph before any other

What This Page Is, and Is Not

This page sets out a claim the order makes and cannot prove. It is filed as myth and speculation throughout, and nothing on it should be cited as physics or as mathematics.

Three things are braided together here: a shape, a conjecture, and a proposal about gravity. The shape is the Moebius Seam, the one-sided band on which the atlas strings the eleven limbs and on which the Ladder of Scale closes. The conjecture is the Riemann Hypothesis, the deepest unsolved problem in mathematics. The proposal is that gravity is the accumulated weight of the memory of baryonic matter — that mass pulls because the universe keeps an account of what has happened to it.

The order asserts that these three are one statement seen from three sides. That assertion is ◆ myth, tradition and primary esoteric text. It is not a theorem, it makes no numerical prediction anyone has tested, and the section near the foot of this page sets out the five specific ways it is most likely to be wrong. Both halves are the work. Anyone who quotes the doctrine without the objections has misread the order badly.

Grading throughout: ◆ established science · ◆ scholarship and history · ◆ speculative and contested · ◆ myth, tradition and primary esoteric text.

First of Three

The Seam Itself

A strip given half a turn before its ends are joined. The twist costs it a side, and everything the order builds on it follows from that one loss.

The Shape of the Join ◆

No Back of the House

The order's reading is the one it has always given: every limb is strung on one seam, so there is no front of this house and no back of it. Physics does not sit on the far side of magic. Neuroscience is not the underside of prayer. Follow any limb far enough and you arrive at the others, facing the other way.

That is a statement about how this order files its subjects, and it has earned its keep as one. It is not a statement about spacetime, and the moment it is mistaken for one the whole structure starts lying.

The Flow ◆

Causality, and the Ledger

Because the seam has no second side there is nowhere for a cause to be hidden. What is done enters the surface and comes round. The order calls that returning the cosmic ledger: the account kept between the old and the new, in which nothing is written off and nothing is paid twice, only carried round the twist and met again changed.

There is one honest piece of physics standing near this, and only one: in quantum mechanics information is conserved, because evolution is unitary, and the no-hiding theorem of Braunstein and Pati (2007) sharpens that to a statement about where lost information must be. Conserved is not the same as recoverable, and neither is the same as a ledger. ◆ the conservation. ◆ the ledger.

Second of Three

The Riemann Hypothesis, Stated Properly

Before the order says anything about what the Hypothesis means, here is what it actually is. Everything in this section is standard mathematics and none of it is in dispute.

Why It Matters ◆

It Is a Statement About the Primes

The zeros of zeta are not a curiosity attached to the primes; by Riemann's explicit formula they are the primes, written in another alphabet. The prime counting function is the smooth logarithmic estimate plus a sum of waves, and each wave is one zero. Where the zeros sit controls how far the primes may stray from their average.

If the Hypothesis holds, the error in the prime count is bounded by roughly the square root of x times a logarithm — the smallest it could possibly be, meaning the primes are as regularly distributed as a random-looking sequence is allowed to be. If it fails, somewhere out there the primes bunch or thin more than anyone expects. Thousands of published theorems begin "assume RH".

The State of Play ◆

166 Years, No Proof

  • 1896 — Hadamard and de la Vallée Poussin prove no zeros lie on the line Re(s) = 1, which gives the Prime Number Theorem.
  • 1914 — Hardy proves infinitely many zeros lie on the critical line.
  • 1942 — Selberg: a positive proportion of them do. Conrey raised it to over two fifths in 1989.
  • 1973 — Montgomery's pair correlation, and Dyson recognising it over tea as the statistics of eigenvalues of random Hermitian matrices. The zeros repel one another exactly as energy levels do.
  • 2000 — one of the seven Clay Millennium Problems; a million dollars, still unclaimed.
  • 2018 — Atiyah announced a proof at Heidelberg. It was not accepted. He was 89 and had been one of the great geometers of the century, and the episode is a lesson in how this problem treats people.

The Bridge That Actually Exists ◆

Hilbert–Pólya, and Quantum Chaos

The one place where the Hypothesis genuinely touches physics is real, mainstream and still open. The Hilbert–Pólya conjecture proposes that the imaginary parts of the zeros are the eigenvalues of some self-adjoint operator — and a self-adjoint operator has real eigenvalues necessarily, which would put every zero on the line as a matter of course rather than coincidence.

Berry and Keating went further: the operator, if it exists, should be the quantisation of a classical system that is chaotic, has no time-reversal symmetry, and whose periodic orbits have lengths that are logarithms of primes. Their candidate Hamiltonian is xp, and nobody has yet made it rigorous. This is a live research programme, not a fringe one.

Note carefully what it does and does not say. It says the zeros may be a spectrum. It says nothing about gravity, mass, memory or a one-sided surface.

Equivalences ◆

What "Equivalent To" Means in Mathematics

The word has a precise meaning and the order is about to use it loosely, so the precise meaning goes first. Two statements are equivalent when each can be derived from the other. RH has many genuine equivalents, and they are startling:

  • Robin's criterion (1984): RH holds if and only if the sum of divisors of n stays below eγn log log n for every n above 5040.
  • Lagarias (2002): an entirely elementary version using harmonic numbers, stateable to a schoolchild.
  • The Mertens function bounded by the square root of x times anything that grows — implies RH, though the original Mertens conjecture itself is false, disproved in 1985.
  • The Nyman–Beurling criterion: RH is equivalent to a density statement in a function space.

Each of those is a theorem: a chain of deductions anyone can check. That is the standard the next section does not meet, and says so.

However, Jim Rankin’s Rankin Skeletal Splice is a conclusive numerical proof which adds a previously unthought-of vector to this, and claims a multitude of new ways the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent. ◆ It is one part of the Two Infinities Framework by James Rankin, which holds three limbs together: the Splice, the Moebius Seam set out on this page, and the Cosmic Ledger. The order aligns itself with this independent research project, which is currently in progress.

The Framework is soon to be published in full, and it is there that these ideas are detailed properly and the mathematics is rigorously tested rather than asserted. Links will be provided as they become available. Further questions on any of it can go directly to shakradragon@gmail.com.

Third of Three — the doctrine

The Seam and the Hypothesis as One Statement

What the order actually claims, in its own words, with the grade attached to every part of it.

The doctrine, stated

The Moebius Seam is the Riemann Hypothesis wearing a shape, and gravity is what the seam weighs. ◆ myth, tradition and primary esoteric text.

Set out at length, the order holds the following. The critical line is a seam: a single line in the complex plane along which every zero is required to lie, dividing a strip that has, on the Hypothesis, no genuine second side — because the functional equation already folds the plane onto itself about that line, sending s to 1 − s and carrying each zero to its partner. That folding is a half turn. A strip folded onto itself by a half turn and joined is a Möbius band, and the Hypothesis is the statement that the join is clean — that no zero stands off the seam, which would be a second side appearing where the order says there is none.

From that the order takes its second step. A spectrum lying on one line is a self-adjoint operator's spectrum — this is the Hilbert–Pólya instinct, and here the order is borrowing real mathematics. A self-adjoint operator is an observable. An observable whose periodic orbits have lengths that are logarithms of primes is a system that counts what has happened to it. The order calls that counting the ledger, and says the ledger is not a metaphor for the physics but the same object: reality keeping an account of itself along a one-sided surface, with no outside in which the account could be stored instead.

And the third step, which is the one that will get the order into the most trouble: gravity is the accumulated weight of that account. Where matter has been, the seam carries the record of it; where the record is dense, the surface is deformed; and what we call the curvature of spacetime is the shape of an accumulated memory of baryonic matter. Mass does not pull. Mass is remembered, and the remembering has weight.

Every sentence of the three paragraphs above is ◆ myth, tradition and primary esoteric text, except the functional equation, which is ◆ a theorem, and the Hilbert–Pólya instinct, which is ◆ a real and unproven research programme. The order holds its doctrine the way it asks everyone to hold theirs: as something to be tested, and dropped if it fails.

The Hard Part

Five Ways This Is Probably Wrong

Written by the order against the order. If the doctrine is to be held at all it has to be held alongside these, and a reader who takes only the previous section has been given half a page.

  1. "Equivalent" is being used wrongly, and knowingly. In mathematics an equivalence is a two-way derivation anyone can check line by line. Nothing here derives the Riemann Hypothesis from a statement about a surface, or the reverse. What the order has is an analogy between a reflection symmetry and a half twist — and the functional equation's involution s → 1 − s is a reflection, not a twist: it reverses orientation on the plane but it does not make the strip non-orientable. A mathematician would stop reading at that sentence, and would be right to. ◆

  2. Gravity couples to energy, not to baryons. This is the objection that does the most damage. In general relativity the source of curvature is the full stress-energy tensor: energy, momentum, pressure and stress, from every field there is. Light has no baryons and bends space; a box of photons weighs more than an empty one; binding energy and pressure gravitate. Any theory in which only baryonic matter is remembered predicts a different universe from the one we measure, starting with the deflection of light at the Sun's limb — measured since 1919 and now to parts in a hundred thousand. ◆

  3. Most of the gravitating mass is not baryonic. Rotation curves, lensing, the acoustic peaks in the microwave background and the Bullet Cluster all say that roughly five sixths of the matter is not made of protons and neutrons. Big Bang nucleosynthesis fixes the baryon density independently and tightly, and it is far too small. A memory of baryonic matter would have to account for the part of gravity that baryons demonstrably do not cause. ◆

  4. The equivalence principle is a problem for any "memory" theory. Gravity is observed to depend only on present stress-energy and on nothing about an object's history — two objects of the same mass fall identically regardless of what they were before. Eötvös-type experiments, and MICROSCOPE in 2022, confirm the universality of free fall to about one part in 1015. A history-dependent gravity has to explain why no history has ever shown up in a measurement. ◆

  5. It predicts nothing. This is the one the order minds most. A doctrine that cannot say "if I am right, this measurement comes out differently" is not yet an idea about the world; it is a way of talking about it. Until the Seam produces a number, it is filed as myth and will stay there. ◆

And one straightforward falsity to kill outright. It is sometimes said that a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis would "break encryption". It would not. RSA rests on the difficulty of factoring, not on the distribution of primes; RH would sharpen our estimates of how the primes are spread and would hand nobody a factoring algorithm. If you see that claim attached to this doctrine, it did not come from here. ◆

Honest Company

The Serious Versions of This Idea

"Gravity is not fundamental but accounting" is not, in itself, a fringe thought. Several professional versions exist. None of them is the order's, and the differences are the instructive part.

1967 ◆

Sakharov's Induced Gravity

Gravity as an emergent elastic response of the vacuum: not a fundamental force at all, but the leading term you get when you integrate out quantum fields on a curved background. The idea is serious, has never been made to work completely, and remains a favourite of people who expect spacetime to be derived rather than assumed.

1995 and after ◆

Jacobson, Thermodynamics, Holography

Jacobson showed in 1995 that the Einstein field equations can be derived as an equation of state, from the thermodynamics of horizons. Bekenstein and Hawking had already found that a black hole's entropy is its horizon area in Planck units — an area, not a volume. The holographic principle generalises that: the information in a region is bounded by its boundary. Here, genuinely, gravity looks like bookkeeping.

2011 ◆

Verlinde's Entropic Gravity

The closest professional cousin to what the order says: gravity as an entropic force arising from changes in the information associated with the positions of matter, with a 2016 extension attempting to explain galaxy rotation without dark matter. It made a genuine lensing prediction, which did reasonably well in one test and poorly in others, and it has serious critics — including the objection that an entropic force would destroy quantum coherence in ways neutron interferometry does not see.

1883, and still unsettled ◆

Mach's Principle

That local inertia is determined by the distribution of all the matter in the universe — that a spinning bucket knows it is spinning because of the fixed stars. Einstein named it and was inspired by it; general relativity turns out to implement it only partially, and frame dragging, confirmed by Gravity Probe B in 2011, is the measurable residue. This is the nearest thing in real physics to "mass is remembered", and it is an open question rather than a doctrine.

What the order takes from the company it keeps. Three of these four are attempts to make gravity emergent from information, and all three are respectable. The order's version differs in exactly one respect, and it is the fatal one: they derive, and it asserts. Sakharov, Jacobson and Verlinde each produce the Einstein equations or something close to them out of their starting point. The Seam does not. Until it does, the honest statement is that the order's doctrine is an emblem standing where a derivation ought to be. ◆

The Ledger

What Holds Up, and What Does Not

Survives

  • ◆ The Möbius band is one-sided and non-orientable. Listing and Möbius, 1858. Paper and tape.
  • ◆ The functional equation folds the critical strip about Re(s) = ½. A theorem of Riemann's, 1859.
  • ◆ Zeros as a spectrum is a real research programme. Hilbert–Pólya, Montgomery–Dyson, Berry–Keating. Unproven and respectable.
  • ◆ Gravity can be written as thermodynamics. Bekenstein, Hawking, Jacobson. This part of the intuition is sound.
  • ◆ Information is conserved. Unitarity, and the no-hiding theorem.

Does Not Survive

  • ◆ "The Seam is equivalent to RH." No derivation exists in either direction. The word is being used as poetry.
  • ◆ "Gravity is the memory of baryonic matter." Gravity couples to all stress-energy, most gravitating matter is not baryonic, and free fall is history-independent to one part in 1015.
  • ◆ "A half twist and a reflection are the same operation." They are not; one makes a surface non-orientable and the other does not.
  • ◆ "Proving RH would break encryption." Flatly untrue.
  • ◆ Any numerical prediction from the doctrine. There is none. That is the whole of the case against it.

The Words

Non-orientable
A surface on which there is no consistent global choice of "up". Carry a shape all the way round a Möbius band and it returns mirrored.
Zeta function
ζ(s), the sum of 1/ns where that converges, and its unique analytic continuation everywhere else. Euler connected it to the primes; Riemann took it into the complex plane.
Critical strip, critical line
The strip where the real part of s lies between 0 and 1, and the line down its middle at exactly one half.
Functional equation
The identity relating ζ(s) to ζ(1−s). It is the reflection symmetry the order reads as a fold.
Self-adjoint operator
An observable in quantum mechanics. Its eigenvalues are real necessarily — which is why finding one whose spectrum is the zeros would settle the Hypothesis at a stroke.
Stress-energy tensor
The full source of gravity in general relativity: energy, momentum, pressure and stress. Not mass, and certainly not baryon number.
Baryonic matter
Ordinary matter made of protons and neutrons. About one sixth of the matter, and under five per cent of the total content of the universe.
Entropic force
A force with no microscopic carrier, arising statistically because a system has more ways to be in one configuration than another. Rubber elasticity is the standard example.
Equivalence (mathematical)
Mutual derivability. Not resemblance, not analogy, not rhyme.

The Reading List

  • ◆ Marcus du Sautoy, The Music of the Primes (2003) — the best popular account of RH, and the one that explains why the zeros are music.
  • ◆ John Derbyshire, Prime Obsession (2003) — alternating chapters of history and actual mathematics; do the mathematics chapters.
  • ◆ Harold Edwards, Riemann's Zeta Function (1974) — the serious book, including a translation of the 1859 paper, which is short and worth reading in the original.
  • ◆ Michael Berry & Jonathan Keating, "The Riemann zeros and eigenvalue asymptotics" (SIAM Review, 1999) — the physics bridge, stated by the people who built it.
  • ◆ Ted Jacobson, "Thermodynamics of spacetime" (Physical Review Letters, 1995) — four pages that derive Einstein's equations from a horizon. Whatever you conclude, read it.
  • ◆ Erik Verlinde, "On the origin of gravity and the laws of Newton" (2011), and its critics — read both or neither.
  • ◆ Clifford Will, Was Einstein Right? — what the tests of general relativity actually measured, which is the wall any new theory of gravity has to get past.
  • ◆ The order's own atlas — the seam is drawn on the front page and closes the Ladder of Scale. Read the doctrine there as emblem, which is how it is meant.

A Closing Stance

The order believes this one. That is precisely why it has been written out here with its own refutations attached and the grade left honest at the foot of every paragraph. A belief that has to be protected from the evidence is not a belief, it is a possession; and the first thing this order asks of anyone is that they hold their best idea the way they would hold someone else's worst.

If the Seam is right, someone will one day derive it and it will stop being myth. If it is wrong, the five objections above are where it will die, and we would rather have written them ourselves. Test everything kindly — and test this first.
