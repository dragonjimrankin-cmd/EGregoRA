The Library

Further Research

The reading list, with its sources visible. Check every one of them.

This is the spine of EGregoRA. Nothing said on the podcast, drawn in a plate or printed in the codex is meant to be taken on authority — including from Ed. The list below is sorted by limb and marked by evidential weight, so you always know whether you are holding peer-reviewed science, serious scholarship, contested speculation or frank mythology. All four are worth reading. Confusing them is the only sin.

◆ Established science · ◆ Scholarship & history · ◆ Speculative / contested · ◆ Myth, tradition & primary esoteric text

✦

Cosmic Physics & Astronomy

  • ◆ Carlo Rovelli — The Order of Time; Reality Is Not What It Seems.
  • ◆ Sean Carroll — The Big Picture; From Eternity to Here (entropy and the arrow of time).
  • ◆ Leonard Susskind — The Black Hole War (information, horizons, holography).
  • ◆ Jim Al-Khalili & Johnjoe McFadden — Life on the Edge (quantum biology, carefully).
  • ◆ NASA ADS, arXiv (astro-ph) and the ESA Gaia archive — the primary literature, free.

Trees, Druidry & the Living Earth

  • ◆ Suzanne Simard — Finding the Mother Tree (mycorrhizal networks, from the researcher herself).
  • ◆ Merlin Sheldrake — Entangled Life.
  • ◆ Robin Wall Kimmerer — Braiding Sweetgrass (indigenous knowledge and botany, held together).
  • ◆ Ronald Hutton — Blood and Mistletoe: The History of the Druids in Britain. The indispensable corrective.
  • ◆ Robert Graves — The White Goddess. Magnificent, enormously influential, and historically unreliable. Read it as poetry.

Sacred Geometry & Natural Form

  • ◆ D'Arcy Wentworth Thompson — On Growth and Form (1917). Still the foundation.
  • ◆ Philip Ball — Patterns in Nature; The Self-Made Tapestry.
  • ◆ H. S. M. Coxeter — Regular Polytopes (the real mathematics of the solids).
  • ◆ Keith Critchlow — Order in Space. Beautiful; treat its metaphysical leaps as claims, not conclusions.
  • ◆ Mario Livio — The Golden Ratio, which usefully debunks half of what you have been told about φ.

Neuroscience, Psychology & the Visionary State

  • ◆ Anil Seth — Being You (predictive processing and the controlled hallucination).
  • ◆ Carhart-Harris & Friston — “REBUS and the Anarchic Brain” (Pharmacological Reviews, 2019).
  • ◆ Michael Pollan — How to Change Your Mind; and Griffiths et al., Johns Hopkins psilocybin trials.
  • ◆ William James — The Varieties of Religious Experience (1902). Still unsurpassed.
  • ◆ C. G. Jung — Memories, Dreams, Reflections; Aion. Psychology on the border of myth.
  • ◆ Daniel Kahneman — Thinking, Fast and Slow; Keith Stanovich on dysrationalia. Know your own failure modes.

Biology & the Stubbornness of Life

  • ◆ Lynn Margulis — Symbiotic Planet (symbiogenesis; she was right and was mocked for it).
  • ◆ Nick Lane — The Vital Question; Transformer (energy, origins, metabolism).
  • ◆ Michael Levin — bioelectricity and morphogenesis; his open-access papers are the frontier.
  • ◆ Maturana & Varela — The Tree of Knowledge (autopoiesis; where biology meets epistemology).

Astrology — History & Honest Assessment

  • ◆ Nicholas Campion — A History of Western Astrology (2 vols). The scholarly standard.
  • ◆ Ptolemy — Tetrabiblos; Vettius Valens — Anthologies. The primary sources.
  • ◆ Shawn Carlson, “A double-blind test of astrology” (Nature, 1985) — and the debate that followed it.
  • ◆ Geoffrey Dean's meta-analyses. Read these before you defend the predictive claim.

God, the Law of One & the Contact Question

  • ◆ Elkins, Rueckert & McCarty — The Ra Material / The Law of One, Books I–V. The primary text; read it as philosophy.
  • ◆ Plotinus — The Enneads; Shankara on Advaita; Meister Eckhart's sermons. The same intuition, three cultures.
  • ◆ Alfred North Whitehead — Process and Reality. The hardest book on this list and the most rewarding.
  • ◆ Avi Loeb — Extraterrestrial; and the Galileo Project papers. Contested, but properly argued.
  • ◆ Jacques Vallée — Passport to Magonia. The UFO phenomenon read as folklore, which may be the most useful lens of all.
  • ◆ The 2021 and 2023 US ODNI UAP reports — the actual official record, rather than what people say is in it.

Magic, Wizards & Symbolic Practice

  • ◆ Frances Yates — The Art of Memory; Giordano Bruno and the Hermetic Tradition. Essential.
  • ◆ Ronald Hutton — The Triumph of the Moon (where modern pagan magic actually came from).
  • ◆ The Corpus Hermeticum; Agrippa — Three Books of Occult Philosophy. The primary sources, in their own voice.
  • ◆ Alan Moore and Grant Morrison on magic-as-fiction-as-magic. Unserious in tone, serious in insight.
  • ◆ Ellen Langer and the placebo/expectancy literature — the measurable edge of ritual.

Alchemy & the Elemental Forces

  • ◆ Lawrence Principe — The Secrets of Alchemy. The best modern account; he reproduces the experiments.
  • ◆ William Newman — Atoms and Alchemy; Newman & Principe on Starkey and Boyle. Where chymistry becomes chemistry.
  • ◆ The Emerald Tablet; Maier's Atalanta Fugiens (1617); the Mutus Liber. Primary sources — read as emblem, not instruction.
  • ◆ C. G. Jung — Psychology and Alchemy; Mysterium Coniunctionis. The opus as a map of individuation.
  • ◆ Peter Atkins — The Four Laws That Drive the Universe; and any good text on phase transitions. The elements, rendered as states and energetics.
  • ◆ Gaston Bachelard — The Psychoanalysis of Fire; Water and Dreams. Why the four still think so well.

The Shadow Cabinet — Occultism, Propaganda & Fascism

Studied as pathology. These are works of history and warning.

  • ◆ Nicholas Goodrick-Clarke — The Occult Roots of Nazism. The definitive, sober account; it deflates as many myths as it confirms.
  • ◆ Heather Pringle — The Master Plan: Himmler's Scholars and the Holocaust (the Ahnenerbe).
  • ◆ Eric Kurlander — Hitler's Monsters: A Supernatural History of the Third Reich.
  • ◆ Primo Levi — If This Is a Man; Hannah Arendt — The Origins of Totalitarianism. Never study the symbols without the consequences.
  • ◆ Umberto Eco — “Ur-Fascism” (1995). Fourteen warning signs; learn them by heart.
  • ◆ The USC Shoah Foundation and Yad Vashem archives — primary testimony, freely available.

How to Read Like a Member of This Order

1. Separate the claim from the feeling it gives you

Awe is evidence that you are alive, not that a statement is true. Enjoy the awe; audit the claim separately.

2. Ask what would have to be false

Any idea worth holding can tell you what observation would overturn it. Ideas that cannot are not knowledge — they may still be beautiful, but file them under myth.

3. Find the best critic, not the worst

Read the strongest opponent of the thing you love. If you cannot state their case in their own words, you do not yet understand your own.

4. Follow the citation to its source

Most esoteric "facts" are a chain of books quoting books. Walk it back to the primary document. Roughly half the chains end in nothing at all.

5. Keep the two ledgers open

One for what is demonstrated, one for what is meaningful. Let them inform each other. Never let one forge entries in the other.
