The Open Door

Ask Ed a Question

No question is too large, too small or too strange. Several are too urgent — see the note below.

The Oracle

Ask about anything and The Order will give you its view. Cooking, grief, a gearbox, a bad week, a line of code: it will talk about the thing you actually asked about rather than steering you back to sacred geometry, and it will say it doesn't know when it doesn't. If a question needs today's news it will go and look it up and show you where it went, it will draw you a picture if you ask, and you can hand it a file to read and work from.

Gink

Gink is the order's familiar, and he keeps its answers. Ask him here and he will read the reply aloud in his own voice; the words are the order's written ones, unchanged.

He was Ed's fox. He died; the skull stayed, and sits on the desk within reach — a charm that feeds the connections and leans on him like an older part of his own soul. That the bone is there is fact; what it does is Ed's testimony, and we file it as such.

Waking Gink…

A room to talk in — the oracle holds the whole thread, and will talk about anything, not only the limbs.

The thread begins when you ask. Everything said stays here, in order.

    ASK GINK TO DRAW

    Make an Image

    Type what you want to see and Gink will draw it — photorealistic unless you ask for a painting, an engraving, a diagram or a cartoon. Made with Stable Diffusion, open weights, running on the order’s own GPU wherever one is awake — nothing borrowed from a free public service. Be specific: subject, setting, light, weather, lens, mood. A picture takes half a minute on a waking machine, and several if the order has to start one.

    Stability AI, open weights. The order’s own GPU is asked first and a hosted endpoint only if no machine of ours is awake. If one route refuses, the queue behind it still draws, and the finished picture says which machine made it.

    Draw the arrangement you want — where things sit, how big they are, roughly what colour. Gink follows the composition, not the handwriting: the lines you put down are a plan, and the picture is rendered properly from them. Eight hundred pixels square.

    ASK GINK TO FILM

    Make a Video

    Five seconds of 480p video from a sentence, made with HunyuanVideo 1.5 — Tencent's open-weights model, 8.3 billion parameters, code and weights published. Describe motion: what moves, how the camera moves, the light. If the order's own GPU is doing the work the clip takes twenty minutes or more, because the model is fetched fresh each time; on a paid endpoint it is about three. Either way, leave the page open and it will appear below when it is done.

    THE TURNING SHOP

    Make a 3D Model

    Build a thing out of solids. Add a box, a sphere, a cylinder, a cone or a torus; move it, turn it, scale it, colour it, and the piece assembles in the window below. It is deliberately simple — blocks and primitives, the way a model is roughed out before it is sculpted. Nothing is sent anywhere: the whole shop runs in this page. When you are done, take the model away as an OBJ file any 3D program will open, or hand the view to Gink and let him render it as a photograph.

    BUILD IT FROM WORDS

    A model is asked for a plan rather than a mesh — which pieces, at what size, turned which way, standing where — and the shop builds that plan out of the same solids and the same pattern book you would have dragged into place yourself. So what arrives has named parts, can be unstitched and worked on, and exports as an OBJ like everything else. It is a rough carpenter, not a sculptor: expect to move things.

    Scan something real with the camera

    Two photographs make a solid. The outline of the first gives the width of every slice of the thing; the outline of the second, taken a quarter turn round, gives the depth of the same slice. Sweep those two profiles together and you have a shape with the photograph wrapped over the front of it. One photograph still works — the depth is then guessed from the width, which is right for a mug and wrong for a book, and the page will say so. Nothing leaves this page: the camera stream, the frames and the finished mesh are all made and kept in your own browser.

      THE CUTTING ROOM

      Make a Montage

      Every clip and every picture you generate on this page is collected here. Put them in order, trim the ends or set how long a still holds, choose a cut or a crossfade, lay sound under the whole thing or onto single scenes, and the page will edit them into one film — rendered in your own browser, with no upload and no service in the middle. The result downloads as a single file. Clips made before you cleared the page are not kept; this is a cutting room, not an archive.

      • No shots yet. Draw a picture or film a clip above and it lands here — then any shot can be continued from, so the next one keeps the same characters.

      Sound

      The models make no sound at all, so every clip here is silent. Bring your own: a bed of music or narration under the whole film, or a piece of audio dropped onto one scene or a run of scenes. Scene sound wins wherever it lands — the bed drops out underneath it for exactly that stretch and comes back after. Nothing is uploaded; the audio is decoded and mixed in this page while the cut plays.

      The edit happens frame by frame on a canvas in this page and is recorded as it plays, so a two-minute montage takes two minutes to make. Nothing is sent anywhere. For anything more ambitious — narration, music, titles, stock footage, a real timeline — OpenMontage is installed in this repository under openmontage/; see the README there.

Used once, never written down, never logged, and the written answers still come first.

      Questions Already in the Pile

      Is astrology real?

      Real as a symbolic system with four millennia of continuous use and enormous cultural weight — yes, demonstrably. Real as a physical force by which Saturn's position alters your week — no; every controlled test of that claim has failed. Both halves of that sentence matter.

      Does the Law of One hold up?

      As cosmology, it is untestable. As philosophy, its core — that consciousness is unitary and that free will is sacred — sits in good company with Advaita Vedanta, Plotinus and Whitehead. Read it as a text, not a transcript, and it becomes much more interesting.

      What “The Order’s view” means, and does not

      The Order will give you its view on anything you ask. That is a promise about willingness, not about authority. What answers you is an oracle built by this order: a body of written answers composed by hand, and, where none of them fits, a language model asked to reason in the order’s voice. It is not Edward Gregory, it is not Jim Rankin, and a view is not a verdict.

      So: it can be wrong, and confidently so. It can misremember a date, a dose, a citation or a law. Where it has looked something up it shows you where it went, and those sources deserve the same scepticism as everything else here. On anything that touches your body, your mind, your money, your safety or the law, treat what you read as a place to start thinking, and take it to a doctor, a lawyer, an accountant or someone who knows you. It does not know your history, cannot see you, and will not notice if you are in trouble.

      If you are in crisis, please reach a human being tonight rather than a page: in the UK, Samaritans on 116 123, free, any hour; elsewhere, your local emergency number or crisis line.

      The order’s own standard applies to its own oracle. Test everything kindly. An answer that cannot survive your scepticism has not earned your belief, whoever — or whatever — wrote it.
