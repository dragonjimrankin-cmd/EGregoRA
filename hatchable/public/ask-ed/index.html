No question is too large, too small or too strange. Several are too urgent — see the note below.

The Oracle

Ask now — and keep asking

This is a conversation, not a search box. The oracle holds the thread, so you can follow up, push back, ask what it meant, or say "go on". It answers from a body of 1,280 written answers composed for EGregoRA and graded by the house rules on evidence; where a model key is configured it composes fresh replies in the same voice, anchored to those same written answers rather than invented. Where it has nothing, it says so. It will also simply talk — a greeting, an aside, a bad day, something with nothing esoteric about it at all — and when a question needs something it cannot know from memory, it can search the open web and read the pages it finds, then tell you which ones it used. It can also draw — ask the fox for a picture and it makes a photorealistic image with FLUX.1-schnell, an open-weights model, shown here and clearly labelled as generated rather than photographed — it can film — five seconds of 480p video from HunyuanVideo 1.5, open weights again — and it can read what you give it: attach a file and it will quote, search, summarise and argue with the contents rather than guessing at them. It is not Ed himself — but everything asked here lands in his mailbag, and the best questions get answered properly on the podcast.

A room to talk in — the oracle holds the whole thread, and will talk about anything, not only the limbs.

The Oracle

Ask me anything inside the eleven limbs — cosmic physics, druidry and trees, sacred geometry, astrology, neuroscience, biology, the Law of One and contact, the Shadow Cabinet, magic and ritual, alchemy, consciousness — or anything else at all. Cooking, grief, a gearbox, a bad week, Roman history, a line of code: I will talk about it properly rather than steering you back to sacred geometry. Ask me something awkward and watch me say I don't know — or just say hello, if that is where you are. If a question needs today's news or a figure I ought to check, I will go and look it up and show you where I went. Ask me to draw something and I will — a photograph of a thing that does not exist, made to order, by an open-weights model the order runs rather than a company's. And you can hand me a file: attach notes, a transcript, a spreadsheet or a chapter and I will read it properly and work from it.

    ASK THE FOX TO DRAW

    Make an Image

    Type what you want to see and the fox will draw it — photorealistic unless you ask for a painting, an engraving, a diagram or a cartoon. Made with FLUX.1-schnell, an open-weights model, so no company's key is required. Be specific: subject, setting, light, weather, lens, mood. A picture takes fifteen to forty seconds.

    Every image here is generated, not photographed. It is a synthesis made to order and is never evidence of anything. The fox will not draw a real person in a situation they were never in, and nothing made here should be passed off as a record.

    ASK THE FOX TO FILM

    Make a Video

    Five seconds of 480p video from a sentence, made with HunyuanVideo 1.5 — Tencent's open-weights model, 8.3 billion parameters, code and weights published. Describe motion: what moves, how the camera moves, the light. A clip takes about three minutes; leave the page open and it will appear below.

    Every clip is generated, not filmed: nothing here records a real event, and it should never be presented as though it did. Video needs a GPU somewhere even when the model is open — if no video key is connected to this project, the fox will say so rather than pretend.

    Test everything kindly. If an answer cannot survive your scepticism, it does not deserve your belief — and the written form below reaches Ed himself.

    Questions Already in the Pile

    Is astrology real?

    Real as a symbolic system with four millennia of continuous use and enormous cultural weight — yes, demonstrably. Real as a physical force by which Saturn's position alters your week — no; every controlled test of that claim has failed. Both halves of that sentence matter.

    Does the Law of One hold up?

    As cosmology, it is untestable. As philosophy, its core — that consciousness is unitary and that free will is sacred — sits in good company with Advaita Vedanta, Plotinus and Whitehead. Read it as a text, not a transcript, and it becomes much more interesting.
