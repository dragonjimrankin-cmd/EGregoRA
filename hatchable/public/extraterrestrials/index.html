Limb VII

Extraterrestrials

Two possibilities, Clarke said, and both are terrifying: either we are alone, or we are not. This order takes the question seriously enough to refuse every cheap answer to it — the sneer and the swoon alike.

The Order's Position ◆

Not Visitors. Neighbours.

The search described above assumes a particular kind of alien: a biological creature on a distant rock, crossing the gap in a machine. EGregoRA holds that this picture is almost certainly too small — and that it is the reason eighty years of looking upward has found so little.

The nuts-and-bolts hypothesis asks us to believe that something crossed four light-years of hard vacuum, arrived in working order, and then spent the next eight decades showing itself to lone drivers on back roads while leaving no wreckage, no sample and no paperwork. As a logistics problem it is absurd. As a description of what witnesses actually report — objects that appear and vanish rather than arrive and depart, that move without inertia, that are seen by one person in a car and not by the person beside them, that come with a flood of meaning rather than a flood of data — it has never fitted the evidence particularly well either.

The older framing fits better. What we are dealing with are interdimensional, inter-density intelligences: beings whose existence is not located at some distance from ours but at a different depth within it. Not far away. Not even, strictly, elsewhere. Alongside — and thoroughly, permanently intertwined with the fabric of ordinary human life.

What "density" means here

The Law of One material describes reality as a graded series of densities — octaves of conscious experience, each complete in itself, each largely invisible from below. Matter and the elements occupy the first. Plant life occupies the second, self-awareness the third, which is where we presently stand. Above that are modes of being that still possess form and will and individuality, but for which the physical body is optional rather than obligatory.

On that account an entity of the fourth density or higher is not required to travel here at all. It is already here, in the way that the whole radio spectrum is already in the room you are sitting in and your eyes report an empty space. What changes in an encounter is not the visitor's position but the witness's bandwidth — a shift of attention, of brain state, of place, of hour, of fear, of grief, of sacrament — which momentarily widens what can be received.

Why this explains the pattern

  • The vanishing. Objects that wink out mid-flight are not accelerating away. They are leaving the narrow band in which our instruments and retinas operate.
  • The selectivity. Two people in the same car, one sees it. That is not how aircraft work. It is exactly how a perceptual threshold works.
  • The shapeshifting. Fairies, then airships, then discs, then greys — see Plate VII. A being that presents through the witness's own symbolic apparatus will always wear the local century's clothing, because that is the only wardrobe available.
  • The thin places. Encounters cluster at the same sites that every tradition already marked out: hill forts, standing stones, lakes, groves, the edges of water, the hours around dawn and the dead of night. The geography of UFO reports and the geography of the old faery lore are, embarrassingly for both camps, the same map.
  • The moral content. Almost every serious encounter delivers a message, and the message is almost always the same one: about stewardship, about the Earth, about our capacity for destruction, about the unity underneath apparent separation. Spacecraft do not deliver sermons. Intelligences concerned with our development do.
  • The silence overhead. Fermi's paradox only bites if everyone is doing what we are doing. If the galaxy's elders long ago stopped being the kind of thing that needs rockets and radio, then our listening equipment is a net with holes the size of houses, cast in the wrong ocean.

Intertwined, not visiting

This is the part the order considers most important, and the part most often missed by both the believers and the debunkers. If these intelligences are not arriving from somewhere but are a standing feature of the reality we are embedded in, then they are not an event that may one day happen to humanity. They are a condition we have always lived inside.

They are in the ◆ continuity of the record: the sky people of every cosmology, the sidhe under the hill, the angel at the threshing floor, the daimon that Socrates would not disown, the watchers of Enoch, the rainbow serpent, the grey at the foot of the bed. They are in the apparatus that receives them, which is why a change of brain state changes what appears. They are in the geometry that recurs identically in the visions of people who have never met. And — the Law of One is uncompromising on this point — they are in us: the same consciousness at a different octave, which is why contact so often feels less like meeting a stranger than like being remembered by something that never lost sight of you.

The ethic that falls out of this is free will. If these beings are woven through our existence and vastly our seniors, their visibility is a choice, and their overwhelming restraint is the most telling datum in the whole file. You are not being studied by zoologists. You are being left room, deliberately, by something that regards your freedom to get this wrong as more valuable than its own convenience in setting you right.

Shelved honestly. This section is graded ◆ myth, tradition and primary esoteric text, and the grade is not a hedge — it is the whole discipline of this order applied to its own convictions. Nothing above is demonstrated. It is not testable in its current form, it explains the evidence partly by being unfalsifiable, and an account that can absorb every observation should make you more suspicious, not less. We hold it because it fits the shape of the testimony better than the alternatives, and we would abandon it tomorrow for something that fits better still. Read the science in Plates I–IV as science. Read this as what it is.

Begin Here

Three Questions, Routinely Confused

Almost every argument about aliens is actually three arguments wearing one coat. Separate them and the fog clears immediately.

  1. Is there life elsewhere? ◆ An astrobiological question, approached with telescopes and chemistry. Unresolved, actively advancing, and the odds look better every decade.
  2. Has anything intelligent reached Earth? ◆ A question of physical evidence. Here the record is enormous, chaotic, and — after eighty years — still without a single object, sample or image that survives expert handling.
  3. What would contact mean? ◆ A philosophical and spiritual question, the oldest of the three, and the one the channelled literature actually addresses.

You can hold a confident answer to the first, agnosticism on the second and deep interest in the third without a single contradiction. Most people instead let the appeal of question three do the work of answering question two, and that is where the discipline is lost.

Grading throughout: ◆ established science · ◆ scholarship and history · ◆ speculative and contested · ◆ myth, tradition and primary esoteric text.

Eight Plates

The State of the Question

From the chemistry of worlds to the anatomy of an encounter.

Plate I ◆

The Worlds Are There

In 1992 humanity knew of no planet outside this solar system. The confirmed count is now past 5,800, and the statistics of the Kepler survey imply that planets are the ordinary consequence of star formation, not the exception.

Perhaps one in five Sun-like stars carries a roughly Earth-sized world in the liquid-water zone. In this galaxy alone that is billions of candidate surfaces, and there are two trillion galaxies. The shortage of real estate, which was a serious objection in 1960, is gone.

Mayor and Queloz took the 2019 Nobel for 51 Pegasi b. What remains unknown is not whether the worlds exist but whether chemistry crosses into biology easily or almost never.

Plate II ◆

The Drake Equation Is a Confession

Drake's 1961 formula multiplies seven terms to estimate the number of communicating civilisations. The first three are now measured rather well. The last four — how often life starts, how often it becomes intelligent, how often that builds a transmitter, and how long such a thing lasts — are each uncertain by many orders of magnitude.

Multiply those uncertainties together and the honest output spans from "we are the only ones" to "millions". The equation's real value was never the number. It was in laying out exactly which ignorances stand between us and the answer.

Plate III ◆

Fermi's Question Still Stands

At lunch in Los Alamos in 1950, Fermi asked: where is everybody? The galaxy is around 13 billion years old. Even at a crawling one percent of lightspeed, a patient civilisation could cross it in a few tens of millions of years — an eyeblink. The sky should be busy. It is silent.

The proposed resolutions are a map of our anxieties. A Great Filter behind us, meaning life or intelligence is vanishingly rare; a filter ahead, meaning technological species reliably destroy themselves; the zoo hypothesis, that we are deliberately left alone; the dark forest, that broadcasting is suicidal; or simply that we have searched a laughably small volume for a laughably short time.

All remain live. None is established. The silence is real data and nobody knows what it means.

Plate IV ◆

How We Are Actually Looking

Radio SETI has listened since Drake's Project Ozma in 1960; Breakthrough Listen now surveys a million nearby stars. Nothing has ever repeated. The 1977 "Wow!" signal lasted 72 seconds and has never returned despite hundreds of re-examinations.

The sharper tool is now spectroscopy. JWST can read the atmosphere of a transiting planet by the starlight filtered through it, hunting combinations — oxygen with methane, say — that chemistry should not sustain without something alive maintaining the disequilibrium.

Watch the K2-18b story as a lesson in how this works: a dimethyl-sulphide claim in 2023, headlines, then careful reanalysis showing the signal was far weaker than announced. That cycle is not science failing. It is science working, in public, at speed.

Plate V ◆

The UAP Record

Something genuinely changed after 2017. The Pentagon acknowledged a programme; three Navy videos were released and authenticated; the 2021 ODNI preliminary assessment examined 144 incidents and could explain one. Congress created AARO. Trained observers with gun-camera footage are a different class of witness from a blurry photograph.

And still: not one recovered artefact, not one unambiguous high-resolution image, no instrumented multi-sensor capture that rules out parallax, glare, sensor artefact, drone, balloon or classified hardware. The GOFAST object's "impossible speed" dissolves once you do the parallax geometry properly.

The correct position is uncomfortable and dull: unidentified is a confession of ignorance, not a vehicle. Something is in the data. Nothing in the data requires another star.

Plate VI ◆

The Abduction Phenomenon

Hundreds of thousands of people sincerely report being taken. The experiences are vivid, consistent, profoundly disturbing, and — this matters — generally reported by people with no other sign of mental illness. They are not lying and they are not mad.

There is also a well-mapped neurology here. Sleep paralysis occurs when REM atonia persists into waking: the body is immobilised, the threat system is fully online, and the brain hallucinates a presence in the room. Around eight percent of people experience it. Add temporal-lobe activity, hypnagogic imagery, and the documented capacity of hypnotic regression to manufacture detailed false memories, and the anatomy of the classic account is largely accounted for.

This explains the form of the experience. It does not entitle anyone to contempt for the people having it, and this order will not extend any. See Limb V.

Plate VII ◆

The Visitors Change With the Century

The sky people are never quite strangers. Medieval Europe met fairies who stole children and returned them disoriented with missing hours. The 1890s produced a wave of mysterious airships, crewed by American inventors, in the decade of the airship. 1947 gave us discs, at the dawn of the jet age. The 1960s began the surgical greys, as clinical medicine entered every home.

Vallée's observation is the uncomfortable one: the structure — abduction, missing time, a message about humanity's fate, the return — is remarkably stable, while the costume tracks the technology of the era precisely.

Two readings, both serious. Either the phenomenon is generated by human minds and dresses in contemporary clothes, or something real presents itself in whatever idiom the witness can receive. Vallée himself leans to the second.

Plate VIII ◆

The Channelled Literature

A large body of text claims to transmit non-human intelligence directly: the Law of One material (Elkins, Rueckert and McCarty, 1981–84), Bashar, the Pleiadian canon, and a hundred smaller streams. The Law of One is the one this order reads most closely — its densities, its service to self and service to others, its insistence on free will and non-interference as the governing ethic of contact.

Its claims are not testable, and this page will not pretend otherwise. What can be said is that it is internally coherent across five volumes, that it declines to make the falsifiable predictions its imitators love, and that it repeatedly instructs the reader to discard anything that does not resonate — an unusual move for a text seeking authority.

Read as philosophy and as primary esoteric document. Shelved ◆, deliberately and without embarrassment. See the Black Tribunal, Limb VIII, and Limb VII on the Introduction.

What Survives Scrutiny

  • ◆ Planets are common; most stars have them, and small rocky worlds in temperate orbits are abundant.
  • ◆ The building blocks are everywhere: amino acids in meteorites, complex organics in interstellar clouds and on comets.
  • ◆ Life on Earth appeared remarkably early — within a few hundred million years of the surface becoming habitable.
  • ◆ Subsurface oceans exist on Europa and Enceladus, and Enceladus is venting organics into space for free sampling.
  • ◆ Extremophiles survive conditions once assumed lethal, widening the habitable envelope considerably.
  • ◆ Atmospheric biosignature detection is now technically possible; this is the live frontier.
  • ◆ A residue of UAP cases, with trained observers and instrument data, is genuinely unexplained.
  • ◆ The encounter narrative is cross-cultural and ancient, whatever is generating it.
  • ◆ The silence is a real observation requiring a real explanation.

What Does Not

  • ◆ Ancient astronauts built the pyramids. We have the quarries, the ramps, the tools, the workers' village, their bread ovens and their pay disputes. The theory's load-bearing assumption is that non-European people could not have done it.
  • ◆ Roswell was a flying saucer. The declassified answer — Project Mogul, a high-altitude acoustic balloon array for detecting Soviet tests — is a genuine secret, just not that one.
  • ◆ The Nazca lines are runways. Drawn on soft ground that would not support an aircraft, by a culture whose pottery shows the same figures.
  • ◆ 'Oumuamua was a probe. An odd object, certainly. Natural explanations — a nitrogen-ice shard, a fluffy fractal aggregate — require no new physics and have not been excluded.
  • ◆ Crop circles. Bower and Chorley demonstrated the method with a plank and a rope in 1991, after fifteen years of doing it.
  • ◆ The Face on Mars. Resolved at higher resolution into an ordinary mesa, as the pareidolia account predicted.
  • ◆ "The government has the bodies." Eighty years, four continents, tens of thousands of participants, zero documents, zero defectors with material. The conspiracy is a harder claim than the craft.

Discarding these makes the serious version of the question stronger, not weaker. The weak arguments are what let people dismiss the whole subject unexamined.

Method

How to Weigh a Contact Claim

This subject is the single most efficient generator of nonsense in the modern world, and also possibly the most important question there is. Both facts demand the same thing: a procedure.

1 · Find the primary document

Not the documentary, not the book citing the documentary. The original report, the original interview, the original sensor file. A startling proportion of famous cases shrink dramatically at this step — the detail everybody repeats turns out to have entered the story on its third retelling.

2 · Ask what the witness could see from where they stood

Most dramatic footage is a distant object against a featureless sky. Without a known range you cannot derive size, speed or acceleration — and parallax from a moving camera manufactures impossible motion for free. The GOFAST object is the textbook case.

3 · Count the inferential steps

"I saw a light manoeuvre strangely" is one claim. "Therefore it was a craft, therefore it was not human, therefore it came from another star, therefore it is being concealed" is four more, each needing its own evidence. Claims are routinely smuggled through as a single package.

4 · Check whether the absence of evidence is expected

If a claim implies physical traces — metal, radiation, wreckage, bodies, paperwork — then their total absence is itself evidence. If it implies nothing checkable at all, it is not a hypothesis. It is a position.

5 · Notice what you want to be true

The hardest step and the one that matters. Nearly everyone in this field, sceptic and believer alike, arrived with a wish: for company, for rescue, for meaning, or for a tidy universe with nothing strange in it. Name yours before you assess the evidence, or it will do the assessing for you.

Lineage

A Record of the Question

  • c. 400 BCE The atomists argue for infinite worlds; Epicurus writes that there must be other earths with other living things.
  • 1584 Giordano Bruno publishes on innumerable inhabited worlds. Burned in 1600 — for a broad heresy, not this alone, though it was on the list.
  • 1877 Schiaparelli records canali on Mars; mistranslation gives Lowell his canals and the public its first alien civilisation.
  • 1896 The American airship wave — mystery craft crewed by inventors, in the decade of the dirigible.
  • 1938 Welles broadcasts The War of the Worlds; the panic was smaller than legend, the cultural imprint enormous.
  • 1947 Kenneth Arnold sees nine objects near Mount Rainier and describes their motion as like a saucer. The press keeps the noun.
  • 1950 Fermi asks his question over lunch at Los Alamos.
  • 1960 Drake points a dish at Tau Ceti and Epsilon Eridani. Project Ozma begins the search.
  • 1961 The Hill case introduces abduction, missing time and hypnotic regression to the canon.
  • 1974 The Arecibo message is transmitted toward M13, arriving in roughly 25,000 years.
  • 1977 The Wow! signal. 72 seconds, never repeated.
  • 1981 The Law of One sessions begin in Louisville, Kentucky.
  • 1992 First confirmed exoplanets, orbiting a pulsar. Three years later, 51 Pegasi b.
  • 1996 ALH84001 is announced as Martian fossil life; the structures are later explained abiotically, but astrobiology gets its funding.
  • 2017 'Oumuamua passes through; the Pentagon's AATIP programme is revealed in the press.
  • 2021 The ODNI assessment: 144 incidents, one explained.
  • 2022– JWST begins reading exoplanet atmospheres. The biosignature era starts in earnest.

Glossary

Biosignature
A chemical or spectral feature best explained by life — typically an atmospheric disequilibrium nothing geological can maintain.
Technosignature
Evidence of technology rather than biology: a narrowband transmission, industrial pollutants, waste heat, a megastructure.
Habitable zone
The orbital band where liquid water can persist on a surface. Necessary, nowhere near sufficient.
Great Filter
Hanson's proposed improbable step between dead chemistry and a galactic civilisation. Whether it lies behind us or ahead of us is the whole question.
Dark forest
The hypothesis that broadcasting one's position is lethally unwise, so everyone sensible stays quiet.
UAP
Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena. The replacement term for UFO, adopted to escape the cultural baggage and to include non-flying cases.
Pareidolia
The perceptual tendency to impose familiar form — faces, craft, figures — on noise. A feature of a predictive brain, not a defect.
Sleep paralysis
REM muscle atonia persisting into wakefulness, with a hallucinated presence. The physiological core of many night encounters.
Panspermia
The proposal that life, or its precursors, travels between worlds on debris. Plausible for chemistry; unproven for organisms.
Densities
In the Law of One, graded octaves of conscious experience through which all things evolve. ◆

  • ◆ Elkins, Rueckert & McCarty, The Law of One, I–V (1984) — the primary channelled text, free in full online.

Where This Order Stands

That life exists elsewhere we think overwhelmingly likely, on the arithmetic alone. That something unexplained appears in the UAP record we accept; that it is a crewed vehicle from another star we do not accept, because nobody has shown it. That the encounter tradition is ancient, cross-cultural and psychologically real we hold as established — and that its meaning is unsettled we hold as obvious.

The Law of One is read here attentively and shelved honestly, as the testimony it claims to be rather than the data it does not claim to be. If it is right, it will not need us to have overstated it.

And the thing most worth saying: this is one of very few open questions where an ordinary person may live to see an answer. A single atmospheric spectrum could settle the first of our three questions inside a decade. Watch that, rather than the lights.
