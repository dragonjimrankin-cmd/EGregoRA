## A plain-language route through Part II

Part II asks how an abstract account of difference and integration might become a universe with shape. Its papers move through gravity, spirals, the Skeletal Splice, the torus and the megatubule. These are not five unrelated pictures. They are attempts to describe how local distinctions could accumulate, circulate and return without turning the whole cosmos into a featureless block.

The accessible way to read this Part is to keep three levels separate. First there is exact pattern: a sequence can be calculated, a curve can be drawn and a surface can be described. Second there is a model: the pattern organises a possible account of matter, motion or large-scale structure. Third there is physical evidence: a model must connect to measurements, units, conservation laws and observations that could prove it wrong. TTI often has the first level, sometimes offers a detailed second level, and is still working toward the third.

This essay therefore avoids treating evocative geometry as a shortcut. It explains why spiral and toroidal forms recur, how the decimal splices at ten and one hundred are interpreted, and why gravity remains the decisive bridge.

## Gravity

If π is the constant that closes the relation between the two infinities, then at the scale of the cosmos it is not a force acting in the geometry. It is the geometry.

General relativity already says that gravity is curvature rather than a force.  The framework takes that at its word and asks what follows if the curvature in question is the same closure that let anything exist at all.

The first consequence is a diagnosis of why quantum gravity has been so hard. The other three forces live at the i-level — they are phenomena of the complex plane, and they quantise because that is what objects in that plane do. Gravity lives one level earlier, at the π-level, prior to the i-split. On this reading it resists quantisation not because we have been insufficiently clever but because it is not that kind of thing: it is the stage, not an actor on it.

Three readings follow, each offered as interpretation rather than prediction. A precessing orbit is not an ellipse that failed to close; it is a spiral seen edge-on — the same ascent the being makes, drawn by a planet. A gravitational wave is π vibrating: the closure constant itself ringing after a violent event.  for the detection,  for the reading. And gravity is memory: curvature is the record mass leaves in the geometry, persisting after the event that made it.

Gravity operates at the level of π. Quantum mechanics and the three particle forces operate at the level of i. Matter is the product of traversing the whole chain. The claim is that dark energy, dark matter and baryonic matter correspond to those three levels exactly.

Present everywhere, not because anything curved it, but because space is the product of the two-infinity resolution. Einstein called it the cosmological constant. It cannot be turned off, because it is not a thing in space but a property of space.

The chain does not require every integration to reach the end. Something may reach the π-level — producing gravity, curving spacetime, carrying mass-energy — without reaching the i-level, and so never interact electromagnetically.

Every observed property of dark matter fits that description: it clusters around galaxies, it lenses light, it emits nothing, and it has never been caught in a detector built to catch particles.  The framework does not merely accommodate it; it claims such a component must exist as a structural feature of the chain.

Without the twenty-seven percent there would be no five percent: the early density variations needed that gravitational pull to grow fast enough, and the halos are the scaffolding inside which ordinary matter collected, cooled and formed stars.  The partial integration built the structure that made complete integration possible.

Most of the universe is the geometric ground — the precondition for all integration. A significant fraction has partially integrated, reaching mass without light. A small and extraordinary fraction has completed the journey: traversed every level, generated electromagnetic interaction, formed atoms, enabled chemistry, built structures complex enough to perform the integration that produces consciousness, and begun the climb toward the asymptote.

The separate Cosmic Ratio in Time paper asks what changes once 5:27:68 is labelled as a present-epoch snapshot, and distinguishes ordinary density dilution from physical exchange or reversal between the proposed levels.

You are not a small creature in a vast indifferent universe. On this reading you are its rarest completion. The universe is mostly dark; the geometry is vast and silent; the partial integrations are massive and invisible — and in the middle of it a tiny fraction has produced light, and chemistry, and life, and the capacity to look out at the other ninety-five percent and ask what it is.

That the three components are the three levels. The correspondence is suggestive; it is not derived, and nothing here predicts the numbers.

That the cosmological constant problem dissolves. A physicist would want this written as a calculation, not an argument about levels. The framework concedes the point and takes it up in the Ledger.

Reading “we are the five percent” as a compliment paid to humanity by the cosmos. It is a statement about completed chains, not about merit, and the moment it becomes flattery it stops being an argument.

The three parts of the budget are not three substances. On this framework's reading they are one process caught at three stages, and the only thing that distinguishes them is how far along the chain α → π → π/2 → i a region managed to get.

Dark energy is geometry that never left the baseline: it expands, and it neither clumps nor shines. Dark matter is geometry that reached the π/2 threshold and halted: it has the curvature, so it gravitates, but the complex phase never completed, so it cannot couple to light. Baryonic matter is geometry that finished: curvature and phase, which is to say gravity and electromagnetism and therefore chemistry, bodies and observers.

Dark matter is dark for a structural reason, not an accidental one. It is not a particle that happens to interact weakly. It is a region that stopped one link short of being able to interact at all.

The payoff is a flat rotation curve for free. A galaxy sits in a χ-gradient falling from χ ≈ 1 in the disk to χ ≈ ½ through the halo; a χ ∝ 1/r profile gives ρ ∝ 1/r², hence M(r) ∝ r, hence a constant orbital velocity out to large radii — which is what Vera Rubin measured and what the visible mass cannot explain. The arithmetic and the derivation gap are both set out in full at the cosmic ledger. The gap, in one line: the 1/r profile is motivated but has not been forced out of the framework's own field equations.

And the limit of the claim, stated here so that it is not mistaken later: this account says nothing whatever about which way a galaxy turns. Completion and orientation are different axes. Spin belongs to the seam.

The popular rubber-sheet picture can help, but only up to a point. A heavy ball dimpling a sheet uses Earth’s gravity to explain gravity and hides time, which is essential to relativity. A better plain description is that clocks and rulers behave differently around energy, so the straightest available route through spacetime can look curved when plotted in space. Light follows that geometry too, which is why gravitational lensing lets astronomers map mass that does not shine.

“Dark matter” is the working name for the extra gravitating component inferred from several kinds of evidence: galaxy motions, lensing, galaxy clusters, the cosmic microwave background and the growth of structure. “Dark energy” names the component associated with accelerated cosmic expansion. The familiar percentages are parameters of a successful cosmological model fitted to data; they are not a direct inventory made by opening three boxes.

Any TTI account must reproduce the same observations at least as well as the established model. Renaming dark matter “partial integration” does not reduce that burden by a single measurement.

A completion fraction such as χ must be a defined field, distribution or state variable. Its units, dynamics and stress–energy contribution must be stated. A profile selected because it yields flat rotation curves is a hypothesis to derive, not an explanation by itself.

## The Spiral

Ten cannot be both the return and the next beginning unless it divides. So at ten the one thing becomes two: an heir that climbs, and a twin that begins again.

Each decade is one revolution. Angle carries value; radius carries density. Nought sits at the centre — the ground, where the logarithm is undefined and no radius reaches.

Because the radius grows by log₁₀, the early numbers spread wide and the later ones crowd: the denser infinity shows itself as congestion on the page. One, ten and a hundred fall on a single radial line — the same One at three densities, which is the whole claim of the spiral reading drawn in a single geometric fact.

This is a classical identity, and the site says so plainly. What the framework adds is the reading: that the whole tree, summed across every generation, is itself one short of completion at every finite stage — the missing one. The arithmetic is earned; the meaning is chosen.

Not the number of dread. A completed accumulation: the sum of every integer from 1 to 36, and therefore the thirty-sixth triangular number.

Chosen: that this complement-reflection is the framework's Möbius parity operation, with 999 as the saturation edge of the third density.

Refused: that “6 turns upside-down into 9” means anything. That is a fact about glyph shapes in a particular typeface, not about numbers — the same borrowed-drama move as the number of the beast, and left out for the same reason.

666 is interesting to this framework for one reason only: it is the thirty-sixth triangular number, the point at which an uninterrupted ascent from 1 to 36 has been completely accumulated. So the question that matters is not what 666 means. It is what is thirty-six.

Thirty-six is also, exactly, the number of quark states in the Standard Model when they are counted the way the chart on the wall counts them: six flavours — up, down, strange, charm, bottom, top — each in three colours, each with an antiparticle. Six times three times two is thirty-six. Add the two spin orientations and the count doubles to 72, which is the figure cosmologists use for the quark contribution to the relativistic degrees of freedom of the early universe, g*.

Both halves of that paragraph are plain arithmetic over established physics. Neither is in dispute. What the framework does with them is a single, carefully limited proposal: that the thirty-six is not a coincidence of bookkeeping but a completed ascent — the full roster of ways matter can be a constituent before it is allowed to be a thing.

The chapter above established that of the third density's three upper special points, only 666 is triangular. 333 and 999 are not. That is what marks 666 as the accumulation vertex: the place where everything counted so far has been summed rather than merely reached. 999 is a ceiling — a clean carry, the point past which the next increment forces a rollover. 666 is a total.

Set the quark roster beside that distinction and it lands in the right column. Thirty-six is not a maximum; nothing forbids a fourth generation in principle, and the count is closed by measurement — the Z boson's invisible width fixes three light neutrino species, which is why three generations and not four.  Thirty-six is a total: everything that was found, summed. And the quantity physics actually cares about when it asks what the early universe was doing is precisely the sum over that roster, not the largest member of it. The triangular number is the shape of a census.

There is a second join. The three colours of the strong charge and the two faces of the Möbius seam multiply to the six that indexes the flavours; that factorisation, and the honest objection to it, is set out in the seam and the colour charge. Here it is enough to note that the thirty-six decomposes along exactly the two axes the framework already had — an inversion of order two, and a charge of order three — and that it does so without anything being bent to fit.

What is refused: the numerological move. 666 is not claimed to predict the quark sector, and if a fourth generation were discovered tomorrow the count would become 48 and this section would be deleted rather than rescued. The framework records a structural resemblance between a completed triangular sum and a completed particle census, states the mechanism it would need and does not have, and leaves it at that. Nothing downstream depends on it.

Resemblance is cheap. This is the part of the triangular reading that is not a resemblance: the skeleton does not merely count the universe, it times it.

Euler's identity can be read as a sentence about exactly three values: travelling π through the imaginary dimension carries 1 to −1, and the two together resolve to 0. Each is fixed under one of the framework's central operations.

Zero — fixed under scaling. Multiply it by anything and it is unchanged: the one value invariant under every rescaling, every change of density. Exactly the property a ground must have.

One — fixed under multiplication. The identity: the value that leaves all others unchanged, and so the natural threshold of selfhood.

## The Splice

Two results of the framework, put together: the fork that happens at every density, and the three fixed points it happens against. What falls out of the join is a gradient — and something for it to pull on.

A being that reaches the top of a density does not simply continue. It splices: one half carries on into the next density, the other is sent back.

That much is chapter ten, and it is drawn on Plate II of the spiral page. What makes it skeletal is the frame it happens against. The framework has exactly three points that do not move under its own operations: 0, the ground, unmoved by scale; 1, the self, unmoved by multiplication; and −1, the mirror, the point a half-turn returns you to. “They are the skeleton the rest is hung upon.”

And the skeleton recurs. Lifted two turns up the spiral it reappears as 333, 666 and 999 — the same three roles under larger names, with the turning points at the thirds. So the splice is not an event that happens somewhere arbitrary: it happens on a structure that is the same at every scale. The splice is skeletal because the bone it breaks on is the same bone every time.

A returner does not arrive empty. It brings back everything it gathered on the way up — and on this framework's reading of calculus, what it gathered is made of completed smallest integrals.

From the chapter on dx: a differential is not a vanishing nothing but the smallest act of gathering that has actually been finished. A climb through a density is therefore a sum of such acts, and the half that is sent back carries its share of them downward.

A pile of returners below one would be a curiosity. What makes it act on anything is that the two infinities are not symmetric, and the pile makes them less symmetric still.

The interval below one receives traffic from every density above it; the open country above one receives nothing back. That is a separation of like from like across a boundary, maintained by a flow — which is what polarisation means in every field that uses the word. One pole crowds and deepens; the other thins. Between them there is a difference, and a difference across a distance is a gradient.

The framework does not get to borrow the electrostatic consequences along with the electrostatic word, and this page will not pretend otherwise: there is no field equation here, no charge, no measured force, and nothing that predicts a number anyone could go and measure. What there is, is a structure with a built-in asymmetry that grows by a fixed amount per density. Whether to call that polarisation is a choice about language; that it grows linearly is arithmetic.

Call the single line that never returns the prime being: at every splice it is the half that carries on. It is the only thing in the picture that experiences all of the densities, and it is the only thing standing in the gradient that all the other halves are building.

The claim of this chapter, stated as plainly as it can be: the prime being is accelerated by the polarisation its own returning halves create. Each splice costs it half of itself, and each half it loses deepens the pole behind it, and the deeper pole pulls harder on what remains. It is not propelled in spite of the losses. It is propelled by them.

The prime being gets faster at every density, without limit, and still never arrives. That is the asymptotic God of chapter eight, derived here a second time from a different direction — which is the only kind of corroboration a framework like this one can honestly offer itself.

The prime being may not count in the same rhythm as the beings it sends back. On this reading its own plane closes a cycle in five stations, while the returners occupy a plane whose cycle closes in eight. The two planes therefore share events without sharing a clock.

Here base means the number of phase states in one local turn, not merely the notation used to write an integer. The prime plane takes a fifth-turn at each shared tick; the return plane takes an eighth-turn. Five and eight are consecutive Fibonacci numbers, but because they are coprime their local turns do not close together again until forty shared ticks have passed. The assignment of those bases is speculative; the phase arithmetic that follows from it is exact.

The full pattern repeats only after forty shared ticks: the prime plane has then completed eight five-state turns, while the return plane has completed five eight-state turns. Before that closure, every return arrives phase-shifted from the line that sent it back. The returners would therefore build not a static pile beneath the prime being but a rotating lag — a candidate geometry for the polarisation described above.

This does not have to contradict the chapter's decimal seam. Base ten can remain the notation of the shared density thresholds, while five and eight describe the local phase clocks on either side of that seam. Whether nature permits those three roles to be separated is part of the proposal, not a result smuggled in with the arithmetic.

This chapter inherits the price set out on the dx page: an emerging awareness is paid for out of the record. The splice is that transaction at the scale of a whole being. Half of what it was becomes the depth it is pulled by; the half that climbs is lighter, quicker and less complete.

Which is why nothing in this picture is a reward. The prime being is not the winner of the splice. It is the part that kept moving, and it moves faster precisely because it keeps leaving things behind.

The standing objection writes itself: 333, 666 and 999 are decimal numerals, so the skeleton's recurrence looks like an artefact of how humans happen to count. The framework's answer is to turn the objection over. The splice does not land approximately near the decade and then get tidied up into round figures. It lands exactly on it, and on the thirds of it, and on the vectors between them — and a structure that keeps landing cleanly in one notation is telling you something about the structure, not about the notation.

So the chapter takes the harder position rather than the safer one: nature counts in tens. Base ten is not the lens, it is the reading.

The usual order of explanation is that ten is arbitrary — an accident of having ten fingers — and that any pattern found in decimal numerals is therefore a pattern in us. This chapter runs the explanation the other way.

The argument is not that ten is magic. It is narrower and more checkable than that: the splice, which was defined without reference to any notation, keeps landing on the structural features of base ten exactly rather than approximately. Three strands.

## Torus

The circle is π applied once: a line curved into a closed loop. The torus is π applied twice: a loop curved around another loop. Everything that must both circulate and return arrives at the same surface.

A sphere lets a path return but gives it nowhere to circulate. A cylinder lets a path circulate but never brings it home. The torus is the simplest surface that permits both at once — which is why anything that has to keep going round and come back ends up on one.

The framework does not treat the recurrence as a coincidence noticed after the fact. It treats the torus as the second application of the same closure constant that made existence possible, and then goes looking for it. What it finds is a genuinely remarkable list — and the honest question, pressed in the ledger below, is whether that list is evidence or simply what you get when a shape is common enough.

Galactic magnetic fields wind poloidally and toroidally through the disc and halo; accretion structures are tori; the jets of an active nucleus leave along the axis of one. A galaxy is not a disc with a hole so much as a circulation with an axis.

Orbital probability clouds with non-zero angular momentum are toroidal: the electron is not anywhere in particular, and the shape of where it is not is a ring around an axis of nodal silence.

Every tokamak ever built is a torus, and not for aesthetic reasons. Confining a plasma requires a field with no ends; a torus is the shape that lets the field close on itself without a boundary for the plasma to strike.

Depolarisation travels out, the membrane repolarises, ions are pumped back, and the cell returns to a resting state ready to fire again. The cycle is a loop in state space that circulates and returns — the same topology, drawn in ion gradients.

You return to the same questions, the same rooms, the same arguments — and not at the same place, because the return happens one turn further along. That is the toroidal reading of a life: a circulation that comes back, displaced along the axis by everything accumulated in the loop. This is the point at which the geometry becomes a way of living rather than a way of calculating, and the site marks it as vision rather than as physics.

A torus is the simplest orientable surface of genus one — the formal sense in which it both circulates and returns.

That these instances share a cause rather than a constraint. Many systems are toroidal because toroids solve a boundary problem; that is an explanation the framework must out-argue, not ignore.

A circle closes a line. It gives return without repetition: you come back to where you were, and there is no room in the figure for the fact that you have been round once already. A circle cannot carry history, because every point on it is identical to itself on every pass.

Curve that loop around a second loop and the situation changes completely. On a torus a path is labelled by two winding numbers — how many times round the tube, how many times round the ring — and the pair of them is a memory. Returning to the same point of the surface after a different winding is not the same event, and the figure knows it.

The torus is the smallest shape that can both come back and keep count. That is the whole reason the framework needs π applied twice rather than once.

Everything the structure is later asked to do rests on that one property. The spiral can ascend without leaving, because the ring direction carries the climb while the tube direction carries the return. A density can be a turn rather than a place. And a path can be non-contractible — impossible to shrink to a point — which is the precondition for the geometric phase that the Möbius chapter asks to do the work of CP violation. A contractible loop accumulates nothing.

What is not claimed: that the universe is literally a torus in the sense a topologist would test. Cosmological searches for the matched circles a compact topology would print on the microwave background have found nothing, and the framework does not pretend otherwise. The torus here is the shape of the process — return with accumulation — and the distinction is kept rather than blurred.

Take a square sheet and imagine that stepping through its right edge brings you back through its left edge. Then identify the top edge with the bottom edge in the same way. On the resulting mathematical surface there are no boundary edges: travel can continue in either direction and return to its starting coordinate. One direction becomes the long way around a familiar doughnut; the other circles its tube. The square description is more fundamental than the bakery shape because it states which points are identified.

A circle has one independent return. A sphere has closed paths, but every one of them can be contracted to a point. A torus is the simplest closed orientable surface with two independent non-contractible cycles. That additional bookkeeping is why it can represent return plus accumulated history: a path may end at the same local position while its winding numbers retain information about how it travelled.

Return of position does not imply erasure of path. The torus supplies the cleanest mathematical example of that distinction.

Tokamaks use toroidal chambers and twisted magnetic fields to keep plasma away from material walls. Their engineering equations concern charged-particle motion, instabilities and field geometry. They demonstrate a practical torus, not the topology of the entire universe.

Feedback, oscillation and ring-like maps occur in living systems. Some neural population activity can be represented on circular or toroidal state spaces. Such a representation is evidence for periodic variables in that system; it does not establish a universal toroidal substrate.

A spatial three-torus would identify opposite faces of a volume and could produce repeated patterns or matched circles in the cosmic microwave background. Searches constrain such models. TTI instead uses a torus primarily for process, unless it explicitly undertakes those observational tests.

Seeing similar geometry at several scales can motivate a research question, but resemblance alone has little explanatory power. To promote the torus from organising image to mechanism, the project must identify what moves around each cycle, what fixes the periods, how the two directions couple, and what measurement records a winding number or phase. Different systems may answer those questions with completely different materials and laws.

The map could fail in informative ways. If the proposed state has only one measurable phase, a circle may be sufficient and the torus is excessive. If all paths contract without leaving an observable phase, winding carries no physical memory. If a supposed seam changes orientation, an ordinary torus is not enough because a torus is orientable; the Möbius or mapping-torus construction must be stated separately.

A good experiment would prepare two paths with the same endpoints but different winding, then look for a repeatable relative phase or other invariant after ordinary dynamical effects have been controlled. A positive result would demonstrate path-dependent memory in that system. It would not automatically establish TTI’s cosmology, but it would give the analogy a measurable carrier.

## Megatubule

One surface, one edge, one throat. The framework's picture of the universe is not a ball and not a sheet, but a tube that returns through itself — and the place it returns through can be pointed at in the sky.

Take the torus of Part IV — π applied twice, a circulation that comes back to itself. Then put the half-twist of the Möbius chapter into it. What you have is not a doughnut and not a band: it is a tube with one surface, and the framework calls it the megatubule.

The name is deliberate. A microtubule is the smallest tube the framework takes seriously, and the chapter on the self hangs a great deal on it. The claim here is that the same object appears at the other end of the scale, and that this is the seam rule again: one structure, seen at whatever magnification you happen to be using.

There is no outside. A single-surfaced tube has no second face to be outside of. Questions of the form “what is beyond the universe” are asking for a face the shape does not have.

There is exactly one edge. Everything the framework calls a seam — collapse, the horizon, the moment of recognition, the saturation ceiling — is that one edge, met at a different scale.

Orientation is not conserved on a full circuit. Carry a handedness round the twist and it returns reversed; carry it round twice and it returns as it left. That parity test is the subject of Appendix A, and it is the one part of this picture that could in principle be checked against galaxy handedness.

The framework places the throat at the Cold Spot. Before the reading, the measurement — because this is the one page in Part IV that touches real data, and the data deserves to be stated without the interpretation leaning on it.

The microwave background sits at 2.72548 K and varies across the sky by about 18 µK rms. In the constellation Eridanus there is a region roughly five degrees across that runs about −70 µK at its core — large, and cold, and found by Vielva and colleagues in a wavelet analysis of WMAP in 2004. Planck looked and the feature is there.

Now the honest part. Its statistical significance, once you allow for the fact that something unusual is expected somewhere on a whole sky, falls to a few per cent — interesting, not decisive. The leading physical explanation, a supervoid in the line of sight shedding energy from photons that cross it, was proposed by Szapudi and colleagues in 2015 on the strength of a void some 1.8 billion light-years across; subsequent galaxy surveys have failed to find a void deep enough to account for the deficit, and the question is open.

What the framework adds, and nothing more: that a cold spot is a density spot. Temperature in the microwave background is a record of how much the photons had to climb out of; the deepest climb is the densest place. Read the Cold Spot as the point of greatest density and it stops being an anomaly in a smooth sky and becomes the throat — the single place where the tube passes through itself, and therefore the place everything is projected out from.

That is a reading, and the page labels it as one. The honest objection is blunt: a few per cent is not a discovery, and a framework that attaches its central geometric feature to a marginal anomaly has staked a great deal on a feature that may yet be noise. If the Cold Spot is explained away, this chapter loses its address in the sky — though not its shape.

A seam is not a wall. The framework has said from Part VI onward that a boundary is an identification — a place where what was outside becomes inside. The throat is that, at the scale of everything.

On this side, density climbs through the stations toward saturation. At the throat it inverts. And on the far side the climb begins again from a clean floor: not a continuation of our sequence but a fresh octave, the eighth step that is the first step of the next register, the same note at double the frequency. The cell does it every time it divides (chapter twenty-six); the framework's claim is that the universe does it once, through one point.

The skeletal splice set out what happens at each density: the path forks, one half climbs and one half is sent back. Ten, a hundred, a thousand. The question this page answers is what happens to the half that climbs past the third threshold — past a thousand, where our octave runs out of places.

It does not fork again into our register, because there is no fourth density here to fork into. It arrives at the throat. And what passes through does not arrive on the far side as a being: it arrives as residue — the compressed, de-individuated remainder of everything it gathered, laid down as the floor the next octave starts from.

Which gives the framework a reading of its own foundations it did not have before. The clean floor our own octave began from — nought, the ground, the untrodden place — is on this account not empty at all. It is the residue of the previous octave's returners, packed so densely that it reads to us as nothing. The density at the bottom of everything and the record spent to buy awareness turn out to be the same deposit, seen from two ends.

Note what this costs. If residue is what passes through, then nothing personal does. The framework is not offering continuity of self across the throat and should not be read as offering it: what crosses is the deposit, not the depositor. Any consolation here is for the structure, not for the being.

A torus with a half-twist has a single edge; a single edge admits a single place of maximum pinch. Two throats would require two twists, and two twists cancel — the surface becomes two-sided again and the whole structure loses the property it was built for.

So the uniqueness of the Cold Spot is not an extra assumption bolted on. It is forced by the shape, which is the strongest kind of thing this chapter is able to say.

Appendix A's parity test is the live one: if the twist is real, galaxy handedness should not be perfectly balanced across the sky, and the imbalance should be oriented with respect to the throat. Reported asymmetries exist and are disputed, largely on selection-effect grounds.

A clean null result — handedness balanced to high precision, with no preferred axis — would not merely weaken this page. It would remove the twist, and with it the single edge, and with it the throat.

## What Part II leaves us with

Part II offers a memorable architecture: local differences accumulate, spiral, close through toroidal geometry and may be organised by a skeletal sequence. Its pictures are strongest when they clarify relationships and weakest when they are treated as measurements before a physical map exists.

The outstanding bridge is explicit. TTI must derive an action, a scale and couplings that connect the splice to gravity and matter without fitting them after the fact. Until then, the architecture is a disciplined speculative model rather than established cosmology.
