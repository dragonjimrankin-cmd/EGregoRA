## A plain-language route through Part III

Part III turns from cosmic architecture to boundaries. A boundary can separate, connect, reverse, preserve or transform. In The Two Infinities, the most important boundaries are called seams. They appear in the Möbius model, the Cosmic Ledger, accounts of entropy, temporal antipodes, residue across cosmic cycles, inverted tori, wormholes and the proposed phase relation between matter and antimatter.

These topics can easily blur into one grand image. This essay slows them down. A topological seam is not automatically a physical doorway. A reversal of orientation is not automatically a reversal of time. A bookkeeping device is not automatically a second universe. A remembered path is not automatically a transferable message. By separating those claims, the Part becomes more coherent rather than less imaginative.

The common question is what can survive change. If a process returns to a similar state, has everything been erased, or can the route leave a residue? Standard physics supplies constraints on entropy, causality, stress and energy. TTI supplies a speculative picture of integration and return. The useful work lies in showing exactly where one ends and the other begins.

## Möbius

The circle and the torus are levels in the chain: stable, closed, orientable. The Möbius strip is something else entirely — a transition between levels, the surface of the moment when what was outside one level becomes inside the next.

The argument. TTI uses the Möbius band as a disciplined model of return with changed handedness: after one circuit a local frame is reversed, and after two it is restored. The physical proposal is that some cosmic seam may act analogously on states transported around it.

The evidence line. A Möbius band is mathematically non-orientable, and its gluing rule is exact. That fact alone says nothing about antimatter, temporal direction, entropy, or the topology of spacetime.

Where the claim stops. To promote the seam into physics, the page must specify what state is transported, the connection governing transport, which observable changes sign, and how a laboratory or astronomical measurement differs from ordinary geometric phase.

## Entropy and Integration

Entropy is a family of precise quantities. “Integration” is TTI’s proposed metaphysical reading of how settled structure gathers. They may be compared; they must not be equated.

The argument. TTI proposes phases in which local organisation is assembled, maintained and released. The useful version of the claim is compatible with the second law: a subsystem can become more organised while producing greater entropy in its surroundings.

The evidence line. Thermodynamic entropy, Boltzmann or Gibbs entropy and Shannon information answer related but non-identical questions. Landauer's result concerns logically irreversible erasure under specified physical conditions; it is not a licence to equate every loss of meaning with heat.

Where the claim stops. No scalar 'integration' measure on this page has yet been shown to enter a thermodynamic state equation. The arrow of time cannot be derived from a verbal contrast between integration and disintegration.

In equilibrium statistical mechanics, entropy counts accessible microstates relative to a stated macro-description. In thermodynamics it is a state function whose changes constrain possible processes. In information theory it measures uncertainty in a distribution. Those definitions are related, but not interchangeable without assumptions.

TTI uses integration for a different idea: degrees of freedom becoming able to act as a coherent whole. That can be a useful organising image. No established law says that a higher TTI integration phase has lower entropy, more consciousness, more mass or a particular cosmological abundance.

For an isolated macroscopic system, entropy does not spontaneously decrease. Local order may grow while the surroundings gain still more entropy. Life, crystals and galaxies therefore do not refute the law: none is an isolated box, and gravitational systems require particular care.

A closed quantum state can evolve unitarily while subsystem entropy rises through entanglement. Decoherence can make interference inaccessible in practice without adding a literal collapse to the unitary equation. This is not, by itself, TTI integration or conscious gathering.

Shannon entropy quantifies expected surprise. Converting it into a thermodynamic cost requires a physical implementation and a protocol, as in erasure. Similar formulae do not license the claim that meaning, spirit or awareness is simply thermodynamic entropy.

Microscopic equations are often reversible while macroscopic records are not. Statistical mechanics explains the overwhelmingly likely direction of change given an extraordinarily low-entropy boundary condition in the past. It does not yet explain why that boundary condition obtained. Cosmology calls this the Past Hypothesis problem.

TTI’s geometric arrow and Temporal Antipodes offer metaphysical pictures of that boundary. They do not replace the probability measure, the microscopic dynamics or the low-entropy initial condition. A picture becomes physics only when it reproduces those ingredients and risks a contrary observation.

The arithmetic material belongs with the triangular-number spiral and Skeletal Splice, where exact identities can be checked before a cosmological meaning is considered. Symbolic resonance is retained here as history of the framework, not entered as physical evidence.

Take a shuffled deck of cards. One exact order is no more or less probable than any other exact order, so entropy cannot simply mean that one sequence looks untidy. The difference appears when many exact arrangements are grouped into a coarse description. “Every suit together in rank order” describes very few arrangements. “Thoroughly mixed” describes enormously many. A system is likely to move into the second kind of macrostate because there are more microscopic ways to realise it.

“Disorder” is sometimes a useful shortcut, but it easily misleads. A well-stacked library can have more thermodynamic entropy than a scattered cold pile of books because molecular energy, not visual neatness, dominates the count. Conversely, a turbulent pattern may look disorderly while carrying organised flows. The technical question is always: which states are being counted, under which constraints?

## Temporal Antipodes

The smaller infinity and the bigger infinity cannot ever fully synchronise because they exist at opposite ends of time.

The argument. TTI places smallward and largeward limits at opposite ends of a compactified temporal description. The conditional claim is modest but precise: if the endpoints are not physically identified and no transport law joins them, they cannot share an operational clock even when finite projections overlap.

The evidence line. Compactification is a standard mathematical technique, and relativity requires a protocol when distant clocks are compared. Drawing infinite limits at finite positions changes coordinates; it does not create signals or simultaneity.

Where the claim stops. The temporal assignment is a TTI postulate, not a consequence of Cantor's theorem. A physical version still needs states, a causal transport rule and a finite observable distinguishable from ordinary redshift, delay and time dilation.

They are proposed boundary conditions at opposite temporal asymptotes. Complete synchronisation would require a shared finite instant, a comparison rule and a channel that can carry timing information between them. On this conjecture, no such common clock exists.

That conclusion is conditional, not a theorem of Cantor, relativity or thermodynamics. It follows only if the two scale-directions are genuinely assigned to opposite ends of time, those ends remain asymptotic rather than physically identified, and synchronisation is required to be an operational relation rather than a resemblance in a diagram.

Smallward infinity means the direction of scale toward zero: halve a positive magnitude without end and it approaches a boundary it never reaches. Largeward infinity means the direction through larger and larger magnitudes. These are geometric directions, not claims about the cardinal sizes of sets.

Indeed, the established mathematics points the other way if the comparison is the real unit interval against the natural numbers:

The interval of numerically small values is uncountable, while the outward integer sequence is countable. The words smaller and bigger therefore name the magnitudes approached, not the amount of infinity present. Assigning either direction to an end of time is a further TTI postulate; Cantor’s result does not do it.

Compactification is established mathematics. Treating its two endpoints as physically real infinity-states is not. The map changes coordinates; it does not create a new cosmology.

If the endpoints are not identified, if comparison remains causal, and if no extra nonlocal law is supplied, then their endpoint states cannot be fully synchronised. The word cannot is earned only inside those assumptions.

Finite projections of the two boundary conditions could still appear in one interior history. If their effective frequencies are commensurate, projected phases may meet periodically. If the frequency ratio is irrational, they may return arbitrarily close without exactly repeating. A single alignment may also occur by initial condition or accident.

Those are the same mathematical distinctions used in the matter–antimatter phase essay, but the subjects are different. Temporal boundary conditions are not particles, and a shared phase is not annihilation, baryogenesis, memory transfer or communication from infinity. The analogy may organise the question; it cannot supply the missing physics.

The conjecture is presently metaphysical structure. To enter physics it must expose a finite observable and give that observable permission to disagree.

Ordinary propagation delays, redshift, decoherence and unsynchronised astrophysical sources are the null explanations. They are not evidence for temporal antipodes merely because they also produce lag.

TTI assigns smallward and largeward infinities to opposite compactified ends. This is a clear geometric proposal: finite history lies between two asymptotic conditions. The additional claim that those ends cannot synchronise is conditional. It follows only if no lawful identification or transport rule joins them. Topological separation on one diagram is not by itself a prohibition in physics.

This matters because it demonstrates a possibility often lost in the language of beginnings: a macroscopic asymmetry can be the visible history of a boundary condition under symmetric rules. But it also sets a limit. Entropy is an unsigned measure of inaccessible detail. Baryon number is signed. A reversible mixer can hide where the red and blue points went; if it preserves their colours, it cannot turn equal numbers of them into a net excess.

The baker’s map can explain how a residue becomes unreadable as an interaction. It cannot, by itself, explain why the residue carries the sign of matter.

Those are not equivalent hypotheses, and none has evidence supplied by the geometry alone.

## What Part III leaves us with

Part III makes return more precise by refusing to call every reversal the same thing. Topological orientation, thermodynamic direction, clock time, causal order and remembered path are different structures. A successful theory must state which one changes and which one remains.

The Part's deepest proposal is that restoration of a state need not erase the history of the route. That possibility is mathematically familiar in some settings and physically demanding in others. TTI's inherited residue remains a conjecture until a carrier, conservation rule and observable consequence are supplied.
