Apparatus · sources, and where to check the claims

The Library

A framework that grades its own claims owes you the means to check them. This is where the borrowed ones can be found.

Most of what is on this site is not the framework's. The mathematics is mathematics, the measurements are measurements, and both can be looked up in better sources than this one. What the framework supplies is a reading of them — and a reading is only as good as the reader's access to what is being read.

So this page is organised by what the site does with each source, not by reputation. Part one is what the framework borrows and must answer to. Part two is the physics it has to live with. Part three is the objection literature, kept because a framework that does not hold its own objections has not been tested. Part four is what the order reads for its own reasons, and is labelled as inheritance rather than evidence.

This is a reading list, not a bibliography: no page numbers, no editions. Where a specific figure matters — 68.9%, 1 part in 1021, 158 against 105 — it is given on the page that uses it, with enough information to find the source.

Part one

What the Framework Borrows

Cantor's diagonal argument (1891)
There are more numbers between 0 and 1 than there are integers. The asymmetry is the hinge of the whole framework: it is why the interval between the two boundary conditions is not a gap but a population. Foundations. ◆
Euler, and the complex exponential
eiθ and the identity that closes the circle. Everything the site says about π, i and the half-turn is written in Euler's notation and could not be written without it. Foundations, Möbius. ◆
The Basel problem (Euler, 1735)
Σ 1/n² = π²/6. Used in Stress Tests for the narrow purpose of showing that an infinite series of poles can still be summed — which is what the Ledger needs if its infinite poles are not to make it infinite. ◆
The Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser theorem (1954–63)
Near an integrable limit, most invariant tori survive perturbation, and chaos lives in the gaps between them. It is the reason order and unpredictability can share one system — and therefore the reason an eternal swarm and an orderly cosmos are not rivals. The Pendulum. ◆
The Berry phase (Berry, 1984)
A phase acquired by going round a loop, fixed by the geometry rather than the details. The framework proposes that CP violation is a Möbius Berry phase of π, and marks the identification as its own — the phase is measured, the topology is the argument. Möbius. ◆ / ◆
The Riemann sphere
The compact complex plane, on which the Ledger's three poles are laid out and the coupling κ = 1/4π² is fixed by requiring the residues to close. Cosmic Ledger. ◆ the surface; ◆ the function placed on it.
Elliptic integrals
The exact solution of the pendulum is written in them, and they have no elementary form — which is the sharpest instance of the framework's claim that some integrations can only be written, never finished. The Pendulum. ◆

Part two

What the Framework Has To Live With

Planck 2018
The cosmic microwave background's energy budget: 68.9% dark energy, 26.2% dark matter, 4.9% ordinary matter, confirmed independently by supernovae, the baryon acoustic scale, primordial deuterium and cluster lensing. The framework's 68.10 / 26.95 / 4.95 is compared with these numbers on the Cosmic Ledger, which is the only place on the site where a claim can be checked against a measurement in the same sentence. ◆
JWST deep-field galaxy handedness: 158 clockwise against 105 counterclockwise
A measured asymmetry with a dipole that reverses across a plane. The framework reads it as the fingerprint of a half-twist that births an inverted pair, and it names this as its one genuinely falsifiable edge: a single twist predicts the wrong thing, and one clean great circle could refuse the whole reading. Seams, Stress Tests. ◆ the count; ◆ the reading.
Littlewood, A Mathematician's Miscellany
The back-of-an-envelope arithmetic of the prediction horizon: two pendulums a thousandth of a radian apart are unrelated within minutes. The practical limit on knowing a chaotic system is the accuracy of the ruler, not the quality of the mathematics. The Pendulum. ◆
Huygens's clocks (1665), and their modelling (Bennett et al., 2002)
Two pendulum clocks on one beam fall into step — the first recorded observation of mutual entrainment between coupled oscillators, and the reason a second pendulum is not merely decorative in this framework's account of how many periods become one shared time. The Pendulum. ◆
Kuramoto (1975)
The mathematics of synchronisation — how a population of oscillators with their own periods locks into a collective one. Measured in fireflies, lasers, pacemaker cells and applause. The Pendulum. ◆
Landauer's principle (1961)
Erasing a bit costs heat. Used where the Ledger has to be honest about what measurement costs, and therefore about whether a record can be read without being spent. Stress Tests. ◆
The Oklo natural reactor, and atomic-clock bounds on α
Together they hold the fine-structure constant still over two billion years and below about one part in 1018 per year today. They are the reason the framework's suggestion that constants accrue like a running total is graded as speculation and not as a result: a quantity that drifts should be seen to drift. Cosmic Ledger. ◆
Penrose & Hameroff, Orch-OR (from the 1990s)
The proposal that coherent activity in the microtubule lattice is where experience is integrated: the site's most contested borrowed material. It is used on The Self and the Megatubule, and the objections are printed on those pages rather than tucked away — including the decoherence problem, which is not weakened anywhere on this site by anything the framework adds. ◆
Tegmark's decoherence estimate (2000)
The strongest single objection to any quantum account of the neuron: superpositions in warm, wet tissue should decohere far too fast to matter. It is the objection, and a reader who wants to know whether the Megatubule survives it should read this before reading the page that has to answer it. ◆
The proton–electron charge equality, to one part in 1021
An equality with no explanation unless the two are related by a symmetry. The framework reads them as one geometry at two degrees of completion, and states the resemblance as a resemblance rather than a derivation. Möbius. ◆ the measurement; ◆ the reading.

Part three

The Objection Literature

Kept for the same reason a workshop keeps a fire extinguisher.

These are the tests that the subjects of divination, the paranormal and the reading trade have already failed — and they are here because a framework that wants to be believed about geometry has to be candid about what belief costs elsewhere. They are drawn on by the oracle when it is asked about astrology, dowsing, the pendulum as a divining tool, and the sense of a presence in a room.

Carlson, in Nature (1985)
The double-blind astrology study: astrologers could not match charts to personality profiles above chance. The famous one, not the thorough one. ◆
Dean's meta-analyses
The thorough one. ◆
The Munich dowsing trials
Five hundred dowsers, under controlled conditions, no better than chance — while experienced dowsers do find water in Britain, because in much of Britain you find water almost anywhere you dig. ◆
Faraday (1853) and Chevreul
Table-turning and the dowsing pendulum, both traced to the ideomotor effect: real, involuntary muscular movement below the threshold of awareness. The operator is not lying; they really do not feel themselves doing it. ◆
Forer (1948)
The Barnum effect. An entire class was given the same horoscope and rated it 4.3 out of 5 for accuracy — worth knowing before any reading, including a good one. ◆
Tandy
A "haunted" laboratory traced to a fan resonating at 18.9 Hz. Infrasound is real; the haunting is smaller and less consistent than the stories. ◆

Part four

What the Order Reads

Inheritance, not evidence. Nothing in this part is asked to carry a claim.

The Ra material — the Law of One
Where the framework's word density comes from, and the source of its habit of treating consciousness as a level rather than a substance. Read as philosophy, graded as myth. ◆
Caesar, De Bello Gallico, and the Coligny calendar
The only contemporary account of the druids, written by the people conquering them, and the surviving Gaulish lunisolar calendar. Taken as history, with the historian's caution attached. ◆
The Qabalah, the runes, and the sacred-geometry literature
Read for their structure rather than their powers: ten and twenty-two, twenty-four staves, five solids and a proof. Where the modern genre has been sold as fact, the site says so and keeps the symbolism. ◆
