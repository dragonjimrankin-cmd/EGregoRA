Part VII — Stress Tests · Chapters 17 to 20

Where It Might Break

A framework is worth what its worst chapter is worth. This is that chapter, and in the Unified Edition it is written by the author against his own work — including one objection he concedes outright.

Chapter 17

The Infinite Poles, and the Basel Rescue

The measurement function does not have three poles. It has infinitely many: sin(πz) vanishes at every integer, and the ledger only works if the residues at all the distant ones behave.

The critical question is whether the sum over those infinite poles converges. If it diverges, the global contour integral is undefined and the whole Ledger collapses. The residues decay as 1/n², because the denominator's derivative at each distant root grows exponentially while the numerator stays bounded — and the sum of 1/n² is Euler's Basel series, which converges to π²/6. ◆ The imaginary parts cancel as complex-conjugate pairs; the real parts sum to a finite correction proportional to π²/6.

The cost, stated plainly: the rescue is real but it dilutes. Those distant residues are not zero, so the three headline percentages are the leading terms of a sum with an infinite tail, not the whole of it. Any claim that the budget is exactly 68.10 / 26.95 / 4.95 has to answer for the tail. ◆

Grading: ◆ established · ◆ contested · ◆ vision.

Chapter 18 — the one the framework loses

The Entropy Objection

The framework describes the cosmic energy budget as the residues of a meromorphic function with fixed poles. Those poles are determined by the function's definition: they do not move, interact or rearrange. In statistical mechanics, entropy counts the distinguishable ways a system can be configured while keeping its macroscopic properties — and a system with one possible configuration has zero entropy. Zero entropy means no thermodynamic arrow, no irreversibility, no explanation for why ice melts or stars burn or memory runs forward.

Real physics has entropy, and a great deal of it. A black hole carries entropy proportional to its horizon area, S = kBA / 4ℓP²; the microwave background encodes roughly 10⁹ kB per baryon. ◆

The honest diagnosis, in the author's own words: the static-pole picture, taken at face value, has no entropy and therefore no thermodynamics. This is a genuine failure — not a failure of vision, but a failure of the framework's current mathematical machinery to capture a feature the universe unarguably possesses.

Since written, this objection has been pressed further and the news is worse. The framework's answer to the arrow — that the asymmetry between the two infinities tilts time — has now been simulated at the toy integrator below, and it does not work: the arrow appears only when a uniform measure over states is assumed alongside the asymmetry, and vanishes when the measure is changed with every cardinality left intact.

Three survival routes are sketched — letting the poles acquire internal structure, letting χ carry microstates, or treating the residues as thermodynamic averages rather than exact values — and each is costed. None is offered as a solution. The site keeps that wording, because a cosmology that announces its own failing in a numbered chapter has earned the right not to have it paraphrased away.

Chapter 19 — Flat rotation curves

Galaxies rotate too fast at their edges for the visible mass to hold them together: the curve goes flat where Keplerian falloff is expected. ◆ The framework puts a χ-field halo in the place conventional models put a dark-matter halo — partially integrated matter, gravitating but dark, distributed so that the enclosed mass grows roughly linearly with radius.

What this requires is spelt out rather than waved at: a specific radial profile for χ, and a reason that profile should arise rather than being fitted galaxy by galaxy. Until the profile is derived, this reproduces dark matter's success without yet improving on it. ◆

Chapter 20 — Beyond the strip

If one half-twist gives a Möbius strip, what gives two? The chapter walks out to the Klein bottle — a closed non-orientable surface with no boundary at all — and then to Dyck's surface and higher genus, asking whether the universe's topology might be non-orientable on the largest scale.

This is the one place the framework touches data it did not choose: matched-circle and antipodal-correlation searches in the CMB constrain such topologies. ◆ No detection has been made. The chapter treats that as a boundary on the idea rather than as support for it.

Carried over from Part II

The Microtubule Question, Stated Fairly

The framework sites conscious integration in the microtubule lattice, following Penrose–Hameroff. That proposal is neither crankery nor consensus, and the site refuses to present it as either.

  • Against: Tegmark's 2000 decoherence estimate put coherence times near 10−13 s — far too short. ◆
  • In reply: Hagan, Hameroff and Tuszyński recomputed it at ~10−4 s in 2002. The disagreement is about the model, not the arithmetic. ◆
  • Newer, and genuinely interesting: cooperative ultraviolet superradiance has been predicted and measured in mega-networks of tryptophan in cytoskeletal architectures (Babcock et al., 2024); the microtubule stabiliser epothilone B measurably delays anaesthetic loss of righting in rats (Khan et al., eNeuro, 2024). ◆
  • The logical gap, named: “we know no classical mechanism, therefore quantum” is affirming the consequent. Evidence that anaesthetics act on microtubules is evidence about anaesthetics, not about consciousness being quantum.

— the test the framework volunteers for

The Galaxy Handedness Test, With Its Outcomes Written in Advance

Everything else on this page is a challenge brought against the framework. This one it brings against itself, and it is the only place in the work where the sky is given a clean opportunity to say no.

The claim under test comes from the two-being reading of the seam: if the half-twist births an inverted pair rather than one oriented thing, there exists a plane across which observed galaxy handedness reverses. The prediction is not the size of the asymmetry — the framework has no amplitude and says so — but its shape. A genuine seam demands an orientation field that is cleanly odd about a single great circle:

A(−n̂) = −A(n̂)

The rival explanation, and currently the favourite, is pedestrian: galaxies turning against the Milky Way's motion are Doppler-brightened, enter a magnitude-limited sample preferentially, and produce an excess centred on our Galactic pole that worsens with faintness. That account predicts an excess tied to our motion vector and apparent magnitude — which need not be perfectly antisymmetric and need not resolve to one circle. ◆

The three outcomes, agreed to before the data

What survives bias-modellingVerdict
A single, clean, perfectly odd dipole A real point for the seam, against pure selection.
A dipole, but not aligned with our Galactic pole Strongly helps the seam: a cosmic-frame feature cannot be an artefact of our vantage.
The signal dissolves into the Milky-Way frame The two beings were a beautiful picture and nothing more. Withdrawn.

The framework's own expectation runs partly against the current data. A primordial seam ought to be a cosmic-frame structure, and the reported axis sits close to our own pole. That is a point against, it is recorded here rather than in a footnote, and no attempt is made to explain it away.

— the list the framework keeps against itself

Twelve More Ways to Break This

A stress test is only worth printing if the failure condition is written before the result is known. Each of these says what would have to happen for the framework to be wrong, and two of them have already been run — one of them against us.

Two that have already been run, in this repository

Experiment 1 — is 666 a fact about the world or about base ten?

The spiral chapter leans on 666 being the thirty-sixth triangular number and a repdigit: three identical figures. If that conjunction is common across bases, it is a fact about notation and the chapter should stop citing it. So we counted. For every base from 2 to 36 we found every triangular number up to 1012 whose representation is three or more identical non-zero digits.

The result does not flatter us. Fifteen of the thirty-five bases have at least one. Base ten has exactly one — 666 — while base nine has eleven (111, 1111, 11111, 111111 and more, all triangular) and base twenty-five has seven. Base seventeen produces a repdigit triangular with ten identical digits. ◆

On this measure base ten is not rich. It is one of the sparser bases that manage the trick at all, and 666 is its only instance. The honest conclusion is that the repdigit property of 666 carries no weight, and the spiral chapter should rest on 666 = T36 = 36·37/2 and the quark count alone — which is base-independent and survives intact.

Experiment 2 — is the Pisano period of ten distinguished?

Here the answer goes the other way, modestly. The Fibonacci sequence modulo n repeats with period π(n), and π(10) = 60. Computing π(n) for every n up to 40, the ratio π(n)/n is maximised at n = 10, where it reaches exactly 6 — higher than every other base in the range. That is not a coincidence of our search: it is a known theorem that π(n) ≤ 6n with equality exactly when n = 2·5k, and we confirmed it numerically at n = 10, 50 and 250. ◆

So base ten attains the maximum possible Fibonacci period for its size, and it is the smallest base that does. That is a real distinction and it is a narrow one: the property belongs to the family 10, 50, 250, and the framework may claim only that ten is the first member, not that ten is unique.

— experiment three, run rather than proposed

The Lyapunov Exponent, Computed

This page demanded a number: chaos is a defined property with a measurable exponent, and the framework does not get to call its pendulum chaotic without computing one. Here it is, with its controls, and with the thing it found that nobody was looking for.

Method

The system is the driven damped pendulum, which is what the thesis sentence invokes:

θ̈ + q θ̇ + sin θ = A cos(ωt)

The trajectory and its tangent-space equations are integrated together with fourth-order Runge–Kutta, the tangent vector renormalised at fixed intervals and the logarithms accumulated — the standard variational method. Transients are discarded. The code is in the repository as scripts/tti-lyapunov.py and the raw output in ops/tti-lyapunov.json, so anyone can check the arithmetic rather than take it on trust.

Result

At the standard parameters q = 0.5, A = 1.2, ω = 2/3, the largest Lyapunov exponent is λ = +0.1286 per unit time. Positive, and not marginally. ◆

Two controls say the number means what it should. With the drive weakened to A = 0.5, and again with the drive switched off entirely, the exponent comes out at exactly -0.250 — which is −q/2, the value forced by the damping for any motion that settles. The method returns the right answer when the answer is known. ◆

So the word survives. The pendulum the framework invokes is chaotic in the technical sense: deterministic, governed by one line, and separating nearby states exponentially. The e-folding time is 7.8 time units, so ten orders of magnitude of initial precision buys about 179 time units of prediction — roughly 19 drive cycles, and then nothing. That is the quantitative content of “exact in its law, incomputable in its future”.

What the sweep found, which the framework did not want

Computing λ at one parameter set proves one parameter set. So the exponent was swept across drive amplitudes from 0.00 to 1.60 in steps of 0.025, and the result is the plate above.

Chaos is not generic. It occupies two narrow bands — roughly A = 1.15 to 1.27, and again near A = 1.50 — with the exponent peaking at λ = +0.1583 at A = 1.18. Between and beyond those bands the same equation is periodic, sometimes sharply so: at A = 1.30 and A = 1.35 the exponent returns to the fully contracted value of −0.25. ◆

This is a new debt and it was created by the framework's own experiment, which is the point of running them. The thesis says the universe swings like a pendulum that cannot settle, and the stronger reading of that sentence used to be that the swing is chaotic. The computation says a driven pendulum is out of control only inside particular windows of its parameters, and is otherwise as regular as a metronome. So the framework now owes an account of why its parameters would sit inside a chaotic band rather than in one of the much wider periodic regions — and it does not have one. Saying the cosmos is chaotic because pendulums are chaotic is no longer available: most pendulums, most of the time, are not.

The repair, if there is one, is that a cosmic system is undamped, and an undamped system cannot settle into the attracting cycles that produce the negative exponents here. That was written as plausible and uncomputed. It is computed in the next section.

Experiment four — the undamped case, which the last section demanded

The repair offered above was that a cosmic system has no outside to lose energy to, so it cannot settle into the attracting cycles that produce the negative exponents — and that this was plausible but uncomputed. It has now been computed: the same sweep with the damping set to zero, amplitudes from 0 to 1.6.

The result is unambiguous. The undamped exponent is never negative at any amplitude tested — the minimum over the whole sweep is +0.00001, which is zero to within the integration error. The periodic windows are gone. ◆

And this is not a numerical accident; it could not have come out otherwise. An undamped system preserves phase-space area, so by Liouville's theorem its exponents must sum to zero, which in two dimensions forces λ₁ = −λ₂ and therefore λ₁ ≥ 0. A negative largest exponent is mathematically impossible without dissipation. The computation agrees with the theorem, which is the correct order of events. ◆

Above A ≈ 0.40 the exponent sits around +0.16 across the entire range, with no windows and no returns to regularity — chaos is generic once the drive is strong enough. Below that the exponent is zero rather than negative: the motion is quasi-periodic, wandering on an intact torus, still never settling but perfectly predictable.

So the debt the sweep created is paid, and the two halves of the thesis sentence come apart cleanly in the process. Cannot settle is now the stronger claim: it holds at every amplitude, and it holds by theorem rather than by computation. Out of control is the weaker one: it holds above a threshold and fails below it, where the motion is unending and entirely foreseeable.

Stated against ourselves, because the repair should not be allowed to buy more than it paid for: all of this is a two-dimensional driven pendulum, and Liouville's theorem applies to the universe only if the universe is Hamiltonian, which is exactly the sort of thing a framework should not help itself to. What has been shown is that the framework's own chosen system behaves as the framework said it would once the dissipation is removed. That was in doubt an hour ago and it is not in doubt now.

— experiment five, and the framework loses this one

The Toy Integrator

The task this page set was exact: build the smallest simulation containing two opposed infinities and see whether an arrow of time appears without one being inserted by hand. It was built. The answer is no, and the reason is more useful than the answer.

Three models, each answering one question

The code is scripts/tti-toy-integrator.py and the output ops/tti-toy-integrator.json. Entropy production is measured as the Kullback–Leibler divergence per step between the forward path distribution and its time reverse, which is zero exactly when the dynamics is statistically reversible — the standard definition, not one invented here.

  • A. The baker's map. The two infinities as stretching against folding: doubling in one direction, halving into the interval in the other. Exactly invertible and area-preserving, so any arrow found in it must be an artefact. Twenty thousand points start in a small blob and are run forwards and backwards.
  • B. The branching walk. The framework's asymmetry stated as plainly as it can be: from any level there are b ways down, because the interval subdivides, and one way up, because counting proceeds by ones. Choose uniformly among the available states.
  • C. The same tree, one thing changed. Choose uniformly among the available directions instead. Same state space, same asymmetry in the number of states, a different and equally arbitrary measure.

What came out

Model A found no arrow, and found it loudly. Coarse-grained entropy rose 8.323 bits running forwards and 8.321 bits running backwards, with the two directions differing by 0.002 bits — noise. A blob spreads whichever way you run the clock. The rise is a fact about starting small and about looking through a grid, not a fact about a direction in the dynamics. ◆

Model B found a strong arrow. With b = 2 the walk drifts downward at +0.331 per step and produces 0.231 nats of entropy per step; by b = 10 that is +0.817 per step and 1.884 nats. The drift follows (b−1)/(b+1) and the production ((b−1)/(b+1))·ln b exactly. More room downward, so the walk goes down. Exactly what the framework predicts.

And Model C killed it. Same tree, same cardinalities, same “more room below” — measure swapped from states to directions, and the drift falls to +0.0006 per step with entropy production of exactly zero, at every branching ratio. ◆

The verdict, stated plainly

The asymmetry between the two infinities does not, by itself, produce a direction of time. What produces the direction in Model B is not the fact that there are more states below; it is the decision to sample states uniformly. Change that decision and the arrow vanishes while every cardinality in the model stays exactly where it was.

So the step the framework takes on the foundations page — from “there is more room in that direction” to “things therefore tend that way” — is not merely unproven. It is false as stated, and the smallest honest simulation of it says so. The missing ingredient is a measure, and the framework has never supplied one.

Which sharpens the entropy objection rather than answering it, and sharpens it in a particular direction. “Sample uniformly over states” is not a neutral choice: it is the postulate of equal a priori probabilities, the assumption statistical mechanics already makes and from which the second law already follows. Grant it, and the framework's arrow appears — but it is then the thermodynamic arrow wearing different words, and the framework has derived nothing that was not already there. Refuse it, and there is no arrow at all. ◆

What the framework may still say, and all it may say until it has more: that the asymmetry of the two infinities makes the state space the right shape for an arrow to live in, and that something else must choose the measure. That is a weaker claim than the one printed on the foundations page, and the foundations page has been marked accordingly rather than quietly left as it was.

What would change this result

  • Derive the measure. If a uniform measure over states can be shown to follow from the structure rather than be assumed alongside it, Model B becomes an argument instead of a restatement. Nothing in the framework currently does this.
  • Find an arrow in a reversible model. Model A is the fair test and it came back empty. A variant with two infinities and no inserted measure that nevertheless distinguishes the two time directions would overturn everything in this section.
  • Show the measure is forced by the cardinalities. The most interesting possibility, and the hardest: that an uncountable set of states below and a countable set above make “uniform over directions” incoherent. The simulation cannot settle this, because it is finite. A proof could.

Experiment run 14 steps, 20,000 points, seed fixed in the script so the numbers above reproduce exactly.

Ten proposed, with kill conditions attached

TestWhat it probesWhat kills the framework
Matched circles in the sky A twisted or non-trivially connected cosmos should put the same circle of sky in two places. The Cornish–Spergel–Starkman search is the standard method. Searches have come back empty. The megatubule must say at what scale it evades them, or concede that the throat is unobservable in principle — which costs it the Cold Spot as evidence.
Cosmic birefringence A handed universe should rotate the plane of CMB polarisation. A non-zero angle of about 0.3 degrees has been reported and is not yet confirmed. The Möbius reading must pre-register a sign. If LiteBIRD measures the angle and the sign is wrong, the chapter is finished.
Drift in the constants If constants are running totals of residue, they should accrue. Atomic clocks already hold the fine-structure drift below one part in 1018 per year and Oklo holds it flat for two billion years. The ledger must predict a drift smaller than that and say how much smaller, or admit the reading is unfalsifiable.
The radial acceleration relation χ proportional to 1/r gives flat rotation curves. Real galaxies obey a tighter law than that. Fit the SPARC sample. If the χ-field cannot reproduce the observed scatter and the Tully–Fisher slope of four, flat curves were never the hard part and the model fails where it matters.
Proton decay One thing at two phases implies a conversion. Hyper-Kamiokande reaching about 7.8 × 1034 years with nothing seen weakens this and every grand unified theory alike.
Flicker fusion against metabolic rate If the grain is issued by thermodynamics, the length of a perceptual moment should scale with metabolism across species. Critical flicker-fusion data already exist. No scaling, or scaling with body size rather than metabolic rate, and the Landauer argument loses its one biological handle.
Reconsolidation, quantified The claim is that awareness is paid for out of memory. That is a rate, not a mood. Degradation on recall that does not scale with the depth of attention paid. The qualitative effect is established; the proportionality is the framework's and is unmeasured.
A toy integrator Build the smallest simulation with two opposed infinities and see whether an arrow appears without one being inserted by hand. Run. No arrow emerged in the reversible model, and the arrow in the asymmetric one emerged only because the measure put it there. The entropy objection is answered in the negative by the framework's own machinery, at Experiment five.
An actual Lyapunov exponent The thesis calls the swing chaotic. Chaos is a defined property with a number attached. No computable exponent means the word must be dropped. Borrowing the glamour of chaos without the measurement is exactly the failure this page exists to catch.
Pre-registered galaxy spins in Euclid The handedness claim, done properly: method, sample and sign lodged before the data. Any result at all, provided the prediction was filed first. An unregistered reanalysis after the fact is worth nothing and the framework will not count it.

Two of these the framework could run itself with no telescope and no funding — the toy integrator and the Lyapunov exponent — and both have now been run, on this page. One went the framework's way and one did not. The other eight depend on instruments, and four of them on instruments already collecting.

Closing Stance

Three of these four chapters strengthen the framework by bounding it. One of them — entropy — is a loss, recorded as a loss. A reader deciding what to make of the whole volume could reasonably start here, read Chapter 18 first, and judge everything else by whether the rest of the work is written with the same honesty.
