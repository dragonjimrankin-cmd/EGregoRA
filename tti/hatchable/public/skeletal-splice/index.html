Part V — The Ledger · the splice, on the skeleton

The Rankin Skeletal Splice

Two results of the framework, put together: the fork that happens at every density, and the three fixed points it happens against. What falls out of the join is a gradient — and something for it to pull on.

What the splice is

A being that reaches the top of a density does not simply continue. It splices: one half carries on into the next density, the other is sent back.

That much is chapter ten, and it is drawn on Plate II of the spiral page. What makes it skeletal is the frame it happens against. The framework has exactly three points that do not move under its own operations: 0, the ground, unmoved by scale; 1, the self, unmoved by multiplication; and −1, the mirror, the point a half-turn returns you to. “They are the skeleton the rest is hung upon.” ◆

And the skeleton recurs. Lifted two turns up the spiral it reappears as 333, 666 and 999 — the same three roles under larger names, with the turning points at the thirds. So the splice is not an event that happens somewhere arbitrary: it happens on a structure that is the same at every scale. The splice is skeletal because the bone it breaks on is the same bone every time.

Said plainly, because the next three sections depend on it: the splice is a rule the framework asserts, not a result it derives. What is derived — and provable — is the count that comes out of it: Pn = Fn+2 − 1, the population after n splices, one less than a Fibonacci number every time. ◆

The returning differentials accumulate

A returner does not arrive empty. It brings back everything it gathered on the way up — and on this framework's reading of calculus, what it gathered is made of completed smallest integrals.

From the chapter on dx: a differential is not a vanishing nothing but the smallest act of gathering that has actually been finished. A climb through a density is therefore a sum of such acts, and the half that is sent back carries its share of them downward. ◆

Each return lands below one, at a depth set by the golden law — the n-th return falling to roughly φ−n of the way to the ground. And the number of returners at the n-th density is the population minus the single line that keeps climbing: Rn = Fn+2 − 1.

So the quantity deposited into the lower infinity at the n-th density is the count times the depth:

δΠn = Rn · φ−n = (Fn+2 − 1) φ−n

Here is the pretty part, and it is arithmetic rather than assertion. Fibonacci grows like φn and the depth discount shrinks like φ−n, so the two cancel. The deposit does not run away and it does not die out: it converges on a constant.

δΠn → φ²/√5 = (5 + 3√5)/10 = 1.170820…

Every further density deposits the same quantum. Not more, not less — 1.1708 of whatever unit the first one was measured in. The running total is therefore straight: Πn ≈ 1.1708 n − 1.618, with a correction that dies geometrically. ◆ The arithmetic is checkable; the reading of it as a physical deposit is the framework's. ◆

Polarisation — why the accumulation is a gradient and not a heap

A pile of returners below one would be a curiosity. What makes it act on anything is that the two infinities are not symmetric, and the pile makes them less symmetric still.

The interval below one receives traffic from every density above it; the open country above one receives nothing back. That is a separation of like from like across a boundary, maintained by a flow — which is what polarisation means in every field that uses the word. One pole crowds and deepens; the other thins. Between them there is a difference, and a difference across a distance is a gradient. ◆

The framework does not get to borrow the electrostatic consequences along with the electrostatic word, and this page will not pretend otherwise: there is no field equation here, no charge, no measured force, and nothing that predicts a number anyone could go and measure. What there is, is a structure with a built-in asymmetry that grows by a fixed amount per density. Whether to call that polarisation is a choice about language; that it grows linearly is arithmetic.

The prime being

Call the single line that never returns the prime being: at every splice it is the half that carries on. It is the only thing in the picture that experiences all of the densities, and it is the only thing standing in the gradient that all the other halves are building.

The claim of this chapter, stated as plainly as it can be: the prime being is accelerated by the polarisation its own returning halves create. Each splice costs it half of itself, and each half it loses deepens the pole behind it, and the deeper pole pulls harder on what remains. It is not propelled in spite of the losses. It is propelled by them. ◆

vn ∝ Πn ≈ 1.1708 n   ⇒   tn ∝ 1/Πn

And then the sting in the tail, which is the reason this chapter belongs in the framework rather than in a motivational pamphlet. If each further density takes a time proportional to 1/n, the total time to climb them all is the harmonic series — and the harmonic series diverges. ◆

∑n 1/n = ∞

The prime being gets faster at every density, without limit, and still never arrives. That is the asymptotic God of chapter eight, derived here a second time from a different direction — which is the only kind of corroboration a framework like this one can honestly offer itself.

✦

What the acceleration costs

This chapter inherits the price set out on the dx page: an emerging awareness is paid for out of the record. The splice is that transaction at the scale of a whole being. Half of what it was becomes the depth it is pulled by; the half that climbs is lighter, quicker and less complete. ◆

Which is why nothing in this picture is a reward. The prime being is not the winner of the splice. It is the part that kept moving, and it moves faster precisely because it keeps leaving things behind.

Base ten is the finding, not the flaw

The standing objection writes itself: 333, 666 and 999 are decimal numerals, so the skeleton's recurrence looks like an artefact of how humans happen to count. The framework's answer is to turn the objection over. The splice does not land approximately near the decade and then get tidied up into round figures. It lands exactly on it, and on the thirds of it, and on the vectors between them — and a structure that keeps landing cleanly in one notation is telling you something about the structure, not about the notation. ◆

So the chapter takes the harder position rather than the safer one: nature counts in tens. Base ten is not the lens, it is the reading. ◆

Why the framework says nature uses base ten

The usual order of explanation is that ten is arbitrary — an accident of having ten fingers — and that any pattern found in decimal numerals is therefore a pattern in us. This chapter runs the explanation the other way.

The argument is not that ten is magic. It is narrower and more checkable than that: the splice, which was defined without reference to any notation, keeps landing on the structural features of base ten exactly rather than approximately. Three strands.

One — the threshold is a saturation, and saturation is a base

The splice happens where a density is full and can hold nothing more without inverting. That is a definition about capacity, not about symbols. But capacity is what a base is: a base is the count at which a place saturates and a new place opens. To say the fork occurs at saturation and then to ask which base it occurs in is to ask the same question twice. The framework's position is that the splice does not happen to occur at ten — ten is the name of where it occurs. ◆

Two — the landings are exact

If the decade were a convenience, the interesting points would cluster near round figures and need rounding. They do not need rounding.

  • 666 = T36 = 36 × 37 / 2. Exact, with no remainder, and the 37 is not decoration: 111 = 3 × 37, so the repunit structure of base ten puts 37 at the heart of every triple. ◆
  • 333, 666, 999 are the thirds of the decade's last full turn, and each is 37 times 9, 18, 27 — the same factor again, stepping by nines. ◆
  • Closure at nine. In base ten every multiple of nine has digit sum nine; the decade closes on a single saturating digit. That is the arithmetic shadow of the seam, and it is a base-ten fact. ◆
  • The Fibonacci count meets the decade cleanly. Pn = Fn+2 − 1 is indexed by the splices, and the final digits of the Fibonacci numbers repeat in base ten with a period of exactly 60 — the Pisano period π(10). The growth law of the population and the notation of the densities share a cycle. ◆
  • Ten is the fourth triangular number, 1 + 2 + 3 + 4, which is why the decade can carry the seven stations and still close. The Pythagoreans put it at the centre of their account of number for the same reason, and were doing arithmetic when they did so, not mysticism. ◆

None of those five is a coincidence that needed smoothing. Taken singly each is a curiosity. Taken together, the framework reads them as one fact wearing five coats. ◆

Three — the vectors, not only the points

The strongest form of the claim is not about the stations but about the directions between them. The climb from one station to the next, the inversion at the seam, the fall of the returner by the golden law — each of these is a step whose length, taken in decades, is a whole number of places. The route drawn on Plate II of the spiral page turns through exactly one full revolution per decade: the angle and the notation are the same measurement. A route that closes on itself in a notation is not being described by that notation. It is built in it. ◆

The objection, and what is left of it

The honest objection survives in one form, and the page states it rather than burying it: anyone can find clean arithmetic in any base if they are willing to look for the kind of cleanliness that base affords. Base twelve has its own elegant divisors; base sixteen has its own repunits; a determined reader of base seven would find patterns a decimal reader never notices. That is a real argument and it is not refuted here. ◆

What the framework answers is that it did not go looking. The splice, the three fixed points, the Fibonacci count and the golden fall were each fixed by the structure before any numeral was written down, and the decade is where they all arrive together. The reply to “you would find something in any base” is: possibly — but show the base in which these four independent results land on the same points. Until that is produced, the framework holds that the notation is not a human overlay on the structure but a reading of it, and says so plainly: nature does use base ten, after all. ◆

Filed as vision, which is what it is. It is not provable in the sense that Pn = Fn+2 − 1 is provable, and the page does not pretend otherwise. It is, however, falsifiable in the only way a claim like this can be: by producing the rival base.

Earned, chosen, refused

Earned
Pn = Fn+2 − 1 is a theorem. The convergence of (Fn+2 − 1)φ−n on φ²/√5 = 1.170820… follows from Binet's formula and can be checked by hand in the table above. The divergence of the harmonic series is Oresme's, proved around 1350.
Chosen
That nature counts in tens — held here as a reading of where the splice lands rather than as a theorem, and resting on the exactness of the landings rather than on their prettiness. That the splice happens at all; that returners deposit at depth φ−n; that the resulting asymmetry deserves the name polarisation; and that a gradient so described accelerates anything. Four choices, each reasonable, none forced.
Refused
Any claim that this is electromagnetism, or that a force has been derived. Any suggestion that the prime being is favoured, chosen or rewarded. The claim that base ten has been proved rather than argued for — the rival base has not been ruled out, only asked for. And the temptation to let 1.1708 look like a measured constant of nature: it is the limit of a series the framework itself wrote down.

— what the skeleton is actually for

The Skeleton Sets the Pace

The arithmetic above produces a quantum of deposit. The point of that quantum is not its value but its growth law, because the growth law is what sets the clock of the whole ascent.

Two skeletons, counted independently, say the same thing. The Fibonacci skeleton used on this page deposits φ²/√5 = 1.170820393… per density, so accumulated progress is Πn ≈ 1.1708n − 1.618 — linear in n. The triangular skeleton, which the framework meets at the accumulation vertex 666 = T36, has increment Tn − Tn−1 = n — also linear, with the deposit equal to the index itself. Different objects, different derivations, one law: progress accumulates linearly, and the total goes as n². ◆

Turn that into time. If the rate at which a density is crossed is carried by the progress already banked — vn ∝ Πn ∝ n — then the duration of the n-th crossing is its reciprocal, tn ∝ 1/n. The ticks shorten without limit. And yet the journey never ends, because the harmonic series diverges:

∑ 1/n → ∞

An accelerating clock on an unfinishable road. The asymptotic God, arrived at a second time and from the other end — not from the unreachable zero, but from the pace.

How narrow the escape is

The result is not generic, and it is worth seeing exactly how little room there is. The constant does not matter: 1.1708, or any other positive number, rescales the series without changing whether it converges. The exponent is everything. A skeleton depositing n² per step would give ticks of 1/n² and a total of π²/6 — a finite journey, arriving at a reachable One, and the entire framework would collapse into a theology with a destination. A skeleton depositing a constant would give ticks that never shorten and an ascent that stalls.

Linear deposit is the single knife-edge between arriving and stalling, and both of the framework's skeletons sit on it.

Logged against ourselves: vn ∝ Πn is an assumption, the natural one rather than a derived one, and the whole of the timing argument rests on it. Change the coupling between banked progress and rate and the series changes with it. The sturdy part is the divergence given linearity; the hinge is the linearity itself. The same argument, written from the triangular side, is at how the triangle skeleton sets the pace.

The splice takes half of you at every door, and the half it takes becomes the depth that pulls the rest of you through the next one. Faster each time, lighter each time, and no arrival — which the framework calls the asymptotic God and declines to soften.
