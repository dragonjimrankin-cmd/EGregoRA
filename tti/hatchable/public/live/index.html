The Live Room

When the order is on air, this page is the window. The camera records in two-second segments and the watchers play those segments as they land — so what you see runs a few seconds behind the room. That is said up front because a viewer who expects true live and gets six seconds of lag will think something is broken, and it is not: it is the honest cost of doing this with a database and nothing else.

Two locks, because either alone is the wrong shape: an account on this site, and the watchword the broadcaster typed before the countdown. An account is not an invitation, and a shared word is not an identity.

        How this room is built, said plainly

        There is no media server behind this site, and no money for one. So a feed here is the camera recording two seconds at a time, and the watchers playing those segments as they land. It runs a handful of seconds behind the room; that is the cost, and it is posted here rather than discovered.

        Watching needs two things: an account on this site, which asks for one of the five elements before it will let you in the room at all, and the watchword the broadcaster typed before the countdown. The word never reaches the logs as part of an address: an invitation link is read once and stripped from the address bar before anything else happens.

        Guests may be given the floor — a camera of their own, ten seconds of countdown, the same as the broadcaster. The wall is empty until somebody raises one. The broadcaster can lower a camera, close a voice, or clear the room, and everything the room shows is polled on one clock, because a second connection would buy nothing here.

        Recording: a broadcast is kept only while it runs unless the broadcaster ticks “keep the recording”, in which case the segments are filed to the tape archive, where they can be played back or burned. Nothing is uploaded from a watcher's device; the only thing written down is where you left your own floating camera.
