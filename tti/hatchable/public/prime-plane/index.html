Part V — Speculative Appendix · the Prime Being

The Prime Plane

Suppose the line that keeps climbing counts in fives. What clock do the beings sent back obey? An eight-state return preserves the Fibonacci pairing and falls behind. A four-state return restores the quarter-turn and moves ahead. The arithmetic is simple. The choice between the two is not.

The conjecture, before the calculation

What “base” has to mean

A numeral base cannot create a physical phase shift merely by changing how a number is written. For this speculation to say anything, base must mean the number of equally spaced phase states in one local cycle.

On that definition the prime plane has five states and advances by one fifth-turn at every shared tick. The return plane is allowed two rival clocks: eight states, as in the Fibonacci and octave reading; or four states, as in the quarter-turn and grounding reading. Let the relative phase be measured as prime minus return:

\[ \omega_b=\frac{2\pi}{b},\qquad \Delta\theta_{5\to b}=\omega_5-\omega_b =2\pi\!\left(\frac15-\frac1b\right) \]

The sign now has content. A positive value means the prime plane advances farther on each tick, so the return plane lags. A negative value means the return plane advances farther, so it leads. This is a convention about orientation, not yet a force law. ◆

The fork

Two Return Clocks

Both alternatives are internally clean. They differ in the size of the shift, the time required to rephase, and — most importantly — which plane is ahead.

Model A — base 5 to base 8

The return plane lags

The prime clock moves by \(72^\circ\); the eight-state return clock moves by \(45^\circ\). The prime plane therefore gains twenty-seven degrees per shared tick.

\[ \Delta\theta_{5\to8} =\frac{2\pi}{5}-\frac{2\pi}{8} =\frac{3\pi}{20}=27^\circ \]

\[ N_{5,8}=\operatorname{lcm}(5,8)=40 \]

First full rephasing: forty ticks. The prime plane completes eight turns while the return plane completes five.

Model B — base 5 to base 4

The return plane leads

The prime clock still moves by \(72^\circ\); the four-state return clock moves by \(90^\circ\). The sign reverses: the return plane gains eighteen degrees per shared tick.

\[ \Delta\theta_{5\to4} =\frac{2\pi}{5}-\frac{2\pi}{4} =-\frac{\pi}{10}=-18^\circ \]

\[ N_{5,4}=\operatorname{lcm}(5,4)=20 \]

First full rephasing: twenty ticks. The prime plane completes four turns while the return plane completes five.

The comparison in one ledger

The table compares consequences, not evidence. Nothing observed has yet selected a five-state prime clock, much less decided whether its partner has eight states or four.

Why eight is attractive

Five and Eight Belong to One Fibonacci Sentence

Five and eight are consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Their ratio is already close to the golden limit governing the return depth:

\[ \frac85=1.6\approx\varphi=1.618033\ldots \]

The difference between the two phase counts is three, the preceding Fibonacci number. The relative turn can therefore be written as one small Fibonacci relation:

\[ \Delta\theta_{5\to8} =2\pi\frac{8-5}{5\cdot8} =2\pi\frac{3}{40} \]

That is why the forty-tick closure has a distinctive exchange. At closure the five-state plane has made eight turns, and the eight-state plane has made five. Each plane returns carrying the other's count. Within a framework already built from Fibonacci branching, the swap is not arbitrary decoration; it is the strongest internal argument for the eight-state return. ◆

Its narrative consequence also fits the existing Prime Being chapter. The climbing line advances farther on every common tick. The returners remain behind it by an accumulating angle, so their deposits form a lagging field. If that lag couples to the polarisation, it gives the chapter a geometry for its phrase “pulled from behind.” ◆

The weakness: Fibonacci adjacency does not establish a clock. The framework still owes a reason why a returned being should have eight distinguishable phase states, why the splice should synchronise the two planes, and how an angular lag becomes acceleration.

Why four is a real rival

Five and Four Put the Return in Quadrature

Four has a different claim. It is the first spatial grounding in the framework's integer sequence: four directions, the square, the tetrahedral count of faces, and the quarter-turn that puts two axes into quadrature. A returner may not preserve the Fibonacci clock of the ascent. It may be projected back onto the fourfold plane that makes location possible.

The four-state clock advances by a right angle while the prime plane advances by a pentagonal angle. Their relative step is smaller than in the eight-state model but has the opposite sign:

\[ \left|\Delta\theta_{5\to4}\right|=\frac{\pi}{10}=18^\circ, \qquad \operatorname{sgn}(\Delta\theta_{5\to4})=-1 \]

The shorter twenty-tick beat is not merely half the other period; it exchanges the cycle counts in a different order. At closure the prime plane has made four turns and the return plane five. The returned field is now ahead of the prime line under the same orientation convention.

That changes the story. A phase lead does not naturally picture a depth pulling from behind. It is better suited to anticipatory feedback, resistance, braking, or a reversal of handedness at the seam. None follows automatically, but the base-four alternative forces the framework to state which kind of coupling it needs instead of treating every non-zero phase as equivalent. ◆

The weakness: four is structurally abundant, which makes it easy to invoke after the fact. This model gives up the consecutive-Fibonacci relation and must explain why the returning branch changes clocks from a fivefold ascent to a fourfold ground.

✦

A second meaning that must not be mixed with the first

If “Base” Means a Multiplicative Scale

The cyclic model above treats base five, eight or four as a count of phase states. There is another possible meaning: the prime plane may mark densities at \(5^n\), while the return plane marks them at \(b^m\). On that reading, alignment would require

\[ 5^n=b^m,\qquad b\in\{8,4\} \]

There is no positive-integer solution for either rival. Every positive power of four or eight is a power of two; every positive power of five has only five as a prime factor. The two grids meet at one and never meet exactly again. Equivalently, both \(\log_8 5\) and \(\log_4 5\) are irrational. ◆

A logarithmic phase can still be defined:

\[ \Phi_b(n)=2\pi\left\{n\log_b5\right\}, \qquad b\in\{8,4\} \]

where the braces mean fractional part. This phase never repeats exactly; it winds densely around the circle. That is a very different cosmology from the forty- or twenty-tick beat. One gives periodic rephasing because the clocks have finitely many states. The other gives permanent quasiperiodicity because the density scales are multiplicatively incommensurate.

The framework must choose which meaning of base it intends. It cannot claim the finite closure of the cyclic model and the non-closing drift of the scale model in the same argument.

✦

What would distinguish them

Three Edges the Speculation Volunteers

One — recurrence

A forty-tick reset favours the five-to-eight cyclic model. A twenty-tick reset favours five to four. No exact reset, together with continuing phase drift, favours the multiplicative-scale reading.

Two — handedness

Under one fixed orientation, base eight produces a lag and base four a lead. Any proposed coupling must preserve that sign. A model that predicts the same effect from both has discarded the only sharp difference between them.

Three — the clock

The framework must identify a physical event that deserves to be called the shared tick. Without one, “forty” and “twenty” count marks on a diagram rather than events in a world.

The present verdict

No such recurrence, handed coupling or common clock has been measured. The two models are therefore alternatives inside the vision, not rival physical theories. ◆

Earned, Chosen, Refused

Earned
Given five- and eight-state clocks, the shift is \(+3\pi/20\), the return lags, and the joint period is forty. Given five and four, the shift is \(-\pi/10\), the return leads, and the joint period is twenty. The least-common-multiple calculation, sign reversal, and irrationality of the multiplicative logarithms are ordinary mathematics.
Chosen
That the Prime Being has a five-state clock; that a returner has either eight or four states; that both receive one common tick; that relative phase survives the splice; and that phase can couple to polarisation, motion or memory.
Refused
Calling a radix a mechanism. Treating Fibonacci adjacency or spatial quadrature as evidence by itself. Ignoring the reversed sign of the base-four shift. Mixing finite cyclic closure with non-repeating logarithmic drift. Claiming acceleration before a coupling equation exists.

Closing stance

If the Prime Being must be pulled by what it leaves behind, five against eight is the cleaner internal fit: the returners lag, the Fibonacci language survives, and the beat closes only after forty ticks. If return means projection back into a grounded quarter-turn plane, five against four is the stronger rival: the beat closes at twenty and the returners lead, forcing a different account of feedback.

The value of the alternative is not that it disproves the first picture. It reveals which parts of that picture were doing work.
