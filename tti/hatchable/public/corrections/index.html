Corrections

A framework that grades its own claims has to keep a record of the times it changed them. This page is that record, and the standing invitation that goes with it.

Every chapter on this site closes by separating what is earned from what is chosen from what is refused. That habit is worth nothing if it is only applied at the moment of writing. A claim that is later shown to be wrong has to be marked as wrong in public, by the same hand that made it, or the grading was decoration.

So: three kinds of change are recorded here, and one kind is not.

  • Correction — a claim was wrong, and something changed it: a measurement, an arithmetic error, an argument that does not hold. ◆
  • Revision — the claim stands, but it was stated badly enough to mislead. The restatement is recorded because a reader who was misled is owed the correction of the prose. ◆
  • Refusal — a reading was available, it was declined, and it is recorded here because a refusal is a decision rather than an absence. The refusals are the most useful entries on this page. ◆
  • Not recorded — typography, phrasing, a broken link. If it does not change what a page claims, it does not belong here.

The log

Everything That Has Changed Since r3.5

Newest first. Each entry names the claim, the change, and what brought it about.

  • 8 October 2026 — Revision, and a partial refusal. Added The Pendulum, the first paper written after r3.5. It takes one sentence — the universe is an eternal integration of opposing infinities which together swing like a double pendulum — and in testing it, refuses a reading that had been left available: that the two infinities are the two turning points of the swing. They are not. The bob visits those twice a period, exactly. The two infinities are the pendulum's two rest points — the bottom, approached for ever and never occupied, and the inverted position, whose crossing the separatrix never completes because its period is infinite. ◆
  • 8 October 2026 — Addition, and the first live figure. The Pendulum is the first page on this site to draw its own figure rather than carry a drawn one: the same double pendulum released twice, from initial conditions a thousandth of a radian apart, with the simple pendulum alongside for comparison. It is computed in the reader's browser, and so it is deliberately not one of the plates — the plate room holds vector figures, and a computed figure cannot be copied into it without ceasing to be what it is. The count there stays at twenty-one, and that is the correct number rather than an omission. ◆
  • 8 October 2026 — Correction, in the sentence. The framework's sentence was carried on this site as an out of control pendulum. It now reads like a double pendulum everywhere it appears. The change is not cosmetic. An out-of-control pendulum is one whose behaviour is unpredictable — a claim about knowledge. A double pendulum is one whose motion is exactly determined and whose long-run state is nonetheless beyond computing — a claim about tractability. The framework is making the second claim, and the earlier phrasing made the first. ◆
  • 8 October 2026 — Clarification, in the plate room. Under Foundations, the plate room carries “The Phase Portrait of a Pendulum, and the Orbit That Never Arrives”. That is a single pendulum's portrait, and the orbit that never arrives is its separatrix, the one orbit of infinite period. The paper's figure is a double pendulum and lives on the paper. The two are different objects, and the paper points to the plate from its own text. No correction was needed here; the entry exists so that a reader who takes the plate for the paper's evidence finds the distinction already drawn. ◆
  • 8 October 2026 — Correction, in the count. The site's titles and descriptions said twenty papers in every page's metadata. With the Pendulum added there were twenty-one. All of them now read correctly — which is a small thing that was wrong in ninety places, and therefore worth logging. ◆
  • 8 October 2026 — Addition, not counted. This page, together with The Reading Path, Notation & Terms and The Library, is apparatus rather than argument. It is not counted among the twenty-one papers or the eleven essays, and it will not be: a map is not a territory, and a glossary is not a claim.

Earlier revisions — up to r3.5 — were drafts of the same edition rather than changes to a published one, so the log begins where publication does. If that convention is wrong, it is itself a correction worth filing.

The decisions that look like omissions

Already Refused

Readings that were available, were tempting, and were declined. Recorded so that their absence is not mistaken for an oversight.

  • The turning points as the two infinities. Refused in The Pendulum. Prettier, and wrong.
  • “π is in the period because the universe favours circles.” Refused: π is in the period because a period is a return, and every return is priced in π. The other version is a mood wearing a derivation's clothes.
  • That a Möbius strip proves anything about minds. Refused on Möbius, in as many words: the topology is used as a precise analogy and labelled as one. The mystic may say “I am the universe”; the strip does not say it for them.
  • That the pendulum is evidence. Refused: it illustrates and it connects, and it does not extend. What is testable on this site is on Seams and Stress Tests, and a metaphor may not borrow their authority.
  • That the χ-field has been found. Refused on Stress Tests: the page puts a χ-field halo where other models put a dark-matter halo, and grades it as the framework's own construction. A construction that fits a rotation curve is not a detection, and the page does not claim one.
  • Drama in place of grading. Refused everywhere. No page claims a measurement it does not have, and no page hides an objection it knows.

The invitation

How To File A Refutation

Send it to dragon.jim.rankin@gmail.com. Four things make a refutation usable, and none of them is politeness:

  • Name the claim precisely. Not “the framework is wrong about consciousness” but “the claim that dx can be a completed sum without a smallest interval” — with the page and the sentence.
  • Name the grade you are attacking. A refutation of a ◆ vision item is not a refutation of the framework: the site already said it was vision. The strongest targets are ◆ items that turn out not to be established, and ◆ items whose arithmetic does not close.
  • Give the measurement or the argument. A number, a citation, or a derivation that reaches a different place. “This feels unfalsifiable” is a request for a better statement of the claim, and will be treated as one.
  • Say whether you want your name on it. Corrections that arrive with something checkable attached are posted in the log above, with the refutation summarised as fairly as it can be put.

Two of the entries in the log above came from the framework's own pages refusing their own most attractive readings. Nobody had to write in for those. The ones that need a letter are usually the ones the author cannot see, which is the reason this page exists.

The one thing that must not change

The method, not the conclusions.

Some of what is on this site will be wrong. The arithmetic in the Ledger may not close; the reading of the galaxy counts may be an artefact of how the counts were taken; the χ-field may be a fiction that happens to fit one curve. All of that is survivable, and none of it is a reason not to look.

What is not survivable is a page that claims more than it has. If that ever happens here, it is the most serious entry this log could carry — and it would be written by the same hand, in the same place, for the same reason as the others.

Test everything; keep what survives.
