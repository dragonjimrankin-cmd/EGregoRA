1. Recording

Everything starts with the sound itself: a file you recorded elsewhere, or one you make right here.

Straight into the browser, with ten seconds to settle first.

Stops the take, files it and puts it on the site in one press. Every save is kept in the owner’s vault.

2. Work

All of this happens in this browser, on this machine, before anything is sent. The file is decoded, run through the chain below and rendered offline — usually faster than real time — then encoded to the form chosen below, MP3 or FLAC. Nothing is uploaded until you have listened to it.

Why the chain is shaped this way — pauses, stutters and the room’s tone

A pause is not a fault — silence removed completely sounds wrong, because conversation has rhythm. Long stretches are shortened to the breath you leave here, not deleted. The gate opens just before each word and holds across it, so speech is never clipped; only noise that stands alone between phrases — a cough, a sniff, a chair knock — gets shut out.

Stuttering removal is on by default and deliberately shy. It looks for one pattern only — the same short syllable landing two or more times in a row, then the word itself — and mutes only the repeats before it: the last repeat and the word always stand, so a word a person chose to say twice survives. If the pattern fires too often to be trusted, nothing is touched at all. Every place it touched is listed under the reading, and the original can always be published instead.

The room’s tone is the opposite of the gate’s scissors: where the gate shut and left digital zero, noise measured from the recording’s own floor is laid back down — warmed, slowly breathing, never louder than the room itself — so the background runs unbroken from the first word to the last. Where the room already breathes, nothing is added; a recording with no floor at all is left in its studio silence.

Whichever is chosen, the recording is converted to that form first — before any of the work is done — and the enhanced take is published in the same form.

3. Words

One click makes the transcript, summary, title, tags and links from the recording itself.

Private local Whisper: the separate transcription app runs on this machine and the recording is never sent to a speech-to-text service. Its Whisper Base model downloads once (about 206 MB) and is cached privately by this browser. Only the finished transcript is given to The Two Infinities for the summary and, when you publish, storage with the episode.

For Ed — how to publish a new episode. Open the panel above, choose the MP3, and let the door do the rest: it is transcribed, given its summary and its shape, and put on the shelf — the RSS feed at /feed.xml picks it up in the same request. Under each player the reader can turn the subtitles on or off and choose their language; the words arrive as soon as an episode's transcript is taken.
